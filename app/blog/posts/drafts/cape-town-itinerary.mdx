## Ending Our Southern Africa Adventure in Cape Town

After six weeks of road tripping through South Africa, Eswatini, and Lesotho, we saved the best for last: Cape Town. We spent our final week based in this stunning city, using it as a launching point for day trips to see penguins, whales, cheetahs, and explore the famous wine region. Cape Town delivered everything we'd hoped for: dramatic mountain scenery, incredible food and wine, diverse wildlife experiences, and a vibrant urban energy.

We stayed in an Airbnb in Cape Town for most of the week, with just one night in Hermanus for whale watching. This setup was perfect because Cape Town is ideally located for day trips while offering plenty to explore within the city itself.

## Rental Car

We rented a car for the week through Avis. Having a car gave us the flexibility to explore at our own pace and reach more remote spots like Boulders Beach and the West Coast National Park. Driving in Cape Town is straightforward, we didn't find that other drivers were aggressive, and once you were out of the city it's just like driving on country roads. If you do decide to rent a car just make sure to find accommodation with secure parking as car break-ins are common.

It's not required to have a rental car, I would say most people we met didn't have one, but it definitely made our trip easier. You can easily take Ubers to all the major attractions, and most tours will provide pickup and drop-off from your accommodation at an extra cost.

## Budget

Cape Town can be done on a range of budgets, from backpacker hostels to luxury hotels and fine dining. We aimed for a mid-range budget, staying in Airbnbs, eating at breakfast at home and eating out for lunch and dinner, and doing a variety of activities.

## Where To Stay

We stayed in an absolutely beautiful Airbnb in Cape Town's City Center for most of the week. This area is centrally located with lots of shops and museums within walking distance and close to the Table Mountain cable car. During the day, the area felt very safe and vibrant. At night, we noticed the visible effects of Cape Town's housing crisis and economic inequality, with many people sleeping in the streets. While we never had any issues, we strongly recommend taking Ubers after dark rather than walking, especially as we were unfamiliar with the area.

My recommendation would be to stay in the Sea Point or Green Point neighborhoods.

**Sea Point**: This area is right along the coast with beautiful views of the ocean and Table Mountain. There are lots of great restaurants, cafes, and shops within walking distance.

**Green Point**: This neighborhood is close to the V&A Waterfront and has a lively atmosphere with plenty of dining and entertainment options. It's also within walking distance to the city center, and a quick uber drive away Table Mountain.

**Camps Bay**: If you want to be right on the beach, Camps Bay is a great option. It has stunning views, beautiful beaches, and a variety of restaurants and bars. However, it is a bit further from the city center.

## Day 2

### Penguins, Cape of Good Hope & V&A Waterfront

We packed a lot into our first full day exploring the Cape Peninsula. We started the day early in Bo-Kaap, the famous neighborhood known for its brightly colored houses. We stopped to take photos and tried koeksisters, a traditional South African syrup-soaked donut that's incredibly sweet and delicious.

**Boulders Beach Penguins:** Next we went to Boulders Beach and arrived at around 10:00am to see the famous African penguin colony. These little penguins waddle around the beach and nest in the boulders, completely unbothered by human visitors. It's surreal to be so close to penguins in their natural habitat. The beach has boardwalks that take you right through the colony. Entry costs R190 per person. If Boulders is too crowded, there's another viewing spot at Cape Point, but Boulders is worth it for the up-close experience.

**Cape of Good Hope:** After the penguins, we continued to Cape Point and the Cape of Good Hope, often mistakenly called the southernmost point of Africa (that's actually Cape Agulhas, which we visited earlier in our trip). The scenery here is dramatic, with rugged cliffs dropping into the ocean. You can hike up to the old lighthouse for panoramic views or take the funicular. We saw ostriches, elands and tons of birds in the reserve.

**V&A Waterfront:** We ended the day here, Cape Town's main tourist hub with shops, restaurants, and harbor views. It's touristy but well done, and the setting with Table Mountain as a backdrop is beautiful. We grabbed dinner here while watching the sunset.

## Day 3

### Hermanus: Wine & Whales

We were in Cape Town in late October, the end of Southern Right whale season, so seeing whales was a key bucket list item for us. We drove 1.5 to 2 hours southeast to Hermanus, known as one of the best whale watching destinations in the world.

**Creation Wines:** We stopped for lunch and a wine tasting at Creation Wines around 12:30pm. This winery offers incredible views over the Hemel-en-Aarde Valley and has an excellent food and wine pairing experience. The tasting menus are creative and beautifully presented. This was one of our favorite wine experiences in South Africa.

**Hermanus Airbnb:** We checked into our Airbnb in Hermanus around 3:00pm. The town sits right on Walker Bay and has a cliff path where you can often spot whales from shore during whale season (June to November).

**Whale Watching Tour:** Our boat tour departed at 5:00pm (we arrived at 4:30pm). We saw multiple southern right whales, including mothers with calves. The whales came incredibly close to the boat, breaching and tail-slapping. Watching these massive creatures in their natural habitat was absolutely unforgettable. This was a highlight of our entire trip.

If you have time, you could add another winery between Creation and Hermanus, as there are several excellent options in the area.

## Day 4

### Cheetahs & Franschhoek Wine Tram

We left Hermanus early for another big day.

**Ashia Cheetah Sanctuary:** We departed our Airbnb at 7:30am for the two-hour drive to the Ashia Cheetah Sanctuary, arriving around 9:30am. This ethical sanctuary focuses on cheetah conservation and education. We got to watch the cheetahs during their morning exercise session, where they chase a lure to stay fit and engaged. Being so close to these incredible cats was amazing, and the staff were passionate and knowledgeable about conservation efforts.

**Franschhoek Wine Exploring:** After the sanctuary, we drove about 30 minutes to Franschhoek, one of the most beautiful towns in the Winelands. We explored various wineries in the area, tasting excellent wines with mountain views. The town itself is charming with great restaurants and cafes.

There's an option to take the Franschhoek Wine Tram, which is a hop-on-hop-off tram and tram-bus that connects several wineries. We didn't do it this time, but it looked like a fun way to visit multiple estates without worrying about driving.

**Return to Cape Town:** We drove back to Cape Town in the late afternoon. We had considered taking the cable car up Table Mountain this evening but decided to save it for another day.

## Day 5

### West Coast Safari & Bloubergstrand

We headed north for a completely different landscape experience.

**West Coast Safari:** We drove about an hour to a private game reserve for a morning safari. This reserve has free-roaming animals including giraffes, zebras, various antelope species, and more. While it's not the same scale as Kruger or Etosha, it was a fun wildlife experience close to Cape Town and gave our friends who joined us later in the trip their first safari experience.

**Lunch:** We had lunch around 12:30pm.

**West Coast National Park:** Around 1:00pm, we explored West Coast National Park, a marine and coastal park known for its wetlands, birdlife, and spring wildflowers (if you visit in August/September). We did some easy hiking and enjoyed the coastal scenery. The park is also a good spot to see turtles if you're lucky.

**Bloubergstrand Beach:** On the way back to Cape Town, we stopped at Bloubergstrand Beach around 4:00pm. This beach is famous for having the iconic postcard view of Table Mountain across Table Bay. The beach is popular with kiteboarders, and watching the sunset here with Table Mountain in the background is spectacular. This became one of our favorite spots in the Cape Town area.

## Day 6

### Exploring Cape Town

We left this day mostly unplanned to explore Cape Town itself at a relaxed pace.

**Coffee Shops:** Cape Town has an incredible coffee culture. We spent the morning hopping between cafes, trying different roasters and enjoying the laid-back atmosphere.

**Beaches:** We hit a few of Cape Town's beautiful beaches. Camps Bay is the most famous, with its white sand, turquoise water, and dramatic Twelve Apostles mountain backdrop. Clifton has four small beaches (numbered 1-4) that are sheltered from wind. The water is cold, but the scenery is beautiful.

**District Six Museum:** This museum tells the painful history of District Six, a vibrant multi-racial neighborhood that was destroyed during apartheid when 60,000 residents were forcibly removed. It's a powerful and important museum that provides context for understanding Cape Town and South Africa's history.

**Other Options:** We considered visiting Robben Island, where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned for 18 years, but didn't end up booking it. If you're interested, book well in advance as tours sell out quickly.

We also explored different neighborhoods, tried various restaurants, and simply soaked in the atmosphere of this diverse, creative city.

## Cape Town Tips

**Length of Stay:** Six days felt like a good amount of time to see the main highlights without feeling rushed. You could easily spend two weeks exploring Cape Town and the surrounding region.

**Weather:** Cape Town can be windy, especially in summer. The wind doesn't ruin the experience but can impact activities like Table Mountain cable car (it closes in high winds) and beach days.

**Accommodation:** Staying in an Airbnb gave us more space and a kitchen, which was perfect after weeks of eating out. There are great options in various neighborhoods depending on what vibe you want.

**Day Trips:** Most major attractions are within 1-2 hours of the city, making Cape Town perfect for day trips. Having a car gives you the most flexibility.

**Wine Tours:** If you don't want to drive, there are excellent wine tour companies that will take you through Stellenbosch, Franschhoek, or the other wine regions.

**Safety:** Use the same precautions as elsewhere in South Africa: don't leave valuables visible in your car, be aware of your surroundings, and use Uber at night rather than walking alone.

**Table Mountain:** The cable car is expensive at R450 return, but the views are worth it if weather cooperates. Check conditions before going as it often closes due to wind. You can also hike up if you're fit and adventurous.

**Whale Season:** June to November is whale season, with peak viewing in September and October. If seeing whales is a priority, plan your visit accordingly.

**Penguins:** Go to Boulders Beach earlier in the day for smaller crowds. The penguins are active and adorable no matter what time you visit.

---

Cape Town exceeded all our expectations. The combination of natural beauty, wildlife, wine, food, and culture makes it one of the most well-rounded destinations we've visited. It was the perfect way to end our six-week Southern Africa adventure, and we left already planning our return. If you're considering Cape Town, don't hesitate. Just go. You won't regret it.

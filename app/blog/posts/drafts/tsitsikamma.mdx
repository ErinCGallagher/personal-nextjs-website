Where the Tsitsikamma mountains meet the Indian Ocean, you'll find one of South Africa's most spectacular coastal landscapes. This is Tsitsikamma National Park, a jewel along the famous Garden Route, where ancient forests meet rugged coastline.

During our October visit, we tackled two stunning trails that showcase the best of what this park has to offer. If you're planning a trip to the Garden Route, these hikes should be at the top of your list.

## About Tsitsikamma National Park

Tsitsikamma, which means "place of abundant water" in the Khoi language, stretches for about 80 kilometers along South Africa's southern coast. The park protects one of the last remaining indigenous forests in South Africa, along with a spectacular rocky coastline.

The marine protected area extends 5.5 kilometers out to sea, making this one of the few parks in the world that protects both terrestrial and marine ecosystems. The park is part of the larger Garden Route National Park and forms a crucial link in South Africa's conservation efforts.

## Where We Stayed: Oceanette Accommodation

We rented one of the oceanette apartments which are self-catering roofed accommodations right in the park. These units sit halfway between both trailheads, making them perfectly positioned for exploring. Ours came fully equipped with everything we needed (pots, pans, cooking facilities), so we only had to bring food.

The oceanettes offer stunning ocean views and the convenience of having your accommodation right where the action is. After our second hike, we fired up the braai (South African barbecue) and watched the sunset, although we had to keep a watchful eye on Klemm, a persistent seagull who patiently waited for any opportunity to steal our dinner.

The park offers various accommodation options beyond oceanettes, including camping sites, forest cabins (for those seeking shelter from the sea breeze and a different vibe), and chalets.

## Planning Your Visit

**Best Time to Visit:** Year-round destination, though spring (September-November) offers whale watching opportunities. Summer (December-February) can be hot and humid, while winter (June-August) is cooler but still pleasant for hiking. We visited in October and had warm afternoons and cool evenings.

**Getting There:** Tsitsikamma is located along the N2 highway between Plettenberg Bay and Jeffreys Bay, making it easily accessible as part of a Garden Route road trip.
Park Fees: Daily conservation fees apply (check South African National Parks website for current rates).

**Book Accommodation Early:** The oceanettes and other accommodations fill up quickly, especially during South African school holidays. We were hoping to book a cabin but only oceanettes remained when checking 1 month in advance.

## Suspension Bridges Trail

The sound hits you first, the relentless roar of waves crashing against rocks. The air carries that distinctive tang of salt and sea spray. The suspension Bridges trail is one of Tsitsikamma's most iconic features. The bridges span a deep canyon where the Storms River meets the Indian Ocean. The trail starts at Storms River Rest Camp and follows a well-maintained boardwalk that meanders through dense indigenous forest before emerging along the cliff face.

### Trail Details

|                    |                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**           | Storms River Mouth Suspension Bridges Trail                      |
| **Distance**       | 4km return (we walked 2.2km)                                     |
| **Duration**       | Duration: 1-2 hours                                              |
| **Elevation Gain** | 50m some steps to climb, but mostly on well maintained boardwalk |
| **Level**          | Easy                                                             |

### What to Expect

The trail begins at the newly built kayak tour center, taking you over a small sandy beach before transitioning to the boardwalk. While the path is well-marked and easy to follow, it's not completely accessible as there are still plenty of stairs to navigate.

As you walk through the forest section, the thick canopy provides welcome shade and acts as a natural windbreak. The cheerful chorus of birdsong accompanies you here, though the ocean's roar is never far away. Every so often, the trees part to reveal postcard-perfect views of the Indian Ocean stretching to the horizon.

We visited in October, which happens to be peak whale season. Despite keeping a watchful eye on the waves, we only spotted a couple of whale blows: telltale spouts that signal something magnificent swimming beneath the surface. No breaching or tail slaps this time, but the possibility of spotting whales adds an extra element of excitement to the hike.

After about an hour of easy walking, you'll reach the highlight: three suspension bridges spanning the river canyon. These swaying bridges offer a unique perspective of the Storms River gorge that you simply can't get any other way (unless you opt for the kayak tour through the canyon). We spent considerable time here, soaking in the views, snapping photos, and scanning the waters for marine life. The trail does continue on, going up a steep incline to the next viewpoint, but we decided to turn around at the suspension bridges along with most other hikers.

### Practical Tips

**Parking Alert:** The rest camp is currently undergoing construction of a large new restaurant and general store, which has significantly reduced available parking spaces. We walked the 1km from our oceanette accommodation to the trailhead, which turned out to be a pleasant warm-up.

**Food & Drink:** The camp restaurant makes an excellent lunch spot. We stopped there after our hike and were rewarded with dolphin sightings as we ate.

**Best For:** Families, photographers, and anyone wanting spectacular coastal views without a strenuous hike.

## Waterfall Trail

While most visitors stick to the suspension bridges, this trail turned out to be the highlight of our day. It follows the first section of the famous Otter Trail: a legendary 45km, five-day hike that runs along the Garden Route coastline from Storms River Mouth to Nature's Valley. The hike just out to the waterfall is totally worth completing, and don't feel like unless you do the full Otter Trail you shouldn't go.

### Trail Details

|                    |                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**           | Waterfall Trail (First Leg of the Otter Trail)                                        |
| **Distance**       | 4km return (we walked 2.2km)                                                          |
| **Duration**       | 3-4 hours                                                                             |
| **Elevation Gain** | 200m+ you're not climbing the cliffs, but there's a lot of up and down over the rocks |
| **Level**          | Moderate to Challenging                                                               |
| **Important**      | Start no later than 2:30 PM to return before dark                                     |

### What to Expect

The landscape is rugged from the very start, and it doesn't let up. This craggy coastline is so stunning that its protected status as a national park feels entirely justified. You begin with ocean views where white-breasted cormorants perch and preen on offshore rocks, before the trail temporarily ducks into dense forest.

Walking through the forested section feels like traversing a tunnel. The canopy of tangled branches is so thick it almost blocks out the sky. But even here, that reassuring roar of waves reminds you the ocean is never far away.

Then comes the transformation. As you exit the forest, the rocks begin to change from dark grey to brilliant orange. This striking color comes from lichen that thrives in the perpetually damp, sea-spray environment. The rocks seem to glow, especially when wet, creating an otherworldly landscape that's unlike anything else on the Garden Route.

### The Challenge

Here's where things get interesting and significantly more challenging. The reported elevation gain is misleading because it doesn't account for the constant ups and downs you'll navigate over these orange rocks. There are only tiny fragments of level ground, so you're constantly choosing your route, scrambling over boulders and finding footholds.

Occasionally you'll spot painted yellow footprints reassuring you that you're on the right track, but honestly, you can't go too far wrong since you're bounded by crashing waves on one side and towering cliffs on the other.

This rock-hopping is tiring work. What looks like a moderate 5km hike on paper becomes much more demanding when you're constantly climbing, balancing, and picking your way across uneven terrain. With just under a kilometer to go, the path mercifully climbs off the rocks and skirts the cliffs instead.

### The Destination

After all that effort, we reached the waterfall, or rather, where the waterfall should have been. October is spring in South Africa, and despite the balmy 20-degree weather and humid conditions that had us sweating profusely, there wasn't much water flowing over the precipice that day. It was a bit disappointing after the challenging hike, but the journey itself more than made up for it.

The return leg is no easier than the outbound route as you'll be doing all that rock scrambling again, this time with tired legs.

### Wildlife Encounters

As the sun sank lower, we noticed the trail coming alive with wildlife. Huge grasshoppers were everywhere (oddly, many were missing a jumping leg, we're still puzzled by this). A Swainson's grouse used the trail to commute but wasn't quite brave enough to pass us, so we stepped aside to let it through.

### Essential Tips

**Hydration is Critical:** Bring more water than you think you'll need. The humidity and constant exertion mean you'll be sweating heavily, and there's no water available along the route.

**Snacks:** Pack plenty of fuel to keep your energy up through all that rock scrambling.

**Timing:** The 2:30 PM cutoff is serious. The trail is treacherous in the dark, and you don't want to be navigating those rocks without daylight.

**Footwear:** Sturdy hiking boots with good ankle support and grip are essential. This is not a trail for trail runners or casual shoes.

**Best For:** Adventurous hikers who enjoy route-finding, don't mind a physical challenge, and want to experience Tsitsikamma's raw coastal beauty away from the crowds.

## Final Thoughts

While the suspension bridges trail is the park's claim to fame, the waterfall trail is where Tsitsikamma truly reveals its wild heart. Yes, our legs complained. Yes, as adults we probably should have stretched before bed (we regretted not doing so the next morning). But clambering over those glowing orange rocks, with waves crashing beside us and the forest-clad cliffs towering above, created memories that far outlasted any muscle soreness.

If you only have time for one trail, the suspension bridges won't disappoint. But if you can manage both, you'll experience the full gamut of what makes Tsitsikamma one of South Africa's most remarkable national parks.

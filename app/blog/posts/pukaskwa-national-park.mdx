## Wawa, On The Way to Pukaskwa!

With our first night of camping on the books at Lake Superior Provincial Park ([previous blog post](lake-superior-provincial-park)), we were on our way to our second park, Pukaskwa National Park, pronounced PUCK-a-saw. My favourite Ontario park I've visited to date! Keep reading to learn why.

The drive from our backcountry site to Pukaskwa was about 2 and a half hours and on the way you drive through the Municipality of Wawa. Although many people may just drive past without a second thought, there are 2 reasons why you should make a quick stop here. The first reason is to get a photo with the [Wawa Goose](https://wawa.cc/about-wawa/wawa-ontario/our-famous-canada-goose/). This 8 and a half meter tall steel Canadian goose is the largest monument of its kind in the world and was built to mark the completion of the last link in the Trans-Canada highway. The second more practical reason we stopped was to pick-up groceries for our next few days of frontcountry camping. Wawa was the closest place with a major grocery store on our route. Unfortunately, due to COVID social distancing rules, Saturday was not a great time to stop by because there was a line outside the store and in the end it took us an hour to get groceries.

**⭐ Tip:** To pass the time if you find yourself stuck in a COVID grocery store line in Wawa, play a game of "Spot the Goose". They use it to decorate all aspects of the town.

## Frontcountry Camping, oTENTik Edition

We spent 2 nights in Pukaskwa National Park and managed to get ourselves one of the 5 [oTENTik](https://www.pc.gc.ca/en/voyage-travel/hebergement-accommodation/otentik) campsites. Usually there is a 2 night minimum for the oTENTiks but we called the park and they made an exception for us. Now, if you have never heard of an oTENTik and are trying to figure out the strange capitalization, you are not alone. I too had never heard of one but now that I've tried one I'd really recommend it if you're looking for a more comfortable camping experience.

An oTENTik is a cross between a tent, and a rustic cabin. The frame and floor are made of wood but the roof is made of a thick canvas. At Pukaskwa inside each oTENTik there are 3 beds, sleeping up to 6 people, a table and chairs, wood stove as well as electricity. Our favourite part was the BBQ, and included a propane tank, just outfront allowing us to make amazing burgers for dinner. These campsites are walk in only, but they provide each campsite with a wagon making the short walk from the parking lot with all your gear more manageable. They also provide food lockers just at the edge of the campsite as they are very strict about no food inside the oTENTiks at any time so as not to have a mouse problem.

**⭐ Tip:** oTENTiks are very popular so call/book early if you want to stay in one. We called 3 hours after they were made available and basically all of them were already booked.

## Manito Miikana Trail

After settling into the oTENTik, we wanted to go for a quick evening hike before sunset and were happy to see Pukaskwa had so many trails leaving right from the visitor's centre. We decided on the Manito Miikana Trail as it was a shorter loop with multiple lookout points. To get to the trailhead from the visitor's centre, we started on the Boardwalk Beach Trail which took us through a small beautiful beach on Horseshoe Bay. The Manito Miikana Trail starts at the one end of this beach and requires you walk through the sand and over some small driftwood piles. The trail itself is a short loop and we completed it in about 50 minutes. This included ample time to take in the views and included our walk on the Boardwalk Beach trail. The terrain itself is a lot of up hill with lots of exposed roots, something we learned to expect from most Northern Ontario trails. Although the park isn't well known for this hike specifically the lookout points over the water are amazing and shouldn't be missed. Finishing our hike on Horseshoe Bay was also a perfect way to end the evening. We all needed a quick dip to wash off the sweat from the hike.

## Hattie Cove Frontcountry Camping

In addition to the oTENTiks, Pukaskwa has traditional frontcountry campsites at Hattie Cove. These are your classic car camping sites with picnic tables, fire pits, and access to washroom facilities with running water and flush toilets (a luxury after backcountry camping!). The campground is nestled in the forest with many sites offering privacy from your neighbours.

What makes Hattie Cove special is its location. You're just steps away from multiple trailheads, including the famous Coastal Trail and the Beach Trail. This means you can start your hiking adventures right from your campsite without having to drive anywhere. After a long day on the trails, we loved being able to walk back to camp, cook dinner over the fire, and relax without worrying about getting back in the car. Unfortunetaly it poored rain overnight so we didn't have the most restful sleep. I ended up moving to the car in the middle of the night because we found out our tent was no longer waterproof.

## White River Suspension Bridge via the Coastal Hiking Trail

This was hands down the highlight of our time at Pukaskwa and Northern Ontario road trip, but I won't lie, it was also the most challenging hike of our entire Northern Ontario road trip. At 18 kilometres return, this day hike along the Coastal Trail to the White River Suspension Bridge pushed us to our limits, but the payoff was absolutely worth every tired step.

The trail starts off easy enough with long stretches of boardwalk winding through wetlands and boreal forest. We made good time through these sections, enjoying the changing scenery. But don't let the "mostly flat" description fool you. While there aren't massive elevation gains, the terrain is incredibly rugged. Expect exposed roots everywhere, rocky sections that require careful foot placement, and enough ups and downs to keep your legs working the entire time.

After about 9 kilometres of steady hiking, we finally reached the White River Suspension Bridge. The bridge itself is stunning, swaying gently as you cross high above the rushing White River below. We spent a good amount of time here taking photos, eating snacks, and soaking in the views before tackling the return journey.

By the time we made it back to the trailhead over 5 hours later, we were completely exhausted. Our feet were sore, our legs were tired, and we were ready to collapse. But standing on that suspension bridge surrounded by wilderness, knowing we'd just hiked deep into one of Canada's most remote national parks, made every bit of effort worthwhile. This hike isn't for everyone, but if you're up for a challenge and want to experience the raw beauty of Pukaskwa, absolutely do it.

**⭐ Tip:** Bring way more snacks and water than you think you'll need. We went through our supplies faster than expected and were pretty hungry by the end. Also, start early in the day so you have plenty of daylight for the return hike.

## The Beach Trail

After the grueling Coastal Trail hike, we needed something a bit easier for our final morning at Pukaskwa. The Beach Trail was the perfect choice. At just 2 kilometres return, this short hike delivers big on scenery without demanding too much from tired legs.

The trail follows the shoreline and takes you across a stunning stretch of Lake Superior beach absolutely covered in driftwood. And I mean covered. Massive bleached logs are scattered everywhere, creating this incredible natural sculpture garden. We spent almost as much time scrambling over and around the driftwood as we did actually walking the trail.

The combination of the crystal clear water, smooth stones, and all that weathered driftwood makes this one of the most photogenic spots in the park. We took so many photos here. Even though it's a short trail, give yourself time to explore and enjoy the beach. We ended up spending about an hour out there, and it was the perfect way to wrap up our time at Pukaskwa before hitting the road.

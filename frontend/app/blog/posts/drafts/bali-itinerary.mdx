I love Bali! I’ve been twice, and after exploring various parts of the island, I’ve put together all my top recommendations to help you plan your trip.

**Google Maps List:** [Bali Recommendations](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yKBvrQGreBXo3uXT6?g_st=ia)

## Recommended Itinerary

To see the best of Bali without rushing too much, I recommend the following minimum stays:

- **Ubud (3-4 nights):** No beaches, but incredible food and jungle vibes.
- **Nusa Penida (2 nights):** For breathtaking hiking and coastal views.
- **Seminyak/Canggu (3-4 nights):** Beaches, amazing restaurants, and the digital nomad hub.
- **Uluwatu (2 nights):** For the most rugged and beautiful beaches.

**Note:** Try to avoid visiting during **Nyepi (Bali's "Silent Day")**, which usually falls in early March. The entire island shuts down—including the airport—and you are required to stay inside your accommodation.

## Budget: How Much Does Bali Cost?

Bali is famous for offering incredible value for money. You can live very cheaply or find world-class luxury at a fraction of the price you'd pay in the West. We typically aim for a mid-range budget, staying in nice hotels or villas and eating at a mix of local spots and high-end restaurants.

Here is a breakdown of what a typical 12-day trip for two people might cost (mid-range):

## Ubud

Ubud is my favorite place in Bali for food and relaxation. While you aren't near the beach, the town itself is incredibly charming. It consists of about five main streets filled with cute shops and world-class restaurants. It's also the best place for affordable, high-quality spa treatments.

Being in the heart of the jungle, Ubud offers plenty of adventure: ATVs, waterfalls, and iconic rice paddies. There are also great day clubs just outside of town. I highly recommend staying within the town itself so you can easily walk and explore.

**Recommended Driver:** Agung (+62 812-3967-827). He can pick you up from the airport and is fantastic!

### The Monkey Forest

The Monkey Forest is a popular attraction, but it can be dangerous. The monkeys are aggressive and seem to become more so every year. Unless you've had your rabies shots, I wouldn't necessarily recommend it. If you do go, bring absolutely nothing that can be easily grabbed or stolen. We wore our phones on lanyards around our necks to keep them secure.

### Temple Tours & Waterfalls

A popular activity is visiting the local temples. Your driver will likely offer a "temple tour." It's a great way to learn about Balinese culture. You can choose a driver who will accompany you inside to explain the history, or one who simply drops you off to explore on your own. Most temples provide sarongs to cover your legs and shoulders for free.

These tours can easily be combined with waterfall visits. Most waterfalls have a small entry fee, and occasionally you may be asked to pay for a local guide. Don't feel like you're being ripped off—it's a small way to support the local community.

## Seminyak

If you're choosing between the main beach towns, I think **Seminyak** is the best place to stay. It sits between Kuta and Canggu and serves as a great central hub.

- **Where to Stay:** The Four Points in Seminyak is a great choice.
- **Dining:** You are surrounded by an incredible density of high-quality restaurants.
- **Beach Clubs:** We loved **Finns Beach Club**. Although we aren't typically "beach club people," the VIP experience was excellent value. We had a giant daybed, great food and drinks, and spent the whole day relaxing.

## Canggu

Canggu is right next to Seminyak but has a very different vibe. It's the primary digital nomad hub, which means it has great cafes but also **intense traffic**. The beaches here are slightly better than Seminyak, so if surfing and beach time are your priorities, this might be the spot for you. Otherwise, it's easy enough to visit for a day from Seminyak.

## Kuta

Kuta has a bit of a reputation for being rougher and potentially more dangerous than its neighbors. While it is cheaper because it's less popular, we chose to stay away. However, I have heard positive reviews from some travelers on a budget. If you do choose to stay in Kuta, be cautious, especially at night.

## Uluwatu

Uluwatu is highly hyped, but it's important to understand that it's a large, spread-out area rather than a single walkable town. You will definitely need a scooter or taxis to get around.

That said, Uluwatu has the **most beautiful beaches** in Bali. It doesn't have the same "town vibe" as Ubud or Seminyak with rows of shops and restaurants; instead, you'll find small clusters of amenities near the various beaches. If you go in expecting a spread-out coastal area, you'll love it.

## Nusa Penida

Nusa Penida is an island you can visit as a day trip, but it's much better to stay for 1-2 nights. It features the breathtaking viewpoints and beaches you've likely seen all over Instagram. Like Uluwatu, it is not walkable—you'll need a scooter or a driver.

### Top Beaches & Viewpoints

- **Kelingking Beach:** The most famous spot, known for its T-Rex-shaped cliff. The hike down to the beach is quite difficult, so many people just visit for the viewpoint.
- **Broken Beach:** A stunning natural archway with crystal-clear water.
- **Angel's Billabong:** A natural infinity pool with incredible views.

## Munduk

Located up in the mountains, Munduk is known for incredible views, hiking, and waterfalls. It is stunningly beautiful but much more rugged. If you prefer luxury amenities and polished restaurants, this might not be for you.

- **Note:** I unfortunately got food poisoning here (likely from a tiny local restaurant), so be cautious.
- **Driver:** +62 813-3842-4043

## The Gili Islands

In my opinion, the Gili Islands are worth skipping. They felt overhyped and overrated. The journey there is exhausting, and the fast boats can be dangerous (there have been several accidents involving tourists where boats have capsized and tours have died). The restaurants were minimal, although tasty, and the beaches were only average. The power went out frequently so AC often didn't work. If you go bring cash because the power outages means the ATMs might not work. I think Nusa Penida is a far better island experience.

## Amed

Amed is on the far east coast of Bali. It is significantly quieter and less developed than the rest of the island, but I didn't feel it was worth the long journey to get there. The food options are limited, and the beaches are often crowded with fishing boats, making them less ideal for relaxing.

### Mount Batur

Climbing Mount Batur for sunrise is a very popular activity, but we wouldn't recommend it. The hike is quite difficult and requires a 2:00 AM wake-up call from Ubud.

There is a "Jeep tour" alternative, but it's essentially a scam. They don't take you to the top—only to a viewpoint partway up—where you'll be crammed in with hundreds of other Jeeps. In our opinion, it's better to skip this entirely.

## Practical Tips

### Scooters & Transport

Renting a scooter is common, though you need a proper international license. If you wear a helmet and drive safely, you generally won't have trouble.

If you aren't comfortable driving, **Grab (scooter taxis)** are incredibly cheap and easy. Just be aware that drivers often try to negotiate for extra cash outside of the app price, so the displayed price isn't always final.

### Traffic

Traffic in Bali is notorious. Expect long delays, especially when traveling to and from the airport. Pay the extra for a nice air-conditioned car, bring snacks, and be patient. We recommend not planning any major activities on your travel days between towns (especially between Ubud and Seminyak).

### Communication

Everything in Bali is organized via **WhatsApp**. Whether you're booking a tour, a scooter, or a driver, you'll need it.

### Cash

Most tours and restaurants accept credit card, but many smaller places are cash-only. You will also need to pay drivers in cash so it's a good idea to have some on hand. ATMs are widely available, but be cautious about card scrappers. We had our cards cloned in Bali so make sure to bring more than one credit card in case one gets blocked.

China requires a bit more preparation than most destinations. The apps and payment systems are different, western internet is blocked, and cash is rarely used. Sort these things before you land and the rest is easy.

## Apps You Need

WeChat and Alipay are non-negotiable. Without these two apps, daily life in China becomes significantly harder. Download and set them up before you land. The others we recommend will make your life a lot easier too.

### WeChat

[iOS Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wechat/id414478124) | [Android Download](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tencent.mm&pcampaignid=web_share)

WeChat is China's all-purpose super-app: messaging, payments, maps, restaurant menus, and more. You will need it to communicate with locals and tour operators, scan QR codes at restaurants to view menus, and access websites that don't exist on Western internet. Set it up with your phone number before you arrive. You can also add a payment method to use Weixin Pay, the payment system built into WeChat.

### Alipay
[iOS Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/alipay-SIMplify-your-life/id333206289) | [Android Download](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=alipay&c=apps&hl=en)

Alipay is the dominant payment app in China. Cash is rarely accepted, and many vendors, transit systems, and tourist attractions prefer or exclusively use mobile payment. Alipay now has a Tourist Edition specifically for foreign visitors that lets you add an international credit or debit card without needing a Chinese bank account. To pay, you scan the QR code at the register and confirm the amount on your phone. The payment is instant and the shopkeeper gets a notification on their end.

**Tip:** Both apps require a phone number for verification. Make sure yours is set up and functional before you land. Hotel wifi is fine for setup, but having data on arrival helps.

### Amap
[iOS Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/amap-global/id461703208) | [Android Download](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=amap&c=apps&hl=en)

Google Maps does not work in China. Amap is the best alternative for navigation, especially for public transit. It has an English mode and is fairly intuitive once you get used to it. You can also use it to find nearby restaurants, shops, and attractions. Apple Maps works reasonably well for basic navigation, but Amap is better for transit directions and local points of interest.

### Didi
[iOS Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/didi-china-ride-hailing/id554499054) | [Android Download](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=didi%20china&c=apps&hl=en)

Didi is the Chinese equivalent of Uber and is widely used for taxis and private car rides. It's a convenient option for getting around if you don't want to navigate the metro or if you're travelling late at night when public transit is less frequent. The app has an English interface and accepts international payment methods. Didi has a separate app for rides in China vs the rest of the world.

### Trip.com
[iOS Download](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/trip-com-book-flights-hotels/id681752345) | [Android Download](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=trip.com&c=apps&hl=en)

Trip.com is an essential app for booking tours, hotels, transportation, and attractions. Trip is a Chinese company, so it has the best availability of activities but also great English support given it is used internationally. You can also use it to book your airport transfer or local transport if you prefer not to navigate Chinese apps. It's the best way to buy tickets for long-distance bullet or sleeper trains.

## eSIMs and Internet Access

Western internet is censored in China: Google, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Gmail are all blocked. Purchase an eSIM before you arrive to keep access to these services. We recommend Eskimo or Roamless for the best price per GB; Ubigi or Trip.com offer unlimited packages.

On wifi you'll lose access again unless you have a VPN. Popular options like ExpressVPN and NordVPN are blocked; search Reddit for what's currently working. Note that most wifi networks outside of hotels require SMS verification to connect, so make sure your number can receive texts abroad.

## Money and Payment

Cash is accepted almost everywhere but rarely expected. Most places may struggle to give change, as physical currency is simply not in common use anymore. Over a three-week trip, we used cash on two occasions. On our second visit, we didn't bother withdrawing any.

Visa, Mastercard and Amex are not widely accepted, as China uses its own network, Unionpay. Mobile payment via Alipay or WeChat Pay is the standard. If you do want some cash, ICBC is your best bet for an ATM that accepts foreign cards.

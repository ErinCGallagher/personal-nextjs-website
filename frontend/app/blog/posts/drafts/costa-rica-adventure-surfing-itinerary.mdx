This itinerary is built around three of Costa Rica's most distinct environments, experienced at a pace that lets each one breathe. It opens at Arenal — the country's most iconic active volcano — with two nights split between a sloth sanctuary walk, a 70-metre waterfall hike, and an evening soaking in serene natural hot springs. From there, a scenic drive along Lake Arenal leads to Monteverde for three nights of cloud forest hiking, mountain horseback riding, guided dawn birdwatching, and a rainforest night walk. The trip finishes with five nights in Nosara on the Nicoya Peninsula, where the agenda is surf lessons at one of Costa Rica's most consistent beach breaks, kayaking along the coast, and — if the timing aligns — watching Olive Ridley sea turtles nest at the Ostional Wildlife Refuge.

## Getting Around

A rental car gives you the most flexibility on this route, particularly for the Lake Arenal scenic drive and for getting around Nosara once you arrive. Most of the roads between San José, La Fortuna, and Monteverde are paved and in reasonable condition. The stretch from Monteverde to Nosara is mostly paved through Guanacaste, but the final 10–15km into Nosara town and Playa Guiones involves unpaved roads that can be rough and rutted. A 4WD or high-clearance vehicle is strongly recommended if you're travelling in the rainy season (May–November); in the dry season a standard SUV is usually manageable, though it still pays to have decent ground clearance.

**⭐ Tip:** Confirm your rental car agreement explicitly covers the Nicoya Peninsula. Some rental companies add restrictions or surcharges for the area — ask before you book.

## Day 1: San José — Arrival

Juan Santamaría International Airport (SJO) is well connected to North America and Europe, and is 20km northwest of the city centre. Most travellers spend one night in San José before heading out — it is a practical base rather than a destination in its own right, though the Barrio Escalante neighbourhood has good restaurants, craft coffee, and a pleasant walking pace for an evening.

Pick up your rental car on arrival or the following morning. The drive northeast to La Fortuna takes around 2.5–3 hours via Highway 1 and then east on Route 702 — straightforward highway and mountain road driving.

### Hotels in San José

- **Selina San José** — Budget hostel with private rooms and social spaces; ~$30–45/person/night
- **Aparthotel Villas del Río** — Mid-range; comfortable apartments in the eastern suburbs near the highway out of the city; ~$65–80/person/night
- **Wyndham San José Herradura** — Full-service hotel, business facilities, pool, reliable breakfast; ~$100–130/person/night

## Days 2–3: La Fortuna & Arenal Volcano

La Fortuna is the adventure hub of the northern lowlands. It sits at the base of Arenal Volcano, an almost perfectly conical stratovolcano that last erupted actively in 2010. The town itself is a lively grid of tour operators, restaurants, and open-air bars. Two nights is the right amount of time to hit the essential experiences without rushing.

### Fortuna Waterfall (La Catarata de La Fortuna)

The most visually dramatic experience in the La Fortuna area. The waterfall drops 70 metres into a blue-green swimming hole surrounded by dense jungle — the volume of water is astonishing. Getting there involves a descent of roughly 500 concrete and natural rock steps carved into the hillside, followed by the same climb back up. It takes around 20 minutes each way and is moderately demanding in the heat, so start early.

Swimming in the pool at the base is the highlight: the mist, the roar, and the cool water make for a genuinely exceptional experience.

**Entry:** ~$20/person, payable at the park entrance. Operating hours are typically 7:30am–4:30pm. The site is easy to reach by car (paid parking on-site) or by a short taxi or bike ride from town.

### Bogarin Trail Sloth Tour

The Bogarin Trail is a small private wildlife refuge on the edge of La Fortuna town, specifically designed for guided sloth spotting. Guides use telescopes to locate both two-toed and three-toed sloths in the canopy — animals that are almost impossible to find without trained eyes. The walk also regularly turns up coatis, toucans, poison dart frogs, and occasionally howler or white-faced capuchin monkeys.

Tours run morning and afternoon and last approximately 2 hours. The pace is slow and the distances are short — this is not a hike, it's a wildlife walk.

**Cost:** ~$25–30/person for a guided tour. Book directly with the reserve or through your hotel the day before.

### Ecotermales Hot Springs

Arenal has several hot spring complexes ranging from enormous commercial resorts with swim-up bars to smaller, quieter alternatives. Ecotermales is firmly in the latter category: a private, family-run reserve with a limited daily capacity (bookings required), natural pool surrounds, and a noticeably calmer atmosphere than the big players. The waters are thermally heated to between 37°C and 41°C and the setting — jungle trees overhead, the sound of birds and water — is more restorative than anything a larger resort can offer.

**Cost:** ~$45–55/person, including a buffet dinner. Advance booking is essential as they cap daily numbers deliberately. Check their website for current times and availability.

**⭐ Tip:** Book Ecotermales as soon as your travel dates are confirmed — it frequently sells out, especially at weekends and during peak season.

### Arenal Volcano National Park

Hiking into Arenal Volcano National Park gives you the closest views of the volcano itself and takes you through the lava fields left by the 1992 and 1998 eruptions. The two main trails are **Sendero Los Tucanes** (a 4km loop through secondary forest regenerating over old lava flows) and **Sendero Las Coladas** (which passes through the solidified lava fields with open views of the cone).

The park has a ranger station on the south side of the volcano accessed via Route 142. Trails are well-marked and can be walked independently, though a naturalist guide adds considerable value for identifying the regenerating flora and the birdlife in the forest edge habitat.

**Entry:** ~$18/person. Guides can be arranged through La Fortuna tour operators for around $35–50/person including park entry.

### White-Water Rafting on the Río Toro

If you have energy to spare, the Río Toro offers some of the most demanding white-water rafting in Costa Rica — Class IV rapids through narrow volcanic gorges. It is a full morning experience, typically departing from La Fortuna at 7am and returning by midday. Several operators run it, including Desafío Adventure and Jacamar Naturalist Tours. The Río Balsa is a calmer Class II–III alternative, better suited to beginners or anyone who wants to keep it lower-key.

**Cost:** Río Toro is typically ~$80–95/person; Río Balsa runs ~$65–75/person, both including transport, equipment, and a guide.

### Jeep-Boat-Jeep Transfer to Monteverde

Rather than driving the long way around, the traditional transfer between La Fortuna and Monteverde runs as a jeep-boat-jeep combination: a vehicle to the shores of Lake Arenal, a boat across the lake (~45 minutes), and a jeep from the far shore up into the Monteverde highlands. The journey takes around 3 hours total and the lake crossing is scenic and enjoyable.

If you have your own rental car, you will need to drive the road route around the lake instead (see the Monteverde section below). The boat transfer is primarily for travellers without a car or those who are dropping their vehicle and picking up a new one.

### Hotels in La Fortuna

- **Arenal Backpackers Resort** — Budget; pool, volcano views, social hostel atmosphere; private rooms available; ~$25–40/person/night
- **Hotel Las Coladas Arenal** — Mid-range; comfortable bungalow-style rooms, pool, good breakfast, excellent volcano views from the grounds; ~$70–95/person/night
- **Nayara Resort, Spa & Gardens** — High-end; one of the most-lauded luxury properties in Costa Rica, private plunge pools, exceptional service, directly facing the volcano; ~$350–550/person/night

## Days 4–6: Monteverde

The drive from La Fortuna to Monteverde follows Route 142 along the southern shore of Lake Arenal — a genuinely beautiful road with open views across the lake and the volcano behind you as you head west. Pull over at the mirador (viewpoint) near Nuevo Arenal for one of the best vistas on the route. The full drive from La Fortuna to Monteverde takes around 2.5–3 hours.

As you climb into the highlands beyond the lake, the landscape transforms dramatically — from warm lowland rainforest to cool, mist-draped cloud forest. The temperature drops noticeably and the air becomes noticeably humid. Monteverde itself is spread across a long ridgeline above Santa Elena town. The final stretch of road up from the highway is paved and winding; not as rough as its reputation, but take it steady.

Three nights in the Monteverde area gives you time to do each major experience properly without doubling up or skipping anything.

### Santa Elena Cloud Forest Reserve

Santa Elena Cloud Forest Reserve is the quieter alternative to the adjacent (and much more visited) Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve. It is managed by the local high school community and receives a fraction of the foot traffic — which translates to a more genuine cloud forest experience, with better wildlife encounter chances and no feeling of moving through a managed tourist flow.

The reserve has around 12km of well-maintained trails at elevations between 1,500m and 1,700m. The forest here is classic cloud forest: epiphyte-draped trees, bromeliads, orchids, and a permanent light mist that rolls in from the Caribbean side. Resplendent quetzals are sometimes spotted in the reserve during the early morning — the dry season months of January to April are the most reliable time.

**Entry:** ~$18/person. Open 7am–4pm daily. Guides can be hired at the entrance for ~$20–25/person extra, and are worth it for the birdwatching alone.

### Guided Birdwatching

Monteverde is one of the finest birdwatching destinations in the Americas. Over 400 species have been recorded in the region, including the resplendent quetzal, three-wattled bellbird, bare-necked umbrellabird, and an extraordinary variety of tanagers, hummingbirds, and raptors. Early morning is critical — first light through to around 8am is when activity peaks.

Several local guides operate specifically in the Monteverde area. Look for guides listed with the Monteverde Cloud Forest Biological Reserve or the Monteverde Conservation League. Birding tours typically run 3–4 hours starting at dawn.

**Cost:** ~$35–55/person for a guided morning birdwatching walk, depending on group size and operator.

**⭐ Tip:** Even if you're not a dedicated birder, a guided dawn walk in the cloud forest is an extraordinary experience. The sounds alone — the three-wattled bellbird's mechanical call carries for kilometres — are worth the early start.

### Horseback Riding with Mountain Views

Several farms and outfitters around the Monteverde area offer guided horseback rides through the highland landscape — across pastures and into forested areas, with views over the Gulf of Nicoya and the Nicoya Peninsula in the distance on a clear day. Rides typically last 2–3 hours and are accessible for all experience levels. Serendipity Adventures and El Rodeo are two well-regarded operators in the area.

**Cost:** ~$40–65/person for a 2–3 hour guided ride.

### Night Walk in the Rainforest

A guided night walk in the forest around Monteverde opens up a completely different world. Many of the reserve's most interesting residents — red-eyed tree frogs, kinkajous, sleeping birds, vine snakes, tarantulas, and various insects — are only active after dark. Guides carry powerful spotlights and know exactly where to look.

Several operators run night walks in the Santa Elena and Monteverde areas. Maquenque Night Walk and Original Canopy Tour's night walk are two options with good local reputations. Walks typically last 2 hours and depart around 5:30–6:30pm.

**Cost:** ~$25–40/person. Book in advance as group sizes are limited.

## Days 7–11: Nosara

The drive from Monteverde to Nosara takes approximately 3.5–4 hours depending on road conditions. The route descends from the Monteverde highlands, joins the Inter-American Highway briefly, and then heads south through Guanacaste's dry forest — a landscape that feels completely different from the cloud forest you've just left. The vegetation is lower and sparser, the air is warmer and drier, and the light changes to the golden quality of the Pacific lowlands.

En route, the town of Nicoya is worth a brief stop if you're passing through at a reasonable hour. The colonial-era Iglesia de San Blas on the central square is one of the oldest churches in Costa Rica and takes only 20 minutes to look around.

The final 10–15km stretch into Nosara and Playa Guiones is unpaved. The condition varies significantly by season — in the dry season it is rough but passable; in the wet season it can require genuine 4WD capability. Take it slowly regardless.

### Surf Lessons at Playa Guiones

Nosara's Playa Guiones is one of the most consistently good beginner and intermediate surf breaks in Costa Rica — a long, open beach break with predictable swell, warm water, and very little crowd pressure compared to more famous surf towns. The village behind the beach is small, low-key, and strongly oriented around surfing and wellness; the combination gives it a character unlike anywhere else on this itinerary.

Three days of surf lessons is the right amount of time to build genuine confidence in the water. Most schools run lessons in the morning (typically 7–9am or 9–11am) when the onshore wind is lightest and conditions are cleanest.

Well-regarded schools at Playa Guiones include **Safari Surf School**, **Coconut Harry's**, and **Nosara Surf School**. All offer group and private options.

- **Group lesson:** ~$55–70/person (typically 2 hours including board and rash guard)
- **Private lesson:** ~$90–120/person (2 hours, one instructor to one student)
- **Board rental only:** ~$15–25/day for a foam longboard

**⭐ Tip:** Book at least your first lesson in advance, especially in the dry season. Schools fill quickly in peak months and you'll want a confirmed spot for your first morning.

### Sea Kayaking

Sea kayaking along the coastline around Playa Guiones and the adjacent rocky headlands is a quieter alternative to surfing and offers a completely different perspective on the area. The rocky points north and south of Guiones shelter tide pools and small caves, and dolphin sightings are reasonably common. Several operators based near the beach rent single and double kayaks and offer guided half-day coastal paddles.

**Cost:** ~$40–60/person for a guided 2–3 hour coastal kayaking tour; kayak rental alone is around $20–30/half-day.

### Ostional Wildlife Refuge — Olive Ridley Turtle Watching

Approximately 12km north of Nosara along the coast, Ostional Wildlife Refuge hosts one of the largest and most significant Olive Ridley sea turtle nesting sites in the world. The turtles arrive in mass nesting events called **arribadas**, in which tens of thousands of females haul themselves onto the beach over the course of several nights to lay eggs. Arrivals happen throughout the year — typically timed with the new moon — but peak numbers occur between July and December.

Visits are regulated by the refuge and must be done with a licensed local guide. You cannot wander the beach alone at night during nesting events. The ADIO association (Asociación de Desarrollo Integral de Ostional) coordinates access and provides the guides; tours depart from the Ostional village and typically last 1.5–2 hours.

**Cost:** ~$25–35/person including the guide. Timing depends on turtle activity and the lunar calendar; ask your Nosara accommodation to help arrange it once you arrive and know the current conditions.

**⭐ Tip:** Do not bring a white flashlight to the beach — the light disorients turtles. Guides use red-filtered lights. Follow every instruction the guide gives without exception; these regulations exist to protect an extraordinary natural event.

### Stand-Up Paddleboarding

Stand-up paddleboarding is widely available at Playa Guiones and offers a calmer water experience for days when the surf is too powerful for lessons or when you want a morning off from paddling hard. Several surf schools and beach rental stands offer paddleboards by the hour.

**Cost:** ~$20–30/hour for board rental; guided SUP tours along the coast run ~$45–60/person.

### Yoga and Wellness

Nosara has a well-established yoga community that predates the surf scene. The Nosara Yoga Institute is the most established centre in town and offers drop-in classes open to visitors; Bodhi Tree Yoga Resort is another well-regarded option for multi-day retreats or single drop-in sessions. If you want a recovery morning between surf sessions, a yoga class is the obvious choice.

**Cost:** ~$20–30/person for a drop-in class.

### Hotels in Nosara

- **Kaya Sol Surf Camp** — Budget; basic but well-run surf camp accommodation with communal meals and a young, social crowd; ~$35–55/person/night
- **L'Acqua Viva Hotel** — Mid-range; pool, comfortable rooms, short walk to Playa Guiones, good value for the area; ~$80–110/person/night
- **The Harmony Hotel** — High-end; the most design-forward property in Nosara, beautiful gardens, plunge pools, farm-to-table restaurant, adults only; ~$200–300/person/night

## Getting There and Away

Nosara has a small domestic airstrip (NOB) with regular flights to San José operated by Sansa Airlines (~40 minutes, from ~$90/person one-way). If you plan to fly out of Nosara at the end of the trip rather than driving back, book early — capacity is limited and flights sell out, particularly in the dry season.

Flying into San José and driving a loop (San José → La Fortuna → Monteverde → Nosara) and then flying home from Nosara (via San José) is a clean and practical itinerary structure that avoids backtracking.

## Costa Rica Travel Tips

### When to Go

The dry season (December to April) is peak time in Costa Rica — reliable sunshine, better road conditions into Nosara, and easier access to the national parks. Monteverde's cloud forest is misty year-round by definition, so there is no bad season there. Nosara's surf is consistent throughout the year. If turtle watching at Ostional is a priority, the peak arrivals are July to December, overlapping with the green (rainy) season — roads to Nosara will be muddier but the wildlife rewards are significant.

### Currency and Costs

Costa Rica uses the colón (CRC), but US dollars are accepted almost everywhere in tourist areas and many prices are quoted in USD. It is a mid-range destination — more expensive than much of Central America but significantly cheaper than North America or Europe. Budget around $80–120/person/day for a comfortable trip including accommodation, food, activities, and transport.

### Wildlife and Responsible Tourism

Costa Rica takes wildlife conservation seriously, and most of the experiences on this itinerary — the Bogarin Trail, Ostional, Arenal National Park — are regulated precisely to protect the animals they showcase. Follow guide instructions, keep distances, and resist the temptation to handle wildlife or override regulations for a photograph. The experience is always better when done properly.

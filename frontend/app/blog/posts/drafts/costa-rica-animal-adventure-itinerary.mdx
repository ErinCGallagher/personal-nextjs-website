Costa Rica punches well above its weight in biodiversity. Covering just 0.03% of the Earth's surface, it shelters around 6% of the world's species — everything from resplendent quetzals in the cloud forest to humpback whales off the Osa Peninsula. This 10–12 day loop was designed with a single priority: seeing as much wildlife as possible, in as many different ecosystems as possible.

The route moves through three dramatically different landscapes: the cool, misty cloud forests of Monteverde at 1,600m; the Pacific lowland rainforest and beaches of Manuel Antonio; and the remote, roadless wilderness of Corcovado on the Osa Peninsula — arguably the most biodiverse place on Earth. Each stop rewards patience and early starts.

## Route Overview

**San José → Monteverde (4 nights) → Quepos / Manuel Antonio (2 nights) → Drake Bay & Corcovado (4 nights) → San José**

Total driving and travel time is substantial — Costa Rica's roads are slow and the Osa Peninsula involves a boat transfer — but the pacing gives you enough time in each place to not feel rushed.

## Getting Around

All legs of this itinerary are driveable in a standard car except the final stretch to Drake Bay, which requires either a boat transfer from Sierpe or a rough unpaved road. A 4WD with high clearance is strongly recommended throughout Costa Rica, especially in the rainy season (May–November) when roads deteriorate. Budget travellers often use the shuttle bus network (Interbus, Tropical Tours) as a cheaper alternative to car rental, though this limits flexibility.

## Monteverde: Days 1–4

The drive from San José to Monteverde takes around 3–3.5 hours. The last 35 km into Santa Elena is unpaved and slow — allow extra time. Aim to arrive before dark. Stay in Santa Elena town rather than the reserves; it has better restaurant options and is more convenient for supplies.

### Where to Stay in Monteverde

| Option | Property | Price per person/night |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | Sloth Backpackers | ~$20–28 |
| Mid-range | Hotel El Establo Mountain Resort | ~$65–90 |
| High-end | Monteverde Lodge & Gardens | ~$130–180 |

### Day 1: Curi-Cancha Reserve

Curi-Cancha Reserve is one of the best-kept secrets in Monteverde. It caps visitors at 50 at any time, which means you'll almost never feel crowded — a stark contrast to the famous Cloud Forest Reserve. The reserve spans 7 km of trails across primary and secondary forest at 1,450–1,615m altitude.

**Suggested trail loop:** Combine the Morpho, Leo, Puma, and Alondra trails for a moderate 2.7-mile circuit with around 660 ft of elevation gain. Allow 3–4 hours at a comfortable wildlife-watching pace.

This reserve consistently produces resplendent quetzal sightings, particularly during fruiting season (January–May when quetzals nest). You're also very likely to see howler and white-faced capuchin monkeys, coatis, and a remarkable variety of hummingbirds. Entry: **$25 per adult**.

**⭐ Tip:** Arrive at opening time (7:00 AM) for the best wildlife activity and the calmest light for photography.

**Evening: Night Walk at Curi-Cancha**

The same reserve runs a structured night walk from 5:40–7:30 PM. This is an excellent add-on — you're already familiar with the terrain, and the guides know where resident sloths, armadillos, and kinkajous tend to shelter. Booking is required in advance at [reservacuricancha.com](https://reservacuricancha.com/en/tours/1-caminata-nocturna.html). Cost: **$35 per adult**.

Have dinner in Santa Elena afterwards — the town has a solid range of restaurants for all budgets.

### Day 2: Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve

Book your entry slot well in advance at [cloudforestmonteverde.com](https://cloudforestmonteverde.com/). As of December 2025, the reserve has introduced three circuit options with one-way directional trails and pre-booked time slots. Book the 7:30 AM entry for the best wildlife activity and to beat the midday tour groups. Entry: **$29 per adult per circuit**.

**Recommended circuit:** Sendero Bosque Nuboso (1.9 km, 65m gain, ~1.5 hours) leads to the La Ventana overlook directly on the Continental Divide — on a clear morning you can see both the Pacific and Caribbean slopes. Continue onto Sendero Chomogo (1.8 km, 150m gain), which reaches 1,680m and runs along a steep ridge at canopy level. Complete the loop via Sendero Roble, Chomogo, and Tosi. The full circuit is approximately 2.6 miles and the top-rated route on AllTrails (4.8 stars, 202 reviews).

Highlights include a suspended bridge at 100m height with canopy-level views, dense epiphyte gardens, and if you're lucky, a resplendent quetzal on the Chomogo ridge. Allow 4–5 hours for the full circuit.

**Afternoon:** Bring a packed lunch to eat at the La Ventana viewpoint. If you have energy, the 3.3 km extension to the waterfall via Sendero El Río is worth the extra hour. Otherwise, return to Santa Elena for a leisurely afternoon.

**Dinner recommendations:** Trio (creative local cuisine, good vegetarian options) or Chimera (tapas-style, relaxed atmosphere) — both are reliable in Santa Elena.

### Days 3–4: Optional Extras in Monteverde

Use your remaining days to choose from these activities based on your interests:

**Canopy zip-line tour (~$60–85 per adult):** Selvatura Park and Sky Adventures both operate well-regarded zip-line circuits with multiple platforms and hanging bridges. Sky Adventures specifically combines zip-lines with a tram and hanging bridges. Not primarily a wildlife experience, but tremendous fun and good for aerial canopy views.

**Coffee or chocolate tour (~$30–45 per adult):** Don Juan Coffee offers a combined tour covering coffee, chocolate, and sugar cane production. Genuinely informative and a good way to understand the agricultural landscape of the region.

**Butterfly and insect garden at Monteverde (~$20 per adult):** The Monteverde Butterfly Garden houses four species of the famous blue morpho butterfly alongside leaf-cutter ant colonies and a variety of stick insects. A compact but engaging 1–2 hour visit, particularly worthwhile if you have children in the group.

**Early morning birding walk (~$40–60 per adult with a local guide):** Consider hiring a local birding guide for a dawn walk in the secondary forest around Santa Elena. Quetzal sightings are more likely with an expert who knows the fruiting trees. Ask your hotel for a guide recommendation.

**⭐ Tip:** Don't try to fit everything in. Monteverde rewards slow travel — an extra hour spent quietly at a single fruiting tree will often produce better wildlife sightings than rushing between five paid attractions.

## Quepos / Manuel Antonio: Days 5–6

The drive from Monteverde to Quepos takes approximately 4 hours on paved roads via the Interamericana highway. It's a straightforward drive by Costa Rican standards. Stay in Quepos town rather than along the Manuel Antonio road — it's significantly better value and has more genuine local atmosphere. The national park is a 20-minute drive or short bus ride away.

### Where to Stay in Quepos

| Option | Property | Price per person/night |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | Hotel Villa Romántica | ~$25–35 |
| Mid-range | Hotel Si Como No | ~$80–110 |
| High-end | Arenas del Mar Beachfront & Rainforest Resort | ~$175–250 |

### Day 5 Afternoon: Mangrove Boat Tour

Arrive in Quepos by early afternoon and head straight for a mangrove estuary tour on the Damas Island mangroves — one of the largest mangrove systems on the Pacific coast of Central America. Tours typically run 2–3 hours by small boat through the estuary channels.

Wildlife commonly spotted: American crocodiles, caimans, green iguanas, Jesus Christ lizards (basilisks), boat-billed herons, and three-toed sloths sleeping in the red mangrove branches. Several operators run tours from Quepos, including Iguana Tours and Planet Dolphin. Cost: **$45–60 per adult**.

### Day 6: Manuel Antonio National Park

Book your entry online in advance — the park caps daily visitors and frequently sells out, particularly on weekends. The park is open Tuesday through Sunday (closed Mondays). Entry: **$20 per adult**.

Hire a guide at the park entrance for a half-day morning walk (**$25–35 per adult for a shared group guide**, or ~$80–100 for a private guide). A guide makes an enormous difference here: they carry spotting scopes, know where the resident sloths sleep, and can locate cryptically camouflaged animals that you would walk straight past.

Wildlife you can reasonably expect to see: three-fingered sloths (both two-toed and three-toed species), white-faced capuchin monkeys (highly habituated and often very close), howler monkeys, coatis, agoutis, and scarlet macaws flying over the beach. Humpback whales are visible offshore from August to October.

The park also has several beautiful Pacific beaches — Cathedral Beach and Playa Manuel Antonio are both protected and excellent for swimming.

**Sunset catamaran cruise:** Several operators run evening catamaran cruises from Quepos that include snorkelling, dinner, and open bar. Reputable options include Planet Dolphin and Ocean King Catamaran. The sunset light along this coastline is consistently dramatic. Cost: **$85–110 per adult** (typically 4 hours, with dinner and drinks included).

## Drake Bay & Corcovado: Days 7–10

### Getting to Drake Bay

This is the most logistically involved leg of the trip, and it's worth planning carefully.

**The standard route (recommended):** Drive from Quepos to Sierpe (approximately 3.5–4 hours via the Costanera Sur highway). Leave your car in paid parking in Sierpe — it's not worth attempting to drive to Drake Bay. From Sierpe, take a public or private boat transfer down the Sierpe River and across the open ocean to Drake Bay. The boat trip takes 1–1.5 hours and is genuinely exciting — the river section passes through mangroves and the open ocean section frequently produces dolphin sightings. Cost of boat transfer: **$15–25 per person on a shared public boat**, or **$80–120 for a private boat**.

**⭐ Tip:** The public boat from Sierpe departs at approximately 11:30 AM. Confirm times locally as schedules vary. Arriving late in Sierpe means either an expensive private boat or an overnight stay.

**Alternative: Domestic flight.** SANSA and Skyway operate small-plane flights from San José or Quepos to Drake Bay. This eliminates the Sierpe boat and saves considerable time, but costs **$90–160 per person one-way** and requires returning to San José first if you're coming from Quepos.

**The rough road option:** A 4WD track exists from Rincón to Drake Bay, but it's genuinely difficult, frequently impassable in rainy season, and not recommended unless you have a very capable vehicle and local guidance. Don't attempt it without asking at your Drake Bay lodge first.

### Where to Stay in Drake Bay

| Option | Property | Price per person/night |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | Cabinas Jade Mar | ~$30–50 |
| Mid-range | Drake Bay Wilderness Resort | ~$75–110 |
| High-end | Aguila de Osa Inn | ~$175–240 |

**⭐ Tip:** Several lodges in Drake Bay have their own forest trails with resident wildlife. Aguila de Osa and Drake Bay Wilderness Resort both have scarlet macaws, toucans, and occasionally tapirs in their grounds at dawn. If budget allows, a lodge with forest grounds adds significant wildlife value without any extra effort.

### Day 7: Arrival and Recovery

After the drive and boat transfer, you'll arrive in Drake Bay by early to mid afternoon. This is a good day to settle in, explore the town's short coastal path at dusk, and look and listen for the scarlet macaws that roost in the trees around the village at sunset. Drake Bay has a small cluster of restaurants — dinner options are limited but the fresh seafood is excellent.

### Day 8: Corcovado National Park — Full Day

Book this day well in advance. Corcovado requires a licensed guide, and guides are in high demand. Most Drake Bay lodges can arrange this, or book directly with operators such as Osa Wild or Drake Bay Backpackers.

The standard day trip from Drake Bay visits Sirena station via the coastal trail — a round trip of approximately 10 km along the Pacific coast. The full day runs roughly 5:30 AM to 5:00 PM, including the boat transfer to the park entrance at San Pedrillo or the coastal walk in. Guide cost: **$85–130 per person for a day tour** (guide fee + park entry of $18 per person included in most packages).

Sirena is regarded as one of the best places on Earth to see large mammals in the wild. Commonly seen on this route: Baird's tapirs (frequently spotted on the beach and river crossing), American crocodiles, white-lipped peccaries, spider monkeys, squirrel monkeys, and three species of big cats are resident (jaguar, puma, and ocelot — sightings are uncommon but real). Scarlet macaws are effectively guaranteed in large numbers. Bring plenty of water, sun protection, and a packed lunch — Sirena has no food facilities.

### Day 9: Isla del Caño Snorkelling Tour

Isla del Caño is a biological reserve approximately 19 km offshore from Drake Bay. It's one of the best snorkelling and diving sites on the Pacific coast of Costa Rica, and a UNESCO-recognised archaeological site with pre-Columbian stone spheres.

The standard tour departs Drake Bay by boat at around 8:00 AM and includes two snorkelling spots plus time to walk the island's short trail. Marine life is excellent: white-tip reef sharks, manta rays (seasonal), hawksbill turtles, moray eels, and very dense fish life including parrotfish, snappers, and angelfish. Whale sharks pass through seasonally (July–October). Humpback whales are commonly sighted from the boat from August to October — Drake Bay sits directly in their migration corridor.

Reputable operators include Drake Bay Backpackers and Osa Wild. Cost: **$80–100 per person** (typically includes equipment and lunch on the boat).

### Day 10: Rest Day in Drake Bay

After two intensive early-start days, use this day more gently. Options:

**Guided birding walk in the lodge grounds or local forest (~$40–60 per adult):** Drake Bay's biodiversity rivals Corcovado itself. A 2-hour dawn birding walk around the village and nearby forest can produce toucans, trogons, antbirds, tanagers, and hummingbirds. Ask your lodge to arrange a local guide.

**Kayaking the coastline (~$25–40 per adult for equipment rental):** The calm morning sea around Drake Bay is well suited to kayaking. The rocky headlands shelter sea turtles and pelicans, and dolphins frequently pass the bay in the early morning.

**Second half of the day:** Rest, read, and swim from the beach. Drake Bay has no infrastructure pressure and rewards doing nothing in particular.

## Day 11–12: Drake Bay to San José

### Return Journey

The return from Drake Bay to San José is a long day — plan for it rather than being surprised by it.

**Boat + drive route (most travellers):** Take the morning boat from Drake Bay back to Sierpe (departs approximately 7:00 AM, confirm locally the evening before). From Sierpe, the drive to San José is approximately 4.5–5 hours. Total travel time with the boat: around 6–7 hours. This is manageable in a single day if your flight departs in the evening, but tight for a morning departure.

**Breaking the journey:** If you have an extra day (12-day itinerary), stopping overnight in Quepos on the way back is a pleasant option. It's 4 hours from Sierpe to Quepos, and then 2.5–3 hours from Quepos to San José the following morning. This also gives you time to revisit Manuel Antonio if you want a second look or missed anything.

**Domestic flight option:** SANSA flights from Drake Bay or Palmar Sur to San José cost approximately **$90–160 per person** and cut the journey to under an hour. Worth serious consideration if your schedule is tight.

## Recommended Additions

These are the activities most likely to significantly improve the wildlife experience of this itinerary. They didn't fit neatly into the day-by-day structure, but are worth planning around.

**Dawn birding at Curi-Cancha (Monteverde, Day 1 or 3):** Hiring a local birding guide for the first 90 minutes of daylight at Curi-Cancha is the single best thing you can do to improve your chance of seeing a resplendent quetzal. They are present year-round but easiest to find in the early morning when males make display flights. Cost: **$40–60 per adult** with a local guide.

**Olive ridley turtle nesting (Manuel Antonio, September–October):** The beaches near Quepos see olive ridley turtle nesting from September through November. Playa Biesanz just north of Manuel Antonio is a quiet spot to look at night without a guide, or ask your hotel about organised night walks. No additional cost if self-guided.

**Night walk at your Drake Bay lodge (Days 7–10):** Several lodges have resident naturalist guides who lead walks in the lodge grounds after dark. This is often where you'll see the smallest and strangest residents — red-eyed tree frogs, tent-making bats, fishing spiders, and occasionally kinkajous. Confirm with your lodge whether this is included or costs extra (~$20–30 per person if charged).

**Whale watching from Sierpe/Drake Bay (August–October):** If your dates overlap with humpback season, consider adding a dedicated 3-hour whale watching boat from Drake Bay before or after the Corcovado day. Several operators offer this as a standalone tour for **$65–85 per adult**. The Osa Peninsula corridor is one of the best places in the Americas to see humpbacks.

## Planning Notes

**Best time to visit:** December to April is the dry season and the most comfortable for hiking. May to November brings heavy rain, particularly in the Osa Peninsula, but also full rivers, lush green forest, and fewer tourists. Corcovado's Sirena station can be inaccessible in peak wet season — confirm accessibility when booking. The quetzal nesting season (February–May) is the best window for Monteverde.

**What to pack:** Waterproof hiking boots (mandatory for Corcovado — the river crossings are thigh-deep in wet season), a good rain jacket, insect repellent with DEET, a light headlamp, quick-dry clothing, and a small dry bag for camera gear on boat transfers. Binoculars are close to essential for getting the most out of every wildlife sighting.

**Cash:** The Osa Peninsula runs almost entirely on cash. Bring sufficient colones or USD from San José or Quepos before heading to Drake Bay. ATMs exist in Quepos and Sierpe but not reliably beyond that.

---

Costa Rica is one of the few places on Earth where a relatively short trip can take you from cloud forest to lowland jungle to open ocean with genuinely world-class wildlife at each stop. The Osa Peninsula in particular has a quality and density of primary forest that is now extremely rare in Central America. Go slowly, hire local guides, and let the early mornings do the work.

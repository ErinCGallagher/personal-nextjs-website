Costa Rica is one of those countries where the hardest part isn't getting there — it's deciding what to do once you arrive. In two weeks you can move between cloud forests, Caribbean beaches, active volcanoes, and world-class surf breaks, but you can't do all of it. The country looks small on a map, but driving times are long and the roads don't let you rush.

These four itineraries cover the best ways to see Costa Rica, each built around a different travel priority. Find the one that matches what you're after, then follow it to the full breakdown.

---

## 1. Resort + Adventure

**Route**: Guanacaste → Monteverde
**Duration**: 8–10 days

Guanacaste's Pacific coast is Costa Rica's most developed resort region — calm beaches, reliable sunshine, and infrastructure built for comfort. From there, a 3–4 hour drive inland takes you to Monteverde's cloud forest, which feels like a different country entirely: zip lines through the canopy, hanging suspension bridges, and some of the best birdwatching in Central America. This is the itinerary for anyone who wants a genuine mix of relaxation and adventure without sacrificing either.

**Driving**: Guanacaste to Monteverde is approximately 3–4 hours, mostly paved with a rough final stretch into town.

## 2. Animal Adventure

**Route**: Monteverde → Manuel Antonio → Drake Bay & Corcovado
**Duration**: 10–12 days

This route hits Costa Rica's three most wildlife-dense regions back to back. Monteverde's cloud forest is prime habitat for resplendent quetzals, howler monkeys, and hundreds of bird species. Manuel Antonio combines a national park teeming with sloths, capuchins, and scarlet macaws with a beautiful Pacific beach. The remote Osa Peninsula — home to Corcovado National Park — is where serious wildlife seekers go: tapirs, peccaries, and all four of Costa Rica's monkey species are possible in a single day.

**Driving**: Monteverde to Manuel Antonio is roughly 3–4 hours. Manuel Antonio to Drake Bay requires a combination of driving and a boat transfer, or a short domestic flight.

## 3. Adventure & Surfing

**Route**: La Fortuna & Arenal → Monteverde → Nosara
**Duration**: 10 nights

This itinerary opens at Arenal — Costa Rica's most iconic volcano — with two nights split between a sloth sanctuary walk, a waterfall hike, and an evening soaking in natural hot springs. From there, a scenic drive along Lake Arenal leads to Monteverde for three nights of cloud forest hiking, horseback riding with mountain views, guided birdwatching, and a rainforest night walk. The trip finishes with five nights in Nosara on the Nicoya Peninsula, where the agenda is surf lessons, kayaking, and — if the timing is right — watching Olive Ridley sea turtles nest at nearby Ostional Wildlife Refuge.

**Driving**: La Fortuna to Monteverde is 2–3 hours along the lake. Monteverde to Nosara is about 3–4 hours through Guanacaste's dry forest. Total driving is very manageable spread over 10 nights.

## 4. Caribbean & Volcanoes

**Route**: Tortuguero → Puerto Viejo de Talamanca → Juan Castro Blanco → La Fortuna & Arenal → Monteverde
**Duration**: 10–14 days

This itinerary covers sides of Costa Rica most visitors miss entirely. Tortuguero is only accessible by boat and sits at the centre of one of the world's most important sea turtle nesting sites. Puerto Viejo brings a completely different mood — Afro-Caribbean culture, reggae, Caribbean food, and the coral reefs of Cahuita National Park. Juan Castro Blanco National Park is an off-the-beaten-path cloud forest stop en route to La Fortuna, far less visited than Monteverde and all the better for it. La Fortuna and Arenal deliver iconic volcano views, white-water rafting, and hot springs, and Monteverde rounds things out with cloud forest hiking before the flight home.

**Driving**: Tortuguero requires a boat transfer to reach. Puerto Viejo to Juan Castro Blanco is roughly 3–4 hours; Juan Castro Blanco to La Fortuna is about 1.5 hours; La Fortuna to Monteverde is 2–3 hours. This is the most logistically involved of the four routes — 10–14 days gives you room to do it without rushing.

## Which Should You Choose?

| You want... | Best itinerary |
|---|---|
| Beach resort base with one big adventure | Resort + Adventure |
| Maximum wildlife and jungle time | Animal Adventure |
| Surfing, waterfalls, and cloud forest | Adventure & Surfing |
| Caribbean coast and volcanic highlands | Caribbean & Volcanoes |

If you're still not sure, the Adventure & Surfing itinerary is a strong default for first-time visitors — it hits the volcano and cloud forest highlights before finishing at one of Costa Rica's most beautiful and uncrowded beaches.

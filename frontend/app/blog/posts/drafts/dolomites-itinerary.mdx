The Dolomites were already on our radar before we visited in June 2022, but nothing quite prepares you for seeing mountains rising out of the alpine meadows for the first time. We spent nine days hiking, staying in campsites and alpine huts, and squeezed in a paragliding session above Kronplatz. This itinerary covers the best hikes we did, the best viewpoints, where we stayed and honest notes about what's worth the effort and what's more about the iconic photo than the experience.

There are many multi day hikes in the Dolomites but our itinerary is based on day hikes from two different bases — Val Gardena and Cortina d'Ampezzo. This allows you to experience a wider variety of trails and landscapes without the hassle of packing up camp every day, and gives you the flexibility to take rest days when needed.

## Do You Need a Rental Car?

Yes, getting around the Dolomites without a car is difficult. The region spans a large area across South Tyrol and Veneto, and most trailheads are nowhere near a bus stop. We drove between three different areas during our trip, and the flexibility to leave at different times, park at different trailheads, and move our camp between valleys was essential. Driving is straightforward once you're out of the larger towns, the roads are well-maintained and the mountain scenery makes the drive part of the experience.

## Where to Start

When we visited the Dolomites we were on an extended 3 months road trip driving through Europe. We came from Milan but you have a few a few different options if you are flying from overseas or more regionally.

**International Airports:**

1. Venice Marco Polo Airport (VCE), about a 3.5 hour drive to Val Gardena.
2. Milano Malpensa Airport (MXP), about a 4 hour drive to the Val Gardena.
3. Munich Airport (MUC), about 4.5 hours away, the drive is stunning and takes you through the Austrian Alps before reaching the Dolomites.

**Regional Airports:**

1. Innsbruck Airport (INN), about 1.5 hours away.
2. Bolzano Airport (BZO), about 1 hour away.

## Day 1: Arrival in the Dolomites

Depending on how you are arriving, the first day will likely be spent on the road. We were driving from Milan which was a 4 hour drive to Val Gardena, a valley in the northern part of the Dolomites that is home to some of the most iconic views of the region. Val Gardena was our base for the first part of our trip, and was a great central location for exploring the valley.

### Val Di Funes Detour

If you have time on your drive in, we highly recommend a detour to Val di Funes, a valley just north of Val Gardena that is home to the iconic Church of St. Magdalena with the Odle peaks rising behind it. The church is a popular photo spot and can get busy, but it's worth stopping for a quick walk around the grounds and soaking in the views.

## Day 2: Seceda

We arrived in the Val Gardena and went straight for the most dramatic viewpoint in the valley. Seceda set the tone for the entire trip.

**Seceda Hike:** ([All Trails Link](https://www.alltrails.com/en-gb/trail/italy/south-tyrol/col-raiser-seceda-forcella-pana-rifugio-firenze)) We chose to do the circular loop trail starting from the top of the cable. To get to the starting point you get on a cable car at the Ortisei gondola station, and with a quick transfer you are brought up to the Seceda ridge at 2,519m. The hike itself is not on the ridge, but on a grass valley which gives you beautiful views of the iconic sheer vertical cliffs of Seceda. The tallest peaks you'll be able to see from the top are the neighbouring peaks of Furchetta and Sass Rigais. The hike itself follows an easy path descending through the alpine meadow but there is a 500m of elevation gain on the way back up.

We based ourselves at **Camping Gamp**, a well-equipped campsite in the Ortisei area, ideally situated for exploring the Val Gardena valley.

### Gardena Card (Cable Cars)

You will quickly realize that in the dolomites cable cars are one of the most expensive activities, and critical to getting to the best hikes and viewpoints. If you are planning to do more than one hike in the Val Gardena, the Gardena Card is a great value. It gives you unlimited access to all the cable cars in the valley for 3 or 6 days. It's not cheap at 124,00 € or 160,00 €, but a single return ticket on Seceda, for example, will cost 74,00 € per person.

## Day 3: Malga Sanon

A more relaxed day in the valley, we spent it wandering trails through the alpine meadows and made time for one of our favourite stops of the trip.

**Malga Sanon:** This traditional alpine dairy hut sits in the open pastures above the valley and is the perfect excuse to take the weight off your boots. We stopped for an Aperol Spritz and soaked in the views — a classic Tyrolean rifugio experience and a great introduction to the mountain hut culture that defines hiking in the Dolomites.

## Day 4: Sassolungo

Sassolungo is one of the most recognisable silhouettes in the Dolomites — a jagged cluster of peaks rising sharply above the surrounding meadow, with a summit at 3,181m.

**Sassolungo Hike:** Getting to the trailhead involves three cable cars, the last of which is memorable for all the wrong reasons: narrow, coffin-like cabins that fit just two people at a time. From the top (the hike peaks at 2,685m), the trail skirts around the outside of the mountain with views of Seceda and the distant Marmolada glacier. The final few kilometres felt long on already-tired legs, but the scale of the rocky landscape keeps you moving.

## Day 5: Gampen Alm Overnight

We left Camping Gamp and traded it for a night in the mountains at a rifugio.

**Hike to Gampen Alm:** We left the car at the Zanser Alm parking lot and hiked the 4km up to the hut, gaining just under 400m in elevation. The first half passes through shaded forest before breaking out into open alpine meadow, with Seceda and Sass Rigais filling the skyline behind you. **Gampen Alm** is a family-run rifugio accessible primarily on foot; the owner sat down with every table at dinner, served a generous three-course Tyrolean meal, and we watched the sun set from deck chairs with cattle grazing on the slope below. Breakfast the next morning was equally hearty before the easy hike back down.

## Day 6: Kronplatz Paragliding

After the overnight, we drove to our new base near Cortina d'Ampezzo and checked in at **Camping Rocchetta**, a campsite in a valley setting on the edge of town.

**Paragliding at Kronplatz:** We spent the afternoon on a tandem paragliding flight above Kronplatz, one of the best spots for it in South Tyrol. Flying above the Dolomites with those limestone towers stretching in every direction is a perspective you won't get any other way.

## Day 7: Relaxing Day in Cortina d'Ampezzo

After five big days, we took a proper rest day in one of the most beautiful mountain towns in Italy. Cortina d'Ampezzo is elegant and walkable with good coffee, good gelato, and plenty of shops worth browsing — exactly what aching legs needed before the two best hikes of the trip.

## Day 8: Mt. Lagazuoi & WWII Trenches

Our favourite hike of the entire trip, and it wasn't close.

**Mt. Lagazuoi Hike:** During World War I, the Dolomites formed the front line between Italy and the Austro-Hungarian Empire, and Mt. Lagazuoi is an open-air museum of that conflict — hand-carved trenches, mountain tunnels, and machine gun nests built into the rock by the Austrian Kaiserjäger elite unit still line the trail. We hiked the Kaiserjäger via ferrata, a beginner-friendly route with iron rungs and cables that makes the steep 800m ascent feel like an adventure, and reaching the summit rewarded us with some of the finest views in the Dolomites.

## Day 9: Tre Cime di Lavaredo

No Dolomites itinerary would be complete without Tre Cime di Lavaredo — the three towering chimneys that appear on every poster of the region.

**Tre Cime di Lavaredo Hike:** Starting from the Rifugio Auronzo car park, the 10km loop circles the three peaks and climbs around 450m. It's worth doing for the experience, but honestly, the peaks are almost too large to see from the trail — you need distance to appreciate them. If you have time, the nearby Cadini di Misurina trail (accessible from the same car park) is shorter, less crowded, and delivers more dramatic views of Tre Cime than the Tre Cime trail itself does.

## Tips & Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best time to visit the Dolomites for hiking?

Late June through early September is the sweet spot. Snow clears from most trails by mid-June, the days are long, and the alpine meadows are in full bloom. We visited in late June 2022 and conditions were excellent, warm enough to hike in a t-shirt during the day, cool enough at altitude to keep it comfortable. July and August are the busiest months; if crowds bother you, the shoulder weeks of late June or early September are ideal.

### How difficult are the hikes?

Most of the hikes in this itinerary are rated moderate, with the Lagazuoi via ferrata being the most technical. Via ferrata routes use fixed iron rungs and cables bolted into the rock — you don't need climbing experience, but you do need a via ferrata kit (harness, helmet, and set of lanyards). These can be rented in most outdoor shops in Ortisei or Cortina. Everything else on this list is a standard hiking trail with good signage.

### What should I pack for hiking in the Dolomites?

The weather can change quickly at altitude. Even on a warm summer day, carry a waterproof layer, an extra mid-layer, and sun protection — the UV exposure above 2,000m is intense. Sturdy hiking boots with ankle support are essential; trail runners are fine for easier trails but not recommended for scrambling terrain. Trekking poles help significantly on the steep descents.

### What is a rifugio?

A rifugio (plural: rifugi) is a mountain hut that provides meals, drinks, and often overnight accommodation. They range from simple unmanned shelters to full-service family-run operations like Gampen Alm. Most serve hearty Tyrolean food — think goulash, Käsespätzle (cheese noodles), and strudel. You don't need to hike in just to eat; many are reachable by gondola and are a great stop for lunch on a shorter day.

### Do I need to book rifugio stays in advance?

Yes, especially for overnight stays in summer. Popular huts fill up weeks in advance. Day visits for food and drinks are usually fine without a reservation, but always worth calling ahead if you're planning around a specific stop.

### Is the Dolomites trip suitable for beginners?

The hiking is accessible for anyone with reasonable fitness and the right footwear. The via ferrata on Lagazuoi adds a technical element but is rated beginner-friendly — the iron rungs do the hard work. If you've never done a via ferrata before, consider renting gear and taking a half-day guided introduction before tackling a full route independently.

Egypt was one of the most rewarding trips we've ever taken, and also one of the most confusing to plan. There's no shortage of information online, but a lot of it is contradictory, overly cautious, or written by people who did a package tour and saw Egypt through the window of a bus. This guide is based on a trip we planned ourselves, half independently and half on a Nile river cruise, and covers everything we wish we'd known before booking.

## Arriving in Egypt

Egypt has a straightforward visa-on-arrival process for many Western passport holders, but check before you book your flight. At the airport, you'll find many counters just before immigration where you must purchase a visa sticker: **bring $30 USD in cash**. The fee is fixed and they will not accept other currencies or cards. There are ATMs in the room where you can take out USD if you didn't bring any. After purchasing your sticker, you join the immigration queue and the border agent will put the sticker in your passport. The entire process only took 20 minutes for us, but that can change depending on how many flights arrive at the same time. The whole system felt very chaotic, but since you now know you need a sticker, you can join any queue to buy one from the many vendors, and there's no ambiguity. Just make sure you have your arrival card filled in too, your airline should have handed one to you, but you can also get them in this bustling room.

Cairo International Airport (CAI) is the main entry point to the country. Giza is about 1-2 hour's drive from the airport depending on traffic, and Cairo traffic can be serious, so factor this in if you have an early activity planned the next morning. Remember that the traffic can be bad for your return trip to Cairo airport!

### Transportation from the airport

Although you can take Ubers for shorter distances, we wouldn't recommend relying on them for airport transfers. Instead, arrange a private transfer through your hotel or use booking.com or trip.com to book a private transfer. We've used both Booking and Trip through our travels around the world and they have worked really well (we don't have any affiliation with either platform, we just found that they were good). Check both sites to see which has the better price for Egypt. They will have a driver waiting for you at the airport with a sign and your payment is processed in advance through the app, so you don't have to stress about cash after a long flight. You provide flight information and they monitor for delays, so if your flight is late they will adjust the pickup time accordingly.

## Is Egypt Safe?

The short answer is yes. Egypt is a popular tourist destination, and the country has a significant economic interest in keeping visitors safe, and the tourist sites are well-patrolled. We never felt unsafe, not in Cairo, not in Luxor, not in Aswan, not walking around after dark in any of these cities. You should expect some aggressive panhandlers at tourist sites but these are more of a nuisance rather than actual danger. It is recommended not to make eye contact if you're not looking to buy something, and a firm "no thank you" if they persist. 

Common scams to be aware of:

- Don't photograph camels: camel owners will aggressively try to get you to take photos of their camels, and then demand a tip afterward. Don't take the bait, just say "no thank you" and keep walking.
- Speaking of camels - camel ride prices are strictly regulated by the government. There are plenty of signs, so for check the price before asking a vendor for a ride.
- No photos are free: People will often offer to take your photo for you at tourist sites, and then demand a tip afterward. Ask your guide for photos, or expect to pay.
- Nothing is $1: That's not the cost of the souvenir, that's the cost of the bag that it comes in.
- Vendors inside or right outside temples can be very expensive. Expect to pay 4-5x the normal price for simple things like water, snacks, or soda. You can bargain, but they are pretty firm.

The standard travel precautions apply: be aware of your surroundings, don't flash expensive gear, and use pre-booked transport.

## Finding a Good Guide

"Egyptologist" is not a protected or regulated title in Egypt: anyone can call themselves one regardless of their training or knowledge. The fact that someone has a university degree does not automatically make them a great guide; we met guides with credentials who were dull and/or surface-level, and guides without formal qualifications who were exceptional.

What actually makes a great guide is their ability to tell compelling stories, read hieroglyphs in context, connect sites to each other, and translate the abstract history into something you can actually feel standing inside a 3,500-year-old tomb.

**Our recommendation:** We would highly recommend our guide **Mostafa El Hefny** for the Luxor-Aswan portion of your trip. He is one of the best guides we've ever had anywhere in the world: a genuine Egyptologist, a great storyteller, passionate about the history, and one of the few guides we encountered who can actually read hieroglyphs, which adds an enormous amount to the experience. You can contact him on WhatsApp at **+20 106 132 6028**.

For the Giza portion, guides can be booked through your accommodation or through sites like GetYourGuide/Viator. Ask specifically whether your guide can read hieroglyphs, and ask to see reviews for that guide in particular. If you know someone who visited recently, ask them for a recommendation. Unfortunately we couldn't recommend the guide we had in Giza, booked through Tripadvisor.

## Picking a Nile Cruise

Although we felt that it was easy enough to book Giza ourselves without an organised all-inclusive tour, we were really happy that we went with a tour for the Luxor and Aswan portion along the Nile river. There are a lot of sites to see and a tour really simplified the process and made us feel really comfortable. There are dozens of cruise companies, and the range in quality is significant, so we wanted to share our experience and some tips for picking the right one for you.

### Which Direction?

There are two main options:

1. **Aswan to Luxor**
2. **Luxor to Aswan**

Honestly, the direction doesn't matter as much as the marketing tends to suggest. You will get to see the same temples and the Nile looks the same from both directions. Certain cruise companies will offer different directions starting on different days of the week, so if you have a specific cruise in mind that only departs in one direction, that can be a deciding factor.

### Tour Company

There are a ton of different tour and cruise companies you can book with in Egypt. Nile Cruisen, Luxor & Aswan Travel, and Viking Cruises to name a few. You typically don't book directly with the cruise company, but rather through a travel agent who has a block of rooms on the ship. There will likely be many different tour groups on the same ship at the same time, but this is common practice in Egypt and doesn't affect the experience.

You will also have the option to book a private guide or a shared guide for the excursions. This guide will be with you for the duration of the cruise (they sleep on board), and will be the one leading you through the temples and telling you the stories. We would highly recommend booking a private guide for this portion of the trip, as they will be able to tailor the experience to your interests, adjust your itinerary to suit your pace and answer your questions in more depth. The shared guides are often rushed and have to cater to a larger group, which can detract from the experience.

Typically you have a choice between a 4-day cruise or a 5-day cruise. The 5-day option gives you more time at the temples and a more relaxed pace, but the 4-day option is still very good if you're short on time. We went with the 5-day option but felt that the 4-day option would have been sufficient for us, and saved us some money.

Whatever company you use, ask specifically:

- What's included in the price (meals, excursions, airport transfers)?
- How many other ships will be docked at each temple at the same time? (this varies a lot depending on the time of year)
- What is the guide-to-passenger ratio on excursions?

### Our Pick

We booked through **[Nile Cruisen](https://nilecruisen.com/)**, one of the many travel agents in Egypt, and sailed on the **MS Miriam** (Luxury category). We'd recommend them without hesitation. Our guide, an independent contractor who works with many different tour companies, said that Nile Cruisen really goes above and beyond and organizes a great itinerary. They offer 3 tiers of boat, Standard, Deluxe and Luxury, and their website has tons of photos of each ship which made us feel really confident when booking. We were told by the travel agent that food is one of the main factors that changes between the different ship categories. When requesting a quote, they will narrow the options down to two boats of similar quality, and you won't find out the exact ship until the day before you set sail. We ended up being on the [MS Miriam](https://nilecruisen.com/cruise/ms-miriam-nile-cruise/), but the alternative ship was the [Medea](https://nilecruisen.com/cruise/medea-nile-cruise/) and it was equally nice.

The MS Miriam is a smaller ship with only 24 cabins, which means fewer passengers and a more intimate experience. The staff were all excellent, the food was really good, and the boat really felt luxurious. The rooftop deck had a pool and was a great place to relax and watch the Nile go by. During the day there was free tea, coffee and snacks available in the lounge, and you can always order drinks at the bar (alcohol and canned beverages are extra). We had a double room which had a beautiful juliet balcony, was very well air conditioned, and had a very decent sized bathroom with hot water and good pressure. The excursions were well organized and the guide was fantastic. We would recommend asking Nile Cruisen to book our guide, Mostafa El Hefny, for your cruise if he's available.

### Other Options

**Viking River Cruises** is the most well-known Western option and popular with North American travelers. It is considerably more expensive, starting at $10,000 USD, and has a highly structured itinerary. Our guide told us Viking is known for having excellent tour guides which is a big bonus. If budget is less of a concern and you'd prefer a more standardised experience, Viking is a solid choice.

## Alternatives to the Cruise

The Nile cruise is not the only way to travel between Luxor and Aswan. If the cruise doesn't fit your budget or itinerary, there are two straightforward alternatives. There are also tour options that will handle booking the logistics for you.

**Flight:** EgyptAir operates direct flights between Luxor (LXE) and Aswan (ASW), around 45 minutes in the air. It's the fastest option and not expensive by Western standards. EgyptAir offer 2 checked bags as standard in your ticket price (as of May 2026), which is pretty generous. 

**Private taxi/driver:** Hiring a private driver for the Luxor to Aswan run is a popular alternative that gives you the most control. The drive is roughly four to five hours along the Nile and the road is good. Drivers can stop at Edfu and Kom Ombo temples en route, covering the same ground as the cruise excursions at your own pace. Negotiate the route and price upfront. Your hotel in Luxor can arrange this, or your guide can recommend someone reliable. Expect to pay around $100 USD for the full transfer with temple stops, though prices vary.

**Train:** Egypt has a train between Luxor and Aswan and the journey takes around three to four hours. Foreign tourists are generally directed to purchase tickets through an official booking office or their hotel rather than at the counter, but it is straightforward. We haven't read the best reviews about taking the train but it is an option.

**Night Train:** You can take the train from Cairo to Aswan overnight, but the reviews are universally poor. Expect a hard bed, hot cabins, and a thoroughly rocky ride the whole journey.

## Main Cities

### Giza / Cairo

This is your likely entry point. Giza is effectively part of the Cairo metro area. Most people typically visit the Pyramids complex in the morning and the Grand Egyptian Museum in the afternoon. Cairo itself is a vast, chaotic, fascinating city. If you have extra days, the Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square and the historic Islamic Cairo quarter are worth exploring.

### Luxor

Luxor is the temple city. The east bank holds Karnak and Luxor Temples; the west bank holds the Valley of the Kings, Valley of the Queens, Queen Hatshepsut's temple, and the Colossi of Memnon. This is the densest concentration of ancient sites anywhere in the world, and two to three days is not too much. Most Nile cruises depart from and arrive at Luxor.

### Aswan

Aswan is quieter, cleaner, with a slower pace and stunning views of the Nile. Allow at least a day for the city itself. Philae Temple, the Unfinished Obelisk, and a felucca ride on the Nile are the main draws. From Aswan you can also make the trip to Abu Simbel, either as a very long day trip or (preferably) an overnight.

## When to Go

**Peak season (November - February)** is the most comfortable weather-wise and the most crowded. Christmas and New Year in particular see significant tourist numbers at Giza and Luxor.

**Shoulder season (March - April and October)** is the sweet spot for most travellers: manageable heat, thinner crowds, and full availability of cruises and guides. We went in the last week of April and although it was hot, there were significantly fewer crowds.

**Avoid May-September** unless 40°C heat is managable for you. Some tourist sites have limited hours during the peak summer months because they don't want tourists fainting from heat exhaustion.

## Day 1: Arrival in Giza

_Some hotel links below use affiliate links. We only recommend places we actually stayed._

Fly into Cairo International Airport (CAI). Purchase your visa on arrival ($30 USD cash, at the bank counter before immigration). Transfer to accommodation in Giza. Take today to relax, adjust to the time zone, and enjoy your first views of the city. If you're looking for some activities on your first day you could go on a food tour, get a tour of Old Cairo, explore Al Moez Street (one of the oldest streets in Cairo) or visit one of the many beautiful mosques, like Al-Azhar Mosque. If you want to see mummies, the National Museum of Egyptian Civilization is highly rated, although located between downtown and the pyramids.

The two most popular locations for accommodations in Cairo are:

1. Giza
2. Downtown Cairo

We stayed near the Giza plateau at the [Prestige Pyramids Inn](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=6776&awinaffid=2754110&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.booking.com%2Fhotel%2Feg%2Fprestige-pyramids-inn.en-gb.html), a family run hotel with great views of the pyramids. Our room was exactly like the photos and yet it still exceeded our expectations. There are plenty of restaurants near the hotel and the staff were super helpful with helping us organize our stay, offering room service and booking tours and taxis. There are also some luxury hotels and familiar western brands here, but the benefit is getting the pyramids view at sunset.

Alternatively you could stay in downtown Cairo which is closer to the Nile river and has more of a vibrant restaurant scene. Note that it's about a 30-45 minute drive to Giza depending on traffic, but that's not a deal breaker. Many people recommend hotels like the Ritz Carlton, Four Seasons and Marriott for a luxury experience or the Steigenberger and Novotel for a slightly cheaper, but still very comfortable experience.

**Tip:** We would recommend booking a hotel where breakfast is included. Breakfast in the western sense can be hard to find, so this can save you some stress in the morning.

## Day 2: Pyramids Complex and Grand Egyptian Museum

The recommended way to visit the two most popular sites in Giza, the Pyramids complex and the Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM), is to do one in the morning and one in the afternoon. The Pyramids open at 8am, and arriving right at opening time is a game changer: the crowds are much thinner and the light is better for photos. The difference between arriving at 8am and 9am is significant, both in crowd levels and in the heat. You can buy your tickets on arrival or through your tour operator in advance.

If going inside Khufu's pyramid is on your list: it requires a separate ticket from the main entrance, buy it when you arrive. It's hot, steep, and claustrophobic inside the ascending passage, but the chamber itself is one of the most singular experiences we've had anywhere. You can buy it at the entrance to the pyramid, but it's double the price.

In the afternoon, head to the **Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM)**, minutes from the pyramid plateau and a natural pairing. Budget 2-3 hours. The Tutankhamun collection is the highlight. There are several good restaurants on site, so you could go there directly from the pyramids, recharge over a good meal and then continue your journey into ancient Egypt.

**Ticket note:** Buy your GEM tickets online in advance and make sure to select "Other Nationality." [Ticket link](https://tickets.gem.eg/)

## Day 3: Fly to Luxor, Optional Dendera & Abydos Temples

Fly from Cairo to Luxor. This is a good day to keep flexible.

If you have energy: we would highly recommend visiting **Dendera Temple** and we are really disappointed we missed it. It sits between Cairo and Luxor and is widely considered the best-preserved temple in Egypt, with extraordinary surviving colour on its painted ceilings. Consider combining it with **Abydos Temple**. The two sites are roughly 60km apart and can be done in a single, long day with a private driver. Abydos is one of ancient Egypt's most sacred sites: the Temple of Seti I has some of the finest and best-preserved painted reliefs anywhere in the country. If you can only do one, Dendera would be our recommendation.

If you'd rather ease into Luxor: explore the city, visit the **Luxor Museum**, or walk the Avenue of Sphinxes and Luxor Temple in the evening when it's cooler and lit up. It closes at 8pm, and the lights get shut off around 9pm.

That night we stayed at the [Iberotel Luxor By JAZ](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=6776&awinaffid=2754110&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.booking.com%2Fhotel%2Feg%2Fiberotel-luxor.en-gb.html) hotel which we would highly recommend. It's a beautiful hotel right on the Nile river with a pool and great views. The location is excellent, it's close to the main sites and has 3 different restaurants to choose from, Italian, Chinese & buffet.

## Day 4: Start Nile Cruise, Karnak and Luxor Temples

Today the cruise officially starts. Our private guide picked us up from our hotel at 7am and took us to Karnak and Luxor Temple before boarding the cruise.

**Karnak** is a breathtaking site. This is not a single temple but an enormous walled city of temples and chapels built and expanded over 2,000 years. In sheltered spots between the columns, vivid original colour survives: reds, blues, and yellows that give you a sense of what these spaces looked like as living religious centres. Give it as much time as your itinerary allows.

**Luxor Temple** is a shorter visit and beautiful in the evening, when it's illuminated and cooler. The recently restored kilometers-long Avenue of Sphinxes running between the two complexes is one of the great ancient processional ways.

We finished up at the temples by around noon, and then boarded our cruise ship, had lunch onboard, and relaxed for the rest of the day. This is the common schedule for each day, 7am starts and returning to the boat by around noon. This is ideal because the mornings are significantly cooler than the afternoons, offering better conditions for sightseeing. We lounged in the shade on the top deck with a good book, and then watched our first sunset on the Nile. Dinner was next and was very good. We were told the quality of the food was the main differentiator between the cruise tiers. That night we slept in our beautiful air conditioned cabin onboard the cruise, although the boat stayed docked in Luxor.

## Day 5: West Bank of Luxor

The next morning we woke up early, grabbed a breakfast box from the cruise ship, and did a hot air balloon ride over the west bank at sunrise. The west bank is where the pharaohs were buried and taking it in from the sky is an unforgettable experience. This was one of the most spectacular moments of the trip, and we highly recommend it for an unforgettable addition to your trip ($250 USD for 2 people). Then we spent the rest of the day visiting the main sites on the west bank.

**Valley of the Kings** is the centrepiece. Your entry ticket covers three tombs of your choice out of the eight to twelve currently open. Your guide's recommendation here matters; tomb quality varies significantly. We entered KV11, KV8, and KV2 on our guide's suggestion, as they have the best-preserved colour. Tutankhamun (KV62), Seti I (KV17), and Ramses V/VI (KV9) require separate additional tickets.

**Tip:** The site opens at 8am, which is when all the cruise groups arrive. If your itinerary allows a late afternoon visit instead, you'll have near-empty tombs and better light.

**Temple of Hatshepsut** is next: three colonnaded terraces rising against a sheer limestone cliff, unlike anything else built in ancient Egypt. The interior is sparser than Karnak, but the architecture and the story of Hatshepsut herself (one of the few female pharaohs, later erased from history by her successor) make it essential.

**Colossi of Memnon** are a quick stop on the way back: two 18-metre seated statues that are all that remains of what was once the largest mortuary temple in Egypt. Free to visit if you're already on the west bank.

We returned to the boat at around noon, and then we set sail at 1pm for Esna lock & Edfu. We had a relaxing afternoon on the boat, enjoying the views of the Nile and the riverbanks as we sailed south. We had dinner onboard and then went to bed early to prepare for our next day of sightseeing in Edfu.

## Day 6: Edfu and Kom Ombo Temples

The next morning we were up at 7am to see Edfu Temple before continuing on to Kom Ombo Temple.

**Edfu Temple** is the most structurally complete ancient Egyptian temple still standing. The 36-metre entrance pylons are imposing, and the preserved interior gives a genuine sense of scale. It's also a record of Egypt's complicated later history: tourist graffiti from two centuries, bullet holes left by Napoleon's troops, and sections deliberately defaced by early Christians and later inhabitants.

**Kom Ombo** is more ruined but has a striking position on the Nile bank and an unusual story: it's the only ancient Egyptian temple dedicated equally to two gods simultaneously, with perfectly symmetrical twin sanctuaries. Our cruise arrived right before sunset, this was an amazing time to experience its beauty.

After Kom Ombo, we sailed onwards to Aswan, enjoying the changing scenery along the Nile. The riverbanks are lush and dotted with small villages, and the light at sunset is magical. We had dinner onboard and then went to bed.

## Day 7: Aswan

We woke up in Aswan and had breakfast on the boat before disembarking to explore the city and its surroundings. Aswan is a more relaxed city than Luxor, with a beautiful setting on the Nile and a distinct Nubian culture. We spent the day visiting the main sites in and around Aswan and soaking in the unique atmosphere of the city.

**Philae Temple** is the standout: an island temple complex reached by a short motorboat from the shore, with the water surrounding it on all sides. The whole complex was dismantled and relocated to higher ground in the 1970s before the Aswan High Dam flooded the original island. The engineering story rivals the temple's history.

**Unfinished Obelisk** is a short stop: a massive obelisk that cracked during carving and was abandoned in place. It's the largest known ancient obelisk and gives a very clear picture of how these structures were made.

**Felucca ride** on the Nile is the right way to end the afternoon: a traditional Egyptian sailboat, slow, quiet, and completely different in pace from the rest of the trip.

**Optional:** We opted for a visit to a **Nubian village** further down the Nile (ranges from $50-75 USD per person, includes transport). The Nubian people have their own distinct culture, language, architecture, and aesthetic; their villages are brightly painted and the warm welcome is genuine. This part of the river is exceptionally beautiful, with beautiful sand dunes rising directly from the water on the opposite bank. The village itself is a bit touristy but the private boat ride along the Nile was worth it alone.

## Day 8: End of Cruise, Optional Abu Simbel

The cruise ends at 8am in Aswan. What you do next depends on how much time you have.

**Abu Simbel** is 280km south and extraordinary: two temples carved directly into a sandstone cliff face, fronted by four colossal statues of Ramesses II over 20 metres tall. The relocation story (the entire facade was cut out and reassembled on higher ground in the 1960s to save it from the Aswan Dam) rivals the site itself. The remoteness keeps crowds low.

You have three options for getting there:

1. **Day trip from Aswan:** Departs at ~4:30am, four-hour drive each way, brief visit, back by evening. Very long day.
2. **Overnight by air:** About an hour, limited flight availability.
3. **Overnight by car (what we did):** Drive from Aswan at your leisure, spend the night, see the sound and light show in the evening (genuinely excellent, 40 minutes, beautifully produced), and tour the temples in the afternoon when the morning crowds are gone.

## Day 9: Abu Simbel back to Cairo

Drive back to Aswan in the morning, then fly from Aswan to Cairo. You'll likely need to stay a night in Cairo before your international flight the next day. We booked at Le Meridian at the airport for convenience, but there are also good options in downtown Cairo if you want to be closer to the city for your last night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What currency is used, and should I bring USD?

Egypt uses the Egyptian Pound (EGP). Most day-to-day spending is in EGP, but **you need $30 USD cash** for the visa-on-arrival at Cairo airport, cards and other currencies are not accepted. Many guides, tour operators, and cruise companies invoice in USD for larger payments, so carrying a modest amount of USD is worthwhile beyond just the visa fee. Our guide told us he would have preferred we tipped in USD because Egypt is currently experiencing a lot of inflation and the EGP is losing value.

### Can I use cash or card?

Cards are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and shops in Cairo, Luxor, and Aswan, especially those catering to tourists. However, smaller vendors, local markets, and guides prefer and only accept cash. It's a good idea to carry a mix of cash and cards. We withdrew cash 3 times during our trip, around $600 CAD total.

### How do I get cash in Egypt?

Get Egyptian Pounds out at the airport when you arrive. There are ATMs and exchange desks there. If you need more cash during your trip, **CIB (Commercial International Bank) ATMs** are the most reliable option for foreign cards and don't charge withdrawal fees. The ATMs will dispense a combination of 100 and 200 EGP bills, which are the most useful denominations for tipping and small purchases.

### How does tipping work?

Tipping is expected and taken seriously. Temple staff who will offer to take your photos, drivers who carry your bag, hotel staff who fetch something for you: all of these interactions carry an expectation of a small tip. Nothing in Egypt is free so always expect to tip for any service, no matter how small.

**Rough guide:**

- Restaurants: 10-15% if service isn't included (check the menu, tax and service are sometimes included, sometimes not)
- Tour guides: $10-20 USD per person per day, depending on quality. Your guide will make a big difference to your experience; tip well for a great one.
- Drivers: 50-100 EGP for shorter transfers, 200-300 EGP for longer ones
- Temple staff photos: 20-50 EGP per interaction

Tipping on the cruise is handled differently: there's typically a shared tipping pool for the crew at the end. Your cruise coordinator will advise on amounts, ours recommended $10-20 USD per person per day at the end of the cruise.

### What should I wear?

Lightweight, breathable, modest clothing. Egypt is hot, conservative, and very religious. Covering shoulders and knees when visiting temples and mosques is the standard expectation for both men and women. At major tourist sites like the Pyramids you will see tourists wearing shorts and tank tops, but it's best to err on the side of modesty to avoid unwanted attention and to show respect for local customs. A scarf or shawl is only needed at mosques.

### Can I drink the tap water?

No. Drink bottled water throughout. Staying hydrated in the heat is important; most cruise ships and hotels will provide bottled water freely.

### How to get around cities?

Uber is widely used in Cairo and works well. Some drivers will try to negotiate a higher price through messaging on the app which you have to pay by cash. Our recommendation would be to select Uber Comfort, because from our experience the driver is paid better and won't try and negotiate extra money. In Luxor and Aswan, your hotel can arrange drivers and taxis for you, they are significantly smaller cities.

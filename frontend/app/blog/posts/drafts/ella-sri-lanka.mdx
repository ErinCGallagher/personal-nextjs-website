Located in the heart of Sri Lanka's central highlands, Ella is a must-see on any trip to the island. Small enough to walk across in twenty minutes, it punches well above its weight. The surrounding landscape of rolling tea estates, dramatic ridgelines, and jungle-clad cliffs is some of the most beautiful in the country. I visited for the first time in 2017 and was glad to return and find it had only grown more popular for good reason.

The train from Kandy to Ella is one of the most beautiful railway journeys in the world. For six hours you roll through lush green forests, past hillside terraces of tea plantations, and through the stunning central highlands landscape. It is a bucket list journey in its own right, the carriages fill with travellers hanging out of the open doors for that perfect photograph, everyone grinning at each other as the scenery rolls past.

From Ella, three hikes are worth your time: **Ella Rock**, **the Nine Arches Bridge**, and **Little Adam's Peak**. We did all three over two days, and the combination gives you a complete picture of what makes this place special.

## Ella Rock

The most demanding and most rewarding of the three, Ella Rock sits at 1,041m above sea level and looks out over the valley and the town below. The views from the summit are one of the highlights of a trip to Sri Lanka.

### Getting There

There are two ways to reach the trailhead, both starting from Ella town.

**Option 1: Walk the Railway Tracks:** A flat 2km walk along the railway line to Kitha-Ella station, then a few hundred metres past the station to the trailhead. It takes around 30 minutes. Walking along an active railway feels a little unnerving, but the trains here are very slow and very loud — as long as you don't have headphones in, you'll hear one coming from a long way off.

**Option 2: Tuk-Tuk:** We hailed one outside our hotel at 9:15am and were at the trailhead fifteen minutes later. The fare was 1,500 rupees (~$7 CAD). This is the better option in the morning heat.

In the end, we did both tuk-tuk to the trailhead, then walked the tracks back into town afterwards. That combination works well.

### The Hike

| | |
|---|---|
| **Distance** | 5km return |
| **Elevation Gain** | 300m |
| **Time** | 2–2.5 hours |
| **Level** | Moderate |

From the trailhead, the path passes immediately through tranquil farmland before climbing through tea terraces. There are two cafes catering to hikers along the way, selling cold drinks and snacks before the steeper upper section.

The trail becomes less obvious as you gain elevation. The local farmers frequently alter their paths and access roads, so what you see on a map may not match what you find on the ground. We used a combination of AllTrails and Maps.me, which, when combined, had the most accurate routing, but even with it there were sections where we stopped and looked at each other. **Download a map, don't rely solely on phone signal.**

At 1.5km you reach the first viewpoint, looking out over the valley below. Across the way you can make out hikers on the summit of Little Adam's Peak, a useful landmark to confirm you're on the right track. A small stall here sells water and refreshments if you need to top up before the final push.

The forest cover above the viewpoint offers welcome shade. The ground is fine, dusty soil, which is manageable going up, but slippery on the descent. Take care on the way down.

When you see a wooden hut with a green metal roof, you're close. This is the entrance office where you pay the summit fee of **980 rupees per person** (~$4.50 CAD). Just past it, the forest opens onto the rocky summit viewpoint.

The view is extraordinary. The valley spreads out below you in every direction, and on a clear morning the visibility is excellent. Take your time up here — you've earned it.

**Tip:** Start as early as you can. We were on the trail by 9:30am in early September and it was already 25°C in the shade. Many hikers start at sunrise to beat the heat entirely. But if you're not an early morning person, the mountain will still be there after 9am, it will just be a very sweaty climb.

### Walking Back Along the Railway

The 2km walk along the railway line back into Ella is flat but tiring — stepping from sleeper to sleeper is awkward on tired legs, and there is no shade from the midday sun. It took us about 30 minutes. There is something deeply satisfying about looking back and seeing the summit you just came off, though.

**Total: 7.1km, 313m elevation, 2 hours 23 minutes**

## Nine Arches Bridge & Little Adam's Peak

These two are best combined into a single outing. The bridge is the starting point, Little Adam's Peak is the destination, and the route between them takes you through tea plantations and farmland with hardly another tourist in sight.

### Nine Arches Bridge

Built in 1919 entirely by local labourers, the Nine Arches Bridge is one of the most photographed spots in Sri Lanka. It has a prominent curve and a 9% incline, steep for a railway line, and the stone arches frame the jungle on either side beautifully.

We took a tuk-tuk from our hotel to the bridge, which took five minutes and cost 800 rupees (~$3.70 CAD). Every driver can take you there, but you will need to negotiate a little to get the right price. The path down from the drop-off point to the bridge is very steep, and on a public holiday, we were picking our way down past a constant stream of locals making the climb back up.

Walk across the bridge, then climb the steps on the far side to the small cafe overlooking it. We ordered drinks and didn't have to wait long before a train came puttering out of the tunnel and crossed the bridge. It is a very satisfying thing to watch from above.

**Tip:** Trains cross the bridge several times a day. Check the timetable in town or ask your hotel, timing your visit to see a train cross is well worth the minor planning effort.

### Little Adam's Peak

Named after its much larger and more famous namesake (the sacred peak in Ratnapura District), Little Adam's Peak sits at 1,141m and offers excellent panoramic views of the surrounding valley.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Distance** | 3km one way from the bridge (6km return, or loop back a different way) |
| **Elevation Gain** | ~210m from the bridge |
| **Time** | 1 hour 40 minutes (combined with Nine Arches) |
| **Level** | Easy to Moderate |

From the bridge, we took a quieter back route rather than the main path, it was far less crowded and pleasantly shaded, winding through tea plantations and farmland. The sounds of the bridge faded quickly and the tranquility was only broken by the bell of an ice cream vendor's tuk-tuk threading through the estate roads.

After about 30 minutes we reached the road and followed it for 500 metres. At the 98 Acres Resort, the trail becomes well-signposted and things get noticeably busier. Many tuk-tuks were coming and going, ferrying visitors up and down. In recent years, a zipline, treetop walk, and climbing wall have all been added for those wanting more than the hike alone.

The late afternoon sun was casting long shadows as we reached the summit, and the haze of agricultural smoke in the air turned the light a deep golden colour. The view is wide and clear with hills rolling out in every direction, the valley town of Ella below, the railway line threading through it all.

## Practical Tips

### Getting Around Ella
Tuk-tuks are plentiful and cheap. Most drivers will approach you before you need to approach them. Agree on a price before you set off. For reference: town to Ella Rock trailhead was 1,500 rupees, town to Nine Arches Bridge was 800 rupees.

### When to Visit
Ella's highland altitude makes it cooler than the coast, but the sun is still intense. The dry season (December to March) gives the best visibility from the summits. We visited in September — manageable but very dusty on the trails.

### Trail Navigation
AllTrails has the most accurate maps for Ella Rock, but the trail is still not always obvious. Download the map offline before you go and don't depend on mobile signal. For Little Adam's Peak, signage is good once you're past the 98 Acres Resort.

### What to Bring
- More water than you think you need: there is one resupply point on Ella Rock at the 1.5km viewpoint, and nothing between the Nine Arches Bridge and Little Adam's Peak summit
- Shoes with grip: the dusty soil on Ella Rock is very slippery, particularly on the descent
- Cash: the summit entrance fee (980 rupees), trail cafes, and tuk-tuks are cash only

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the must-do hikes in Ella?

The three essential hikes are Ella Rock, the Nine Arches Bridge, and Little Adam's Peak. Ella Rock is the most challenging and most rewarding: 5km return with 300m elevation gain and panoramic valley views from the summit. The Nine Arches Bridge and Little Adam's Peak are best combined into a single outing — a half-day that covers both with minimal overlap.

### How long should I spend in Ella?

Two nights is the minimum to do all three hikes without rushing. Ella Rock takes a full morning; the Nine Arches Bridge plus Little Adam's Peak fills a second morning or afternoon. If you want a rest day built in, three nights is comfortable.

### Is the Ella Rock hike difficult?

Ella Rock is rated moderate. The 5km return trail gains 300m of elevation and the upper sections are steep. The trail is not always well-marked — download AllTrails offline before you go. The biggest challenge is the heat: start by 9am at the latest, earlier if you can manage it.

### When is the best time to visit Ella?

December to March is the dry season and gives the best visibility from the summits. We visited in September — manageable, but the Ella Rock trail was very dusty. The Kandy–Ella train is worth booking several weeks in advance regardless of season.

### How much do the Ella hikes cost?

Ella Rock has a summit entrance fee of 980 LKR per person (~$4.50 CAD). The Nine Arches Bridge and Little Adam's Peak are free to hike. The main costs are tuk-tuks: 1,500 LKR (~$7 CAD) to the Ella Rock trailhead and 800 LKR (~$3.70 CAD) to the Nine Arches Bridge.

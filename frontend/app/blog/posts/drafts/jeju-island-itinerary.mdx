Jeju Island sits about 80 kilometres off the southern tip of the Korean Peninsula, and it holds a remarkable distinction: it is the only place in the world to have earned UNESCO's triple crown of World Natural Heritage Site, Global Geopark, and World Biosphere Reserve simultaneously. The island is essentially a shield volcano rising out of the Korea Strait, and the evidence of that origin is everywhere — in the hexagonal basalt cliffs, the lava tube caves, the black-sand beaches, and the lone peak of Hallasan looming over the entire island.

Four days gives you enough time to cover the highlights across Jeju's two main bases: Jeju City in the north and Seogwipo in the south. The two cities have quite different personalities. Jeju City is the busier, more urban island capital; Seogwipo is smaller and quieter, hemmed in by waterfalls on one side and dramatic sea cliffs on the other.

## Day 1: Arrival & Jeju City

Jeju International Airport is compact and easy to navigate. Rental car desks are a short shuttle ride from the terminal.

Start with **Dongmun Traditional Market**, the most beloved market on the island and a perfect introduction to Jeju's food culture. Covered stalls sell hallabong (a sweet local tangerine hybrid), black sesame tteok (rice cakes), raw haenyeo seafood platters, and Jeju black pork. It gets lively on weekends; go first thing in the morning or after 5pm for a more relaxed browse.

From the market, drive or walk ten minutes to **Yonduam (Dragon Head Rock)** — a dramatic basalt formation on the seafront that locals say resembles a dragon rearing out of the ocean. The short promenade around it offers your first clear view of Hallasan rising in the centre of the island, and serves as an early reminder that essentially everything you see on Jeju has volcanic origins.

Before dinner, stop at **Samseonghyeol Shrine**, a short walk from Dongmun. It's one of the island's oldest historic sites — a grove surrounding three holes in the earth from which, according to Jeju's founding myth, three demigods emerged to become the ancestors of the island's people. The surrounding woodland is peaceful, and a small museum explains the creation legend. It's an understated site, but worth seeing.

End the day with **black pork BBQ**. Jeju's native black pigs produce fattier, more flavourful meat than standard Korean pork, and grilling it yourself over charcoal at one of the many restaurants near Dongmun Market is a proper Jeju ritual.

### Hotels (Jeju City)

- **Lotte City Hotel Jeju** — Upscale, convenient location near the airport and city centre, reliable amenities
- **Hotel Nan** — Mid-range boutique, comfortable rooms, quieter neighbourhood
- **Jeju Sun Hotel** — Mid-range, central location, good base for exploring on foot

## Day 2: Lava Tubes, Folk Village & Sunrise Peak

Set off early — this is a full day along the island's eastern half.

### Manjanggul Lava Tube

About 40 minutes east of Jeju City, **Manjanggul** is one of the longest lava tube systems in the world, stretching over 13 kilometres through the basalt. The section open to visitors is just over one kilometre, but it's enough to feel genuinely otherworldly — a cathedral-like tunnel with a perfectly arched ceiling, frozen lava waterfalls, and a 7.6-metre lava column at the far end, one of the tallest in the world.

> **Check before you go:** Manjanggul occasionally closes for maintenance or conservation work. Verify current opening hours on the Jeju tourism website before building your day around it. If it's closed, **Hallim Park** on the west coast has two lava tube caves accessible as part of its botanical garden complex.

### Seongeup Folk Village

On the road south towards Seongsan, stop at **Seongeup Folk Village** — a living village that has been continuously inhabited for over 600 years. Thatched roofs, low basalt stone walls, and winding lanes make it one of the best-preserved examples of traditional Jeju architecture anywhere on the island. Unlike reconstructed folk villages, people actually live here. Local grandmothers serve as informal guides, demonstrating traditional crafts and food preparation.

The low stone field walls (*batdam*) that give the Jeju countryside its distinctive patchwork appearance originated here — built over centuries to protect crops from the fierce island winds. The village gives important context to the rest of your time on Jeju.

### Seongsan Ilchulbong (Sunrise Peak)

The centrepiece of any Jeju itinerary, **Seongsan Ilchulbong** is a 182-metre tuff cone that erupted from the ocean floor around 5,000 years ago. Its vast grassy crater sits atop a 20-minute climb, and the views over the surrounding coastline are spectacular.

As the name suggests, it's famous for sunrise — if you're prepared to set a 5am alarm, watching the sun clear the horizon over the open sea and light up the crater walls is an extraordinary experience. The view during the day is equally impressive without the logistics.

At the base of the peak, a small fishing village clings to the rocks, and there are usually haenyeo performances mid-morning — the island's traditional female free-divers harvesting shellfish and sea urchin from the water.

> **Sunrise option:** The drive from Jeju City takes about an hour and is easily done before dawn. Alternatively, stay the night in Seongsan — there are several small guesthouses and pension-style hotels right at the base of the peak.

### Hamdeok Beach

On the drive back to Jeju City, stop at **Hamdeok Beach**. It's one of the most beautiful beaches on the island: the water is an extraordinary shade of turquoise, shallow and protected, with fine white sand and a small rocky headland at one end. There are cafes on the beachfront, and it's worth staying longer than planned.

Check out of your Jeju City hotel on Day 3 morning and drive south across the island — about an hour on the expressway.

## Day 3: West Coast, Waterfalls & the Market

### Hyeopjae Beach (En Route)

If you take the western route south, make a detour to **Hyeopjae Beach** — arguably the most photogenic beach on Jeju. The water is an astonishing mint-green against white sand, with the small offshore island of Biyangdo sitting in the background as a natural frame. It's about 45 minutes from Jeju City and an hour from Seogwipo. Make time for it.

### Jusangjeolli Cliffs

A short drive west of Seogwipo city centre, **Jusangjeolli** is a stretch of coast where thousands of hexagonal and pentagonal basalt columns stack like a giant's staircase from the clifftop down into the sea. The columns formed as volcanic lava cooled rapidly against the ocean, and the geometric precision of the result is genuinely startling. A short walkway above the cliffs provides excellent viewpoints. This is one of the best examples of volcanic coastal scenery on the island.

### Jeongbang Waterfall

**Jeongbang Waterfall** is the only waterfall in Asia said to fall directly into the ocean, and it lives up to its billing. A short walk from the Seogwipo waterfront brings you to a steep staircase down to the base, where you can stand on the rocks in the spray with open sea stretching out behind the cascade. There is a small entrance fee. Visit in the morning before the tour groups arrive.

### Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market

**Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market** (literally "every day market") is the social centre of the city and one of the best markets in Korea. Fresh fish, local produce, haemul pajeon (seafood pancake), hallabong juice, and an enormous range of Jeju specialities fill the covered stalls. It's at its liveliest in the morning, but open throughout the day. Basing yourself in central Seogwipo means the market is a short walk from your hotel — the single biggest advantage of staying in the city centre over the resort complexes further west.

### Lee Jungseop Street

Running along the cliff edge near the waterfront, **Lee Jungseop Street** is named after one of Korea's most beloved twentieth-century painters, who lived in Seogwipo for a short but productive period during the Korean War. The street is lined with murals and small galleries, and the Lee Jungseop Art Museum houses a permanent collection of his work — bold, vivid, and immediately recognisable. Worth an hour of your afternoon.

### Hotels (Seogwipo)

- **Jeju Shinhwa World Marriott** — The standout luxury option on the island. The Shinhwa World complex in western Jeju houses multiple Marriott-brand hotels at different price points — the Marriott, an Autograph Collection property, and the W Hotel — alongside a water park, casino, golf course, spa, and a large entertainment complex. It's a full resort destination in its own right; you could spend the entire stay without leaving the grounds. Marriott Bonvoy members will find strong value here.
- **Seogwipo KAL Hotel** — Mid-range, well located in central Seogwipo, walking distance to the market, Jeongbang Waterfall, and the waterfront
- **The Suites Hotel Jeju** — Mid-range boutique, comfortable rooms near the market area

## Day 4: Coastal Walks, Beaches & Departure

### Cheonjiyeon Waterfall

A second major waterfall just minutes from the market, **Cheonjiyeon** ("Sky Connected Lake") drops into a deep circular pool surrounded by subtropical vegetation. The pathway is lit at night if you're doing an evening visit before an early-morning departure. Small entry fee applies.

### Jungmun Saekdal Beach

Seogwipo's most dramatic beach, **Jungmun Saekdal** is a wide bay of mixed dark and light sand framed by black lava cliffs. The surf here is noticeably stronger than Jeju City's beaches — strong enough to attract surfers — and the surrounding Jungmun Resort area has cafes and restaurants for a relaxed afternoon. This is one of the better beaches on the island for simply sitting and watching the water.

### Jeju Olle Trail

The **Olle Trail Network** — 26 coastal walking routes that circumnavigate the entire island — is at its most dramatic around Seogwipo. Trail No. 6 runs along the southern cliffs past volcanic rock formations, tidal pools, and open sea views that stretch to the horizon. Even walking an hour of any section near Seogwipo gives a strong sense of what makes Jeju's coastline unique. Download the official Jeju Olle app for GPS maps of all 26 routes.

## Optional: Hallasan National Park

**Hallasan** at 1,950 metres is South Korea's highest peak and the dormant volcano at the island's core. Climbing to the summit via the Seongpanak or Gwaneumsa trails is a full-day commitment — plan for 8–10 hours including descent — and the summit trail enforces a strict entry cutoff in the afternoon to ensure all hikers descend safely.

If summiting Hallasan is a priority, it deserves its own dedicated day and ideally its own overnight near the trailhead. It cannot sensibly be combined with other sightseeing. That said, the mountain is visible from almost everywhere on the island, and the lower Eorimok and Yeongsil trails offer spectacular sub-alpine scenery — silver grass meadows, ancient fir forests, and volcanic rock formations — without the full summit commitment.

## Optional Extras: Museums & Attractions

Jeju has a bewildering number of museums and themed attractions, ranging from genuinely excellent to magnificently peculiar. A curated list:

### Worth Your Time

- **Hallim Park** — Subtropical botanical garden with two lava tube caves (Hyeopjae Cave and Ssangyong Cave) running beneath it; excellent if Manjanggul is closed, and pleasant in its own right
- **Aquaplanet Jeju** — One of Asia's largest aquariums, near Seongsan; well-executed, good for a half day
- **Jeju National Museum** — The best starting point for understanding the island's 4,000 years of history, from dolmen culture through the Joseon dynasty; free entry
- **Spirited Garden** — A private bonsai garden with specimen trees over 500 years old. Genuinely beautiful, genuinely peaceful, and easy to spend a morning in
- **9.81 Park** — Gravity-powered kart racing on a track cut through the hillside near Jeju City. Sounds gimmicky, turns out to be exhilarating

### Peculiar & Proudly So

- **Jeju Loveland** — An outdoor sculpture park composed entirely of explicit adult artwork. It sounds absurd, and in some ways it is, but it's executed with genuine craft and has become one of Jeju's most-discussed attractions. Not for the easily offended, but entirely unlike anything else you'll encounter at a tourist destination
- **Africa Museum** — A large, well-resourced museum dedicated to African wildlife, tribal culture, and natural history. On a volcanic island in the Korea Strait. No one seems entirely sure why it exists at this scale, but the exhibits are genuinely impressive and the taxidermy collection is extraordinary
- **Teddy Bear Museum** — An earnest, well-maintained collection of teddy bears dressed as historical figures and posed in famous scenes. The gift shop is remarkable
- **Trick Eye Museum** — Optical illusion installations designed for interactive photos; enormously popular with Korean domestic visitors
- **Jeju Glass Castle** — A large glass-and-hedge maze complex near Seongsan; good fun, particularly if you're travelling with children
- **Mysterious Road** — A short stretch of road near Jeju City where vehicles appear to roll uphill. A topographic optical illusion rather than genuine supernatural geography, but the locals lean into it enthusiastically

## Best Time to Visit

| Season | Months | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| **Spring** | March–May | Cherry blossoms and yellow canola fields; the most photogenic time of year |
| **Summer** | June–August | Hot and humid; beaches at their busiest; typhoon risk in August |
| **Autumn** | September–November | Clear skies, comfortable temperatures, fewer crowds — arguably the best overall season |
| **Winter** | December–February | Cold and sometimes snowy at altitude; quiet and cheaper; Hallasan in snow is magnificent |

## Getting There

Jeju is connected by air to Seoul (both Gimpo and Incheon airports), Busan, Daegu, and several other Korean cities. Flights are frequent and inexpensive when booked ahead — under ₩100,000 (approximately $100 CAD) is achievable with some flexibility. The flight from Seoul takes just under an hour.

Ferry services run from Mokpo and Busan for those who prefer the sea crossing, though the journey takes several hours.

## Practical Tips

**Currency:** Korean Won (₩). Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but carry cash for markets and small restaurants.

**Language:** English signage is common at major tourist sites. Elsewhere, the camera function in Google Translate handles menus and signs reliably.

**eSIM:** Both Roamless (pay-as-you-go, $2/GB in South Korea) and Eskimo (global data bundles) offer strong Korean coverage. See our other travel guides for referral links.

**Tipping:** Not customary in South Korea. You may occasionally feel the impulse — resist it.

**Convenience Stores:** Korean convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) are genuinely excellent. Good coffee, hot food, cheap beer, and ATMs. Do not underestimate them.

## Final Thoughts

Four days is enough to see Jeju's highlights without feeling rushed, provided you have a car. The island rewards slow travel — pull over when you spot something interesting, follow a coastal path further than intended, linger at the market longer than necessary. The volcanic landscape reveals itself gradually, and the more closely you look, the more there is to find.

Northern Vietnam is a world apart from the south. Where Ho Chi Minh City is all neon and noise, the north offers something more peaceful: ancient dynasties, dramatic karst landscapes, misty mountain villages, and a cooler, slower temperament. This 9-day itinerary covers the essentials of the north: a few days in Hanoi to absorb the capital, a night on the water in Ha Long Bay, a day exploring the limestone-ringed ancient capital of Ninh Binh, and two days trekking through the rice terraces of Sapa.

This north Vietnam itinerary flows from the capital outward — no doubling back, no unnecessary rushing. Whether you're doing this as a standalone trip or tacking it onto the [full 2-week south Vietnam route](vietnam-itinerary-blog), it holds up well on its own.

## Day 1: Arrival & Acclimatisation

Vietnam's capital is a lot to take in. Ancient temples rub up against French colonial mansions, and every street seems to function simultaneously as a roundabout, a market, and a pavement cafe. Give yourself the first day to ease in.

If you're coming from North America, expect to wake up before dawn for the first few mornings — lean into it. Walk down to Hoan Kiem Lake before 7am and you'll find the city at its most charming: locals out in force for morning exercise, groups doing aerobics and badminton, retirees dancing in formation. Most breakfast spots don't open until 7 or 8am, so a coffee-first start is the move.

For breakfast, we liked [Bancông Cafe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yodb4LvQxkJsHxin9), which has a balcony looking out over the street below and serves both local dishes and western brunch.

In the afternoon, head to [Train Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KyTycYYPqcZnwyP87). A working railway line threads through a residential alleyway with barely a metre of clearance on either side. Residents calmly clear their plastic chairs and pot plants when they hear the horn, then reclaim the tracks the moment the train passes. If you've never seen it before, it seems so at odds with the western sense of Health and Safety that it's genuinely thrilling.

After that, take a massage — they're stellar value in Hanoi, expect to pay $25–40 CAD for an hour — and wander the Old Quarter's chaotic fabric and luggage streets if you're in the market for designer goods of uncertain provenance.

For dinner, [CO](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YUMsZiLuhUiizg5G6) is a reliable choice: good local fare with plenty of vegetarian options.

### Hotels

- **Carillon Boutique Hotel** — Mid-range, excellent location in the Old Quarter
- **Hanoi Paon Hotel and Spa** — Mid to upscale, French Quarter location, spa services useful for jet lag recovery

## Day 2: History & Hidden Alleys

Start the day at [Movitas](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dCj44LwZjWaCmVKbA) for coffee and pastries, then make your way to the Ho Chi Minh Museum. The mausoleum next door — where you can see the preserved body of the man himself — keeps odd opening hours, so check ahead if it's a priority. Both require covered shoulders and knees. The museum traces his life with a distinct nation-building slant, but it's worth your time.

In the afternoon, join a free walking tour (tip-supported, starting at the Ngoc Son Temple on Hoan Kiem Lake). Ours took us through a temple, a food market, narrow residential alleyways, a large textile market, and finished at the Long Biên Bridge — a century-old railway bridge designed by Gustave Eiffel, still in daily use. The final stop, right next to the bridge, was a bar selling ice-cold draft beer for around 12,000 VND (about 50 cents Canadian).

If egg coffee is on your list, **Café Giang** — a cramped, wonderful dive tucked up a narrow staircase near Hoan Kiem Lake — is where it was invented. The house specialty is whipped egg yolk and condensed milk poured over strong Vietnamese coffee. It sounds strange; it is excellent.

### Restaurant Recommendations

- [Bancông Cafe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yodb4LvQxkJsHxin9) — Breakfast/Brunch
- [CO](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YUMsZiLuhUiizg5G6) — Lunch/Dinner
- **Bun Cha Huong Lien** — The bun cha restaurant where Anthony Bourdain and Barack Obama famously ate in 2016. Expect a queue. Worth it.
- **Café Giang** — Egg coffee, non-negotiable

## Day 3: Morning in Hanoi, Afternoon on the Water

Depending on how you booked, your cruise operator may pick you from your hotel, but if you booked independently, you'll be collected from a central meeting point around 8–9am. We were collected from in front of the Opera House, which is a useful landmark for many cross-country bus transfers.

The transfer to Ha Long Bay takes approximately 3.5–4 hours. Most operators use minibuses; some transfer passengers through a small local taxi first before switching to the main bus. This happened to us and was alarming until it resolved itself. Expect a degree of organised chaos and don't worry if things don't look like what you booked at first, but this is a common theme in Vietnam.

[We have a complete guide to planning your Ha Long Bay cruise here.](ha-long-bay)


You'll arrive at the ferry terminal around 11am–noon. After boarding your tender and being ferried out to the main vessel, lunch is typically served as the boat makes its way into the bay.

The moment you pass the first limestone karsts, you understand why this place is famous. Hundreds of near-vertical rock formations draped in jungle, rising straight from calm water. It is a bucket-list experience and it earns it.

Afternoons on most 1-night cruises include kayaking (often included), a visit to one of the cave systems, and a swimming stop. The caves are genuinely spectacular — enormous, lit-up caverns that feel otherworldly. Sunset from the top deck, cold beer in hand, the karsts going golden in the light, was one of the best moments of the whole trip.

## Day 4: Sunrise & Return

Get up before 6am and you'll have the top deck nearly to yourself, watching the fog lift off the water between the limestone formations. Some cruises offer a sunrise tai chi class on the roof, which sounds gimmicky and turns out to be a genuinely enjoyable way to start the day as the boat sails back through the karsts.

Breakfast on board, then it's back on the tender by around 9am for the return to the ferry terminal, the bus, and the drive back to Hanoi, arriving around 3–4pm.

Rather than spending another night in the capital, use this afternoon to transfer directly to Ninh Binh. It adds a couple of hours to the day but saves you a full travel day tomorrow.


## Day 4 (continued): Travel to Ninh Binh

Buses from Hanoi's Mỹ Đình terminal to Ninh Binh run frequently and take around 2 hours. There is also a train from Hanoi Ga that stops at Ninh Binh station if you prefer. By the time you arrive it'll be early evening — get settled, eat something, and sleep. Tomorrow is a big day.

**Dinner recommendation:** **Thuy Anh Restaurant** near the Trang An entrance is popular and reliable for local food after a long travel day.

### Hotels

- **Ninh Binh Hidden Charm Hotel & Resort** — Upscale, beautiful pool, great views over the karst landscape. Worth the splurge after a road-heavy day.
- **Tam Coc Garden Resort** — Mid-range, peaceful setting, bicycles available for hire from the hotel

## Day 5: Boats, Pagodas & Panoramas

Ninh Binh is often called the "inland Ha Long Bay" and while that undersells both destinations, you can see why the comparison gets made: limestone karsts rising from rice paddies, temples cut directly into cliff faces, and rivers threading between the rock.

The must-do here is **Trang An** — a UNESCO-listed network of waterways that takes you by rowing boat through caves and past ancient temples. Your boatwoman rows with her feet (this is common here and is quite something to watch), the boat gliding silently through low cave ceilings before emerging into the next lagoon. The full circuit takes about 3 hours.

Nearby, **Bich Dong Pagoda** is built directly into a cliff face, accessed by a steep flight of steps. It's a short detour worth taking.

**Hoa Lu Ancient Citadel** — the former capital of Vietnam in the 10th and 11th centuries — is a short ride away. Two ornate temple complexes remain and give some historical grounding to the landscape around you.

For the most dramatic viewpoint in the area, hire a bicycle from your hotel and ride out to **Hang Mua Peak**. The climb is a steep but manageable 500 steps, and from the top you get the iconic view: Tam Coc's rice paddies stretching out below, studded with limestone outcrops in every direction. Allow 2–3 hours for the round trip. Do not skip this.

### Additional Activities

- **Tam Coc Boat Tour** — Similar to Trang An, slightly more commercialised but still scenic; often bundled into local tour packages
- **Cuc Phuong National Park** — For wildlife enthusiasts, this is the nearest national park (45 min away), home to langurs and other primates

### Restaurant Recommendations

- **Thuy Anh Restaurant** — Good arrival-evening option, reliable local dishes
- **Yen Restaurant** — Local Vietnamese food in the Tam Coc area, popular with visitors and locals alike


## Day 6: Night Train to Sapa

Spend your morning tying up anything you didn't get to in Ninh Binh, then head back to Hanoi by bus or car in the afternoon. The overnight train to Lao Cai (the nearest city to Sapa) departs from Hanoi Ga around 9–10pm and arrives at Lao Cai around 6am — 8–9 hours through the mountains. 

Alternatively, there are sleeper buses that leave very frequently from Hanoi

Unlike the trains further south, the sleeper on this route is genuinely comfortable if you book a private cabin. We'd recommend **Livitrans Express** — a private operator that attaches their own carriages to the state train — over the standard state-issue bunks. You get a lockable 4-berth (or 2-berth) cabin with proper beds, air conditioning, and an attendant who brings water. Book in advance: these sell out fast, especially on weekends.

**Note:** You'll need to return to Hanoi from Ninh Binh to catch the train. This takes around 2 hours by bus. Plan your afternoon accordingly and don't leave it too late.

## Day 7: Arrive in Sapa, First Trek

The train pulls into Lao Cai around 6am. Minibuses run up to Sapa town from there — a 38km drive that takes about an hour through increasingly dramatic mountain scenery. At 1,500m elevation, Sapa is noticeably cooler than Hanoi. Bring a layer.

We'd strongly recommend booking a guided trek for at least one of your two days. The ethnic minority communities — H'mong, Red Dao, Tay — who live in the valleys below Sapa are the cultural centrepiece of the area, and a local guide (ideally from one of these communities themselves) will get you access to villages and context you simply won't find walking alone.

Two agencies worth looking into: **Sapa Sisters** and **Ethos Spirit** both employ local H'mong and Red Dao women as guides and have excellent reputations.

A classic Day 7 trek runs through **Muong Hoa Valley** to the villages of **Lao Chai** and **Ta Van** — a 10km route through the terraced rice fields that define Sapa's landscape. These are at their most spectacular in May–June (bright green paddies) or September–October (golden before harvest), but the scenery is extraordinary at any time of year.

### Hotels

- **Topas Ecolodge** — Upscale, a 15-minute drive outside town, bungalows perched on a ridge with panoramic views of the valley. One of the most memorable places to stay in the whole country.
- **Sapa Clay House** — Mid-range, well located in Sapa town, good restaurant on site

## Day 8: Second Day in Sapa

With another full day, you have a few options depending on energy levels.

For those who want a peak: **Fansipan** is the highest mountain in Indochina at 3,143m. It's accessible by a 2-day trek or, much more easily, by cable car from Sapa town. The cable car is one of the longest single-rope cable cars in the world and is spectacular in its own right. Views from the summit on a clear day are extraordinary. It is frequently cloudy, so pick your day carefully and check the forecast before committing.

For those who'd rather stay lower: **Cat Cat Village** is an H'mong village about 3km from Sapa town, accessible by a pleasant downhill walk (stairs back up, or a motorbike taxi if your legs are done). The village has been somewhat commercialised but retains plenty of charm, and there's a small waterfall at the bottom worth seeing.

In the evening, head to **The Hill Station Signature** for one of the better meals of the trip. They serve local cuisine with a contemporary approach — fondue, duck confit using regional ingredients — in a beautifully restored French colonial space. Book ahead.

### Additional Activities

- **Love Waterfall (Thác Tình Yêu)** — A 30-minute walk through forest from Sapa; popular but genuinely scenic
- **Silver Waterfall & Heaven's Gate** — En route between Lao Cai and Sapa; worth stopping if your minibus driver is flexible
- **O Quy Ho Pass** — One of the most scenic mountain passes in Vietnam; best approached by motorbike or as part of a day trip

### Restaurant Recommendations

- **The Hill Station Signature** — Upscale, contemporary takes on local cuisine; book ahead
- **Baguette & Chocolat** — A social enterprise training local young people in hospitality. Excellent pastries and Vietnamese cafe food, great value.

## General Travel Tips

### Cash vs. Card

Card acceptance is reasonable in Hanoi. The further north you go, the more cash-only things become. In Ninh Binh and Sapa, many smaller guesthouses, market stalls, and trekking operators are cash only.

The best free-to-use ATM in Vietnam is **VP Bank** — look for a branch in Hanoi and withdraw what you need before heading out of the city.

### Night Train Booking

Book the Hanoi–Lao Cai overnight train as far in advance as possible. Private cabins on Livitrans and Victoria Express sell out quickly on weekends and during Vietnamese public holidays. Tết in particular renders the entire rail network fully booked weeks in advance. Book directly through the operators or via your hotel.

### Weather in Sapa

Sapa's weather is notoriously unpredictable. Even in what should be clear season, the mountain can be completely socked in with cloud for days at a time. Pack a waterproof regardless of what month you visit, and don't build your Fansipan summit around a tight timeline.

The coldest months (December–February) bring genuine frost and occasionally snow. This is one of the most atmospheric times to visit if you're prepared for it, but pack as you would for cold European weather.

### Scooters in the North

Ninh Binh is one of the more scooter-friendly areas for foreigners — many hostels rent them out and the police presence is low. It's a great way to explore the area at your own pace. Hanoi, however, is not recommended for scooter rental: the traffic is genuinely chaotic and police enforcement is strict.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is 9 days enough for north Vietnam?**
Nine days covers the essential north Vietnam itinerary — Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Ninh Binh, and Sapa — at a reasonable pace with one rest day built in. If you have more time, both Ninh Binh and Sapa reward extra days; the route stretches comfortably to 12–14 days without feeling padded.

**How do I get from Hanoi to Sapa?**
The best option is the overnight train from Hanoi Ga station to Lao Cai (8–9 hours), followed by a 38km minibus transfer up to Sapa town (about an hour). Book a private cabin on the Livitrans or Victoria Express carriages rather than the standard state-issued bunks — the price difference is modest and the comfort difference is significant. Trains depart around 9–10pm and arrive in Lao Cai around 6am. Book well in advance; private cabins sell out quickly on weekends.

**What is the best time to visit north Vietnam?**
October to April for most of the route. Sapa is particularly beautiful in September–October (rice paddies turn golden before harvest) or May–June (vivid green after planting). December to February brings genuine frost and occasional snow to Sapa — atmospheric, but pack for cold weather.

**Is Ninh Binh worth visiting?**
Absolutely — it's one of the most underrated stops in Vietnam. The Trang An boat tour, Hang Mua Peak viewpoint, and Hoa Lu ancient citadel together make a genuinely outstanding day, comparable in impact to Ha Long Bay but with a fraction of the crowds. Most visitors give it one day; if your schedule allows two, take them.

**Do you need a guide for trekking in Sapa?**
For trekking, yes — a local guide makes a significant difference. The trails are manageable independently, but a guide from one of the H'mong or Red Dao communities provides access to villages, cultural context, and routes that no app carries. Sapa Sisters and Ethos Spirit are two well-regarded agencies employing local minority women as guides.

## Final Thoughts

This north Vietnam loop packs a lot in without feeling rushed. Hanoi anchors the start and gives you a city base that rewards slow exploration; Ha Long Bay delivers the drama; Ninh Binh provides the quiet, off-the-beaten-path counterpoint; and Sapa rounds everything out with altitude, rice terraces, and genuine cultural depth.

If you have a few extra days, Ninh Binh rewards a second full day of exploration, and Sapa opens up significantly the further from town you trek. Both places benefit from more time if you have it to give.

**Happy travels!**

Most people do Vietnam north to south. The guidebooks default to it, the flights are often cheaper that way, and there's a certain logic to starting in the more manageable capital before heading into the humid chaos of the south. But going the other direction has real advantages, and more travellers are discovering them.

Starting in Ho Chi Minh City is a great introduction to the current state of Vietnam: a rapidly developing nation on the rise. The city is loud, fast, and inexhaustible in a way that suits fresh legs. As the trip progresses and you move north, the country slows with you. Hoi An's ancient streets, Da Nang's sea breeze, Ninh Binh's silent river valleys, until you reach Hanoi, which is best appreciated when you've already seen what came before. The capital makes more sense, and feels more rewarding, when it's the destination rather than the starting block.

This 14-day Vietnam itinerary covers the best of the country without venturing into the central highlands. It's leaner than the [full 2-week north-to-south route](vietnam-itinerary-blog), but adds Ninh Binh, an often overlooked stop, and arrives in Hanoi with enough time to actually enjoy it.

Vietnam's largest city, formerly known as Saigon, is a whirlwind of energy, culture, history, and incredible food. It's the right place to start: throw yourself into the deep end, get your bearings in the chaos, and calibrate your expectations for everything that follows.

## Day 1: Arrival in Ho Chi Minh City

The airport is close to the city, a Grab should cost around $6–10 CAD and take 20–30 minutes depending on traffic (which is always significant).

After checking in, the best introduction to the city is [Bui Vien Walking Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wb8xma6p8HhavB2h9) after dark. It is crammed with bars blasting music at volumes you have likely never experienced, and will wake you back up after a long flight. DJs playing electronic music, live bands covering western classics, the whole street functioning as one enormous open-air party. I strongly recommend earplugs, even if you don't plan on going inside anywhere, the street itself is the spectacle. We found a bar with a bearable decibel level and a very good cover band playing Fleetwood Mac and called it a night.

For dinner before venturing out, go for Pho at [Pho Viet Nam](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VDkf6SXyiUxynAAQA). The Michelin stars on the wall tell you everything you need to know.

## Day 2: Cu Chi Tunnels

The Cu Chi Tunnels are a day trip well worth making. We booked through [Get Your Guide](https://gyg.me/Pvr6bDhW), though these tours are commodified across platforms — just pick one with good reviews. The drive is about two hours each way in a comfortable minibus collected from the hotel.

The exhibits are extraordinary: captured American military vehicles, a C130 transport aircraft, tanks and helicopters on open display in the jungle clearing. The guide walks you through how this vast underground network was built and used, with firsthand looks at the camouflage techniques, hidden trapdoors, and booby traps that made the area so formidable. The highlight is crawling through a section of the actual tunnels, low, dark, and very cramped, which gives you an immediate appreciation for the conditions.

In the evening, [Clay](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pj472VJbvyF2vNM69) is worth the Grab: upscale food and cocktails with a good view over the river.

## Day 3: City Exploration

Start the day at [The Hungry Pig](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9uG1EfZxdTXMXtWa6) for brunch, an excellent full English breakfast in a city not short on international food options.

From there, make your way to [Ben Thanh Market](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1h9vUEooxSixRrqg6) for clothes and souvenirs, then [The New Underground](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ssAA5VcAWAnaWtyh7) nearby for more considered craft shopping. The city is hot year-round, so stop at [Cong Ca Phe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/UPMwv2NMUTwhMVm47) for a coconut iced coffee in the air conditioning. This cafe chain is uniquely themed, a kind of retro socialist aesthetic, and embodies the Vietnamese take on the coffee culture they inherited and adapted from the French.

Walk northwest from there to [Notre Dame Cathedral of Saigon](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ySUcaYFNLyNATvDTA), a legacy of French colonial rule, which left roughly 10% of the population Catholic. Directly opposite is the [Central Post Office of Saigon](https://maps.app.goo.gl/EoiZZdoC67B9wYAv6), worth visiting for its grand scale and ornate finishings. Vendors sell creative postcards and you can write and mail them on the spot. Finish the afternoon with a stroll down [Book Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/c5nPwzBXWYqwVQoY6).

For your last dinner in the south, check out [Happy Lamb Hotpot](https://maps.app.goo.gl/4a3VcmmCc72X5DdD6) is an authentic Chinese hotpot experience and a sociable way to start a trip.

## Day 4: Travel Day & First Evening in Hoi An

There are frequent flights between Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang — book an early departure to maximise the day. Da Nang airport is close to the city, and a Grab to Hoi An from there takes around 30–40 minutes and costs about $10–15 CAD.

In the heat of the day, the old town streets are very quiet — most visitors and locals alike retreat indoors. Check in, cool off, and head out in the late afternoon when the ancient city comes alive.

Your first priority: get to a tailor. We went to [Tuong Tailor](https://maps.app.goo.gl/szo6CS1CDDJb9nxd6) in Hoi An, which was highly recommended online and didn't disappoint. You'll be guided through fabrics and styles, measured, and given a collection time 1–2 days later. A custom suit and shirt ran us about $300 CAD; a custom dress around $100 CAD. A rush service is available for an extra fee if your timeline is tight.

In the evening, walk the maze of old streets filled with restaurants, boutiques, and galleries. The waterfront comes alive after dark — the river is filled with lantern-bearing boats which you can charter for a relaxed trip up and down. Consider a show at the [Lune Center for Performing Arts](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Lq3fDfqDCvfuDX2YA), which puts on nightly acrobatic performances. Book ahead: we ended up in the back row due to late booking and the domed shape of the building made for a less comfortable hour than we'd hoped.

## Day 5: Old Town & Night Markets

A day of slow exploration. Visit the tailor for a test fitting in the morning, then spend the afternoon wandering. Hoi An rewards aimlessness — every alleyway has something worth discovering.

In the evening, sunset cocktails on the rooftop at [92 Station](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mWPFhD9MrQitPfz29), followed by [Hoi An Memories Land](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KyF5HBUjh6NdcQMK7) — a theatrical light and dance show based on a local folk tale. If you can only pick one performance, the Lune acrobatics show is the better of the two, but this is still worth seeing.

### Additional Activities

- **Lantern Making Class** — Make your own traditional silk lantern
- **An Bang Beach** — Quieter than Da Nang's My Khe, good for a half day
- **Assembly Hall of Cantonese Chinese** — Beautiful example of traditional Chinese architecture

### Hotels

- **Renaissance Hoi An** — Upscale beachfront resort, excellent amenities; 15 minutes from downtown
- **Rokas Hoi An** — Mid-range boutique hotel in the heart of downtown, comfy beds, family-run

### Restaurant Recommendations

- [**Mot**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/tv3W7fyvT2tsZ67u9) — Get the iced tea. There is usually a line; go at lunch to avoid the worst of it. Local dishes are excellent too.
- [**The Son Bistro & Craft Beer**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8NWQsZrjS9oxAHgY9) — Good local food and a fine selection of local craft beers
- [**Hien Sushi Bar**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/vvo5VGVLCEbuvkaY8) — A short walk off the main drag; delicious sushi at reasonable prices



## Day 6: Transfer to Da Nang

Move to Da Nang today, it's around 45 minutes by Grab from Hoi An. Da Nang's famous beach stretches for kilometres along the eastern edge of the city. Grab a lounger with sunshade for around $5 for the whole day, we found one that included a free coconut drink and a 30-minute foot massage. There are also boat rides, jet ski rentals, and parasailing along the strip. Check your travel insurance before going up.

The sea breeze keeps things tolerable even in hot months, Da Nang is noticeably more pleasant than Hoi An or Ho Chi Minh City despite similar temperatures.

In the evening, head to [Ben Cuon Da Nang](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Um7T6u4ygYZU4TWR6) — on the Michelin guide, get the Banh Xeo (crispy pancakes). They were some of the best things we ate in the whole country.

## Day 7: Beach Day
If you decided to get clothes made in Hoi An, you'll probably need to head back down there to collect them in the morning.

In the afternoon, once you're back in Da Nang, rent a scooter if you're comfortable on one. Henry Motorbike Rental (WhatsApp +84 37 755 5365) will deliver to your hotel for no extra charge and charges around $5 a day — card accepted. Da Nang is one of the more scooter-friendly cities in the country; no police hassle, manageable traffic, and the city is laid out well for two-wheel exploration.

Head north to the **Lady Buddha** — a 67-metre tall white statue looking out over the bay from the Son Tra Peninsula. It's a 20-minute ride from downtown with a small entry fee. The bonsai gardens around the base are peaceful, and the view back over the city is excellent.

## Day 8: Ba Na Hills, Cooking Class & Dragon Bridge

If you want to spend another day at the beach enjoy it! Otherwise, take a Grab to Ba Na Hills, a theme park about an hour's drive west of the city.

Later, this [cooking class](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HyFSuwZApY7TF1e58) is one of the best ways to spend an evening in Da Nang. Ours started with a visit to a local food market to shop for ingredients, then moved to the kitchen to make pho, bun cha, spring rolls, and fresh rolls. 

On Fridays and Saturdays, the Dragon Bridge puts on a show at 9pm: the dragon head at the eastern end breathes fire and water high into the air. It's a surreal, genuinely spectacular 15 minutes, and there's a night market nearby for snacks and late shopping.

## Day 9: Travel Day — Da Nang to Ninh Binh

Fly Da Nang to Hanoi (flights are frequent, about an hour in the air, $35–80 CAD). From Hanoi's Noi Bai airport, take a Grab or pre-booked minibus to Ninh Binh — about 2 hours south. Buses also run from Hanoi's Mỹ Đình terminal regularly.

You'll arrive in the early evening. Get settled and eat something good: **Thuy Anh Restaurant** near the Trang An entrance is reliable and a short walk from most of the area's accommodation.

### Hotels

- **Ninh Binh Hidden Charm Hotel & Resort** — Upscale, beautiful pool, views over the karst landscape; a good splurge after a travel-heavy day
- **Tam Coc Garden Resort** — Mid-range, peaceful setting, bicycles for hire from the hotel

## Day 10: Boats, Pagodas & Panoramas

Often called the "inland Ha Long Bay", both destinations are undersold by the comparison, but you can see why it gets made. Limestone karsts rise out of rice paddies, ancient temples are cut directly into cliff faces, and rivers thread silently between the rock.

**Trang An** is the centrepiece: a UNESCO-listed network of waterways, navigated by rowing boat, through low cave ceilings and open lagoons. Your boatwoman rows with her feet, a technique that is quite something to watch, and the whole circuit takes around 3 hours. This is not to be rushed.

**Bich Dong Pagoda**, built into a cliff face, is a short detour worth making. **Hoa Lu Ancient Citadel** — the former capital of Vietnam in the 10th and 11th centuries — gives the landscape some historical grounding.

For the definitive viewpoint, hire a bicycle from your hotel and ride to **Hang Mua Peak**. The climb is a steep 500 steps, and from the top you get the iconic panorama: Tam Coc's rice paddies below, studded with limestone outcrops in every direction. Allow 2–3 hours for the round trip.

## Day 11: Board the Cruise

Many Ha Long Bay operators offer pickups from the Ninh Binh area, check with your cruise operator in advance, as this saves a detour back through Hanoi. If your operator only collects from the capital, a bus or taxi from Ninh Binh to Hanoi takes around 2 hours, leaving you in good time for an 8am pickup.

The transfer to the ferry terminal takes 3.5–4 hours. The moment the boat clears the port and the first karsts rise up from the water around you, the journey instantly feels worthwhile.

Afternoons typically include kayaking, a cave visit, and a swimming stop. The caves are enormous and lit-up and feel genuinely otherworldly. Sunset from the top deck, cold beer in hand, is one of those travel moments you don't forget.

## Day 12: Sunrise & Return to Hanoi

Get up before 6am for the top deck, watching the fog lifting between the karsts at dawn is extraordinary. Some cruises offer a sunrise tai chi class on the roof, which sounds like a gimmick and turns out to be a lovely way to start the morning.

Back on the tender by around 9am, then the bus to Hanoi, arriving mid-afternoon. This gives you the rest of the day to check in and begin exploring the capital.

## Day 12 (continued): Arrive in Hanoi

Vietnam's capital rewards slower exploration than a frantic half-day allows, but the afternoon gives you enough time to get oriented. Drop your bags and walk down to Hoan Kiem Lake before dinner, the old town surrounding it is the most atmospheric part of the city.

For dinner, [CO](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YUMsZiLuhUiizg5G6) is a reliable first night choice: good local fare, plenty of vegetarian options.

## Day 13: History & Old Quarter

Start early at [Bancông Cafe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yodb4LvQxkJsHxin9), a balcony cafe overlooking the street, serving local dishes and western brunch. Then head to the Ho Chi Minh Museum. Check ahead if the mausoleum, where you can see the man himself lying in state, is open; it keeps odd hours and imposes a strict dress code: covered shoulders and knees.

In the afternoon, join a free walking tour (tip-supported, starting at Ngoc Son Temple on Hoan Kiem Lake). Ours covered a temple, a food market, narrow residential alleyways, a textile market, and ended at the Long Biên Bridge, designed by Gustave Eiffel, over a century old, and still in daily use. The final stop was a bar right next to the bridge serving ice-cold draft beer for about 12,000 VND.

Don't leave Hanoi without trying egg coffee at **Café Giang**, a narrow staircase dive near Hoan Kiem where it was invented. Whipped egg yolk and condensed milk poured over strong Vietnamese coffee. It sounds strange; order two. They are ubiquitous across the country, so if you are pushed for time, almost any cafe will serve you one.

## Day 14: Train Street & Final Day

Walk down to [Train Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KyTycYYPqcZnwyP87), a working railway line that threads through a residential alleyway with barely a metre of clearance on either side. Locals clear their chairs and plant pots when they hear the horn, then reclaim the tracks the moment it passes.

Spend the rest of the day at whatever pace suits you. The Old Quarter's streets dedicated to garments and luggage are worth a wander if you're in the market for designer goods of uncertain provenance. Massages in Hanoi are excellent value,$25–40 CAD for an hour at any of the countless parlours in the old town.

For a final dinner, **Bun Cha Huong Lien**, where Anthony Bourdain and Barack Obama ate together in 2016, is as good a way to end the trip as any.

### Hotels

- **Hanoi Paon Hotel and Spa** — Mid to upscale, French Quarter, spa services available

## General Travel Tips

### Cash vs. Card

During our time in Vietnam, roughly 80% of our spend was on card. That said, cash is essential: markets, street vendors, and smaller guesthouses in Ninh Binh and Sapa won't take card.

The best free-to-use ATM is **VP Bank**: there's often a queue of foreigners at them because every other ATM charges significant fees. Find a branch before leaving Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City.

Our recommendation: use your mobile wallet (Apple Pay or equivalent) wherever possible. Your card can't be cloned that way. It happened to us in Bali, and since switching to contactless wallets we haven't had another incident.

### Street Food

Both times I ate meat from a street stall, I got food poisoning, chicken banh mi, pork bao bun. Stick to uncut fruits, coconuts, and coffee from street vendors and you'll likely be fine. Otherwise exercise caution.

### eSIMs

Two eSIMs that have been reliable across Southeast Asia:
- **Roamless** — Pay as you go, rates vary by country ($2/GB in Vietnam), credit never expires, 100+ countries. [Get $5 free here](https://app.goroamless.com/OOq0/2pe6g5md)
- **Eskimo** — Global data packs that never expire ($90/30GB). [Get 500MB free here](https://eskimo.travel/en/KA64836) to try it out.

Note: both links are referral links — I only recommend services I actually use.

### Grab

Grab is your best friend for much of Southeast Asia. Vietnam is no exception, rides are very cheap, drivers are easy to find, and it removes all need to negotiate fares. Use it everywhere.

### Flying vs. Trains

Domestic flights are the right choice for most long legs on this itinerary. The Hanoi–Da Nang train takes 18 hours and costs more than a flight. A flight takes an hour and costs $35–100 CAD. Unless you specifically want the train experience, fly.

### Scooters

Foreigners can't legally obtain a Vietnamese driving licence, so renting a scooter is technically illegal everywhere. That said, enforcement varies dramatically by city:

**Generally fine:**
- Da Nang — Low police presence; we rented twice from Henry Motorbike Rental (WhatsApp +84 37 755 5365) without any issues
- Ninh Binh — Low police presence; many hostels rent to guests or you can find vendors near the train station
- Hoi An — Low police presence; note that much of the old town is pedestrian-only

**Not recommended:**
- Ho Chi Minh City — Significant police presence, chaotic traffic
- Hanoi — Highest police presence in the country, not worth the risk

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should I travel Vietnam north to south or south to north?**
Both work well, and the choice often comes down to flight prices and personal preference. North to south is the conventional approach — it's how most guidebooks are structured and flights into Hanoi are sometimes cheaper from Europe and North America. South to north, as this itinerary does, means arriving into the energy of Ho Chi Minh City with fresh legs and finishing in Hanoi — a more layered city that rewards being a destination rather than a starting block.

**What are the advantages of starting in Ho Chi Minh City?**
You hit the loudest, most intense city when you have the most energy for it. The route then naturally slows as you move north — Hoi An's ancient pace, Da Nang's sea breeze, Ninh Binh's silent river valleys — until you reach Hanoi, which makes more sense when you've already seen what came before. It's a more satisfying arc for many travellers.

**Is 2 weeks enough for a Vietnam trip?**
Yes — two weeks covers Vietnam's highlights without feeling rushed, provided you fly for the long legs rather than taking buses. This 14-day itinerary covers Ho Chi Minh City, Hoi An, Da Nang, Ninh Binh, Ha Long Bay, and Hanoi. With three weeks you can add Da Lat, Mui Ne, or slow down at the stops you love most.

**What is the cheapest way to get between cities in Vietnam?**
Domestic flights are the best value for long distances — a Ho Chi Minh City–Da Nang or Da Nang–Hanoi flight costs $35–100 CAD and takes an hour versus many hours by bus. For shorter legs, sleeper buses are cheap and widely available. Grab handles in-city transport efficiently and affordably everywhere in the country.

## Final Thoughts

The south-to-north direction gives this itinerary a natural arc: begin in the energetic, modern south, slow down through the ancient charm of Hoi An, relax into Da Nang's beach days, and then let the north deepen things — Ninh Binh's quiet river valleys, Ha Long Bay's drama, and finally Hanoi, which rewards every hour you give it.

Vietnam is an extraordinarily welcoming country, and two weeks is enough to get a genuine feel for it — without ever feeling like you've exhausted it.

**Happy travels!**

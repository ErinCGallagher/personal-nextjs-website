Sri Lanka is one of the best value destinations in Asia. For a country with ancient UNESCO temples, some of the world's best surf breaks, dense national parks, a bucket-list train journey, and a coastline that goes on for hundreds of kilometres, the price of getting around and staying comfortable is remarkably low. Two weeks and a tight budget gets you a lot here.

This itinerary covers the highlights of the south and east — Kandy's temples, the famous scenic train to Ella, surfing and safari at Arugam Bay, whale watching at Mirissa, and the colonial streets of Galle — connected almost entirely by bus and train. No taxis unless necessary, no private drivers, no shortcuts that blow the budget.

## Trip Overview

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Duration** | 16 days |
| **Route** | Colombo → Kandy → Nuwara Eliya → Ella → Arugam Bay → Mirissa → Galle → Colombo |
| **Best Time to Visit** | Nov–Apr for south/west coast; Jun–Sep for east coast (Arugam Bay) |
| **Getting Around** | Trains, intercity buses, tuk-tuks |

**A note on timing:** The southwest coast (Mirissa, Galle) and the east coast (Arugam Bay) have opposite best seasons. The sweet spot for this itinerary is **October–November** or **March–April** — transitional months where both coasts are usually reasonable. Arugam Bay's surf season peaks June–September; Mirissa's whale watching peaks December–April. If you're committed to both, accept that one leg will be slightly off-season.

## Day 1: Arrival

Most international flights arrive at Bandaranaike International Airport in Negombo, about 30km north of Colombo. There's no need to spend a night in the city — head straight to Kandy on arrival day. The bus from Colombo's Bastian Mawatha terminal to Kandy takes about 3 hours and costs around 250 rupees (~$1.20 CAD). The train from Colombo Fort station is roughly the same time and cost, and a good deal more comfortable.

If your flight arrives late, a night near the airport in Negombo is the easy option before heading inland the next morning.

---

# Kandy (3 Nights)

Sri Lanka's second city sits in a bowl of green hills at 500m elevation, and the cooler air is immediately noticeable after the coast. The city centres on an artificial lake, and beyond the inevitable tourist crowds around the Temple of the Tooth, it has genuine character.

## Day 2: Arrive Kandy & the Temple

Check in, get your bearings, and head to the **Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic** (Sri Dalada Maligawa) in the late afternoon. This is the most sacred Buddhist site in Sri Lanka — the temple reportedly holds a tooth of the Buddha — and the evening puja ceremony at around 6:30pm is an atmospheric experience: drumming, chanting, and large crowds of pilgrims filling the courtyard. Dress conservatively (shoulders and knees covered) and remove your shoes before entering. Entrance fee is approximately 1,500 rupees (~$7 CAD) for foreigners.

Walk the lakeside promenade in the early evening, and eat dinner at one of the local rice-and-curry spots near the market — budget $3–5 CAD for a full plate.

## Day 3: Day Trip to Sigiriya

Sigiriya Rock Fortress is one of the most extraordinary archaeological sites in Asia: an ancient royal citadel perched on a 200-metre volcanic plug rising straight out of the jungle. The frescoes on the rock face, the lion paw gateway, and the terraced gardens at the base are all extraordinarily preserved. It is a genuine bucket-list experience.

The journey from Kandy takes about 2.5 hours each way. The most budget-friendly route is a bus to Dambulla (150 rupees, ~90 min) and then a local bus on to Sigiriya (100 rupees, ~45 min), with a tuk-tuk for the final stretch if needed.

**Entrance fee: $30 USD (~$40 CAD) per person.** This is expensive by Sri Lanka standards. It is worth it.

**Budget alternative — Pidurangala Rock:** Directly adjacent to Sigiriya, Pidurangala is a 45-minute hike with an entrance fee of around 500 rupees (~$2.50 CAD). The summit looks directly back at Sigiriya Rock rising from the jungle — many people consider it the better photograph. It is not the same experience as being on Sigiriya itself, but if the entrance fee is a dealbreaker, Pidurangala is a genuinely rewarding alternative.

The **Dambulla Cave Temples** are on the same road — five cave temples carved into a rock face, decorated with hundreds of Buddha statues and paintings. They make a natural stop on the way back to Kandy. Entrance is 1,500 rupees (~$7 CAD).

**⭐ Tip:** Bring plenty of water for Sigiriya. The climb is exposed and can be hot, and the queues for the summit can be long in peak season. Start as early as possible.

## Day 4: Kandy at Your Own Pace

**Royal Botanical Gardens at Peradeniya**, 6km outside Kandy, are worth a morning — 60 hectares of meticulously kept grounds including an orchid collection, a bamboo grove, and a giant java fig tree covering nearly half an acre. Entrance is 1,500 rupees (~$7 CAD).

In the afternoon, wander the Kandy Market for local colour and cheap food, and catch a **Kandyan Cultural Show** in the evening — traditional drumming, dancing, and fire-walking. These are openly touristy but well-done and give useful context for the culture you'll see throughout the trip. Tickets are around 1,000 rupees ($5 CAD).

### Accommodation

Budget hostels in Kandy range from $10–20 CAD for a dorm bed. The area around the lake has several well-located guesthouses in the $25–40 CAD range for a private room.

### Transport to Next Stop

The **train from Kandy to Ella** is one of the most beautiful railway journeys in the world. See below.

# Optional Stop: Nuwara Eliya (1 Night)

*This adds one night to the itinerary but is worth it if your schedule allows.*

Nuwara Eliya is a highland town at 1,868m — Sri Lanka's highest city — built by the British as a colonial hill station in the 19th century. The town has a peculiar, slightly faded Englishness to it: Tudor-style bungalows, a racetrack, a golf course, strawberry farms. Surrounding it are some of the most extensive tea estates in the country.

Get off the Kandy–Ella train at **Nanu Oya station** and take a tuk-tuk up to town (about 15 minutes, 400 rupees). Spend the afternoon walking through the tea estates, visiting a tea factory, and watching the sun set over a landscape that looks like the Scottish Highlands if they happened to have tea growing on them.

Budget accommodation in Nuwara Eliya is limited but there are a few solid guesthouses. Board the train back at Nanu Oya the next morning and continue to Ella.

# The Train to Ella

## Day 5: The Train to Ella

Six hours through the central highlands on one of the world's most beautiful railway journeys. You roll through lush forests, past tea estate terraces, over high viaducts, and through the dramatic central highlands landscape. The carriages fill with travellers hanging out of the open doors for photographs. Everyone is grinning.

Book as far in advance as possible. 2nd class reserved seats can be booked through the **Sri Lanka Railways website** and cost around $4–6 CAD per person — worth every cent over the unreserved 3rd class crush. If you can't get a reservation, join the queue early at Kandy station.

**⭐ Tip:** Sit on the right side of the train (as you face the direction of travel toward Ella) for the best views.

# Ella (2 Nights)

A small hill town with big scenery and a well-developed backpacker scene. Hostels are cheap, cafes are good, and the three main hikes give you plenty to do without ever needing to spend much.

## Day 6: Ella Rock

The biggest hike in the area and the one with the most rewarding views. The trail is 5km return, gains 300m of elevation, takes around 2–2.5 hours, and has an entrance fee of 980 rupees (~$4.50 CAD) at the summit hut.

Get started early — we were hiking at 9:30am in September and it was already 25°C in the shade. The trail is not always obvious; AllTrails has the most accurate map but even that leaves some sections ambiguous. Download the route offline before you go.

A tuk-tuk from Ella town to the trailhead costs around 1,500 rupees (~$7 CAD). Walk back along the railway tracks for free — 2km, 30 minutes, flat but exposed.

## Day 7: Nine Arches Bridge & Little Adam's Peak

These two are best combined into one afternoon outing. A tuk-tuk to the Nine Arches Bridge costs 800 rupees (~$3.70 CAD). The bridge is a beautiful colonial-era stone viaduct in the jungle, built in 1919, and best seen with a train crossing it — check the timetable and time your visit accordingly.

From the bridge, the trail to Little Adam's Peak (1,141m) is 3km one way and gains around 200m. The route through tea plantations to the quieter back approach is worth taking over the busy main path. The views from the summit are excellent.

[We have a full guide to all three hikes here.](ella-sri-lanka)

### Accommodation

Ella has a good spread of budget hostels and guesthouses. Dorm beds from $12–20 CAD, private rooms from $25–45 CAD.

### Transport to Next Stop

The journey from Ella to Arugam Bay takes 6–7 hours by bus and involves at least one transfer in Wellawaya. There is no direct service. The total fare is around $3–5 CAD. Leave early.

---

# Arugam Bay (4 Nights)

Arugam Bay — "A-Bay" to regulars — is a small fishing village on the southeast coast that has become one of the best surf destinations in Asia. The main point break is world-class, the vibe is relaxed, the food is cheap, and the surrounding area offers wildlife that rivals anywhere in the country.

## Day 8: Arrive & Settle In

The bus journey from Ella is long and hot. Arrive, check in, eat something at the beach, and sleep.

## Days 9–10: Surf Lessons & the Beach

The **Arugam Bay point break** is best for experienced surfers, but several smaller breaks along the bay are ideal for beginners. Surf schools operate along the beach offering lessons from around $20–35 CAD for a 1.5–2 hour session, including board hire. Most will also rent boards independently if you already know what you're doing.

Surf season peaks June–September. Outside those months the surf is typically smaller and more inconsistent, but still rideable and far less crowded.

The bay itself is long, sandy, and lined with low-key beach bars, restaurants, and hammocks. If you haven't reached beach-doing-nothing saturation yet, this is the place to hit it.

## Day 11: Kumana National Park Safari

**Kumana National Park** (also known as Yala East) sits immediately south of Arugam Bay and is significantly less visited than Yala to the west, while offering similar wildlife. Elephants are frequently sighted, particularly at the **Kumana Tank** — a large reservoir that attracts herds at dusk. Leopards are present but less reliably seen than at Yala. The park is exceptional for birds, with the lagoon hosting large breeding colonies.

Safari jeeps can be arranged through your guesthouse or directly at the park entrance. A half-day safari costs around $40–70 CAD including the jeep and guide. Add the park entrance fee on top.

**⭐ Alternative:** If Kumana safaris aren't running during your visit (the park closes briefly for certain seasons), **Pottuvil Lagoon** boat tours offer excellent bird and crocodile sightings at much lower cost — around $10–15 CAD.

### Accommodation

Arugam Bay has a strong hostel scene with dorm beds from $10–18 CAD and beach bungalows from $25–50 CAD. Book ahead in peak surf season (July–September) as it fills up fast.

### Transport to Next Stop

From Arugam Bay to Mirissa, you're crossing the island from east to south — a journey of 7–9 hours depending on the route and connections. Go via Wellawaya and Matara. Budget $5–8 CAD for the total bus fare and leave very early.

---

# Mirissa (3 Nights)

Mirissa is Sri Lanka's premier backpacker beach town on the south coast — a wide horseshoe bay, a coconut palm-lined beach, and an easy-going atmosphere that makes it very easy to stay longer than planned. It also happens to be one of the best places in the world to see blue whales.

## Day 12: Arrive & The Beach

Arrive, find a hammock, apply sunscreen, and do very little. Mirissa earns this.

## Day 13: Whale Watching

From November through April, blue whale and sperm whale sightings are highly reliable off the Mirissa coast. Half-day tours depart from Mirissa harbour at around 6am and return by noon. Blue whales are the largest animals on earth, and seeing one surface 50 metres from a small boat is an experience that is difficult to overstate.

Tours cost around $30–50 CAD per person, including the boat transfer and guide. Book the night before through your guesthouse or one of the operators along the beach road.

**⭐ Tip:** Take seasickness medication if you're susceptible — the waters can be choppy in the open ocean, and the boats are not large.

## Day 14: Explore the Headland & Parrot Rock

Mirissa has a small rocky headland at the western end of the bay that you can walk around at low tide. Beyond it is **Parrot Rock**, a small islet accessible via a rickety rope bridge that has become something of a Mirissa landmark. Beyond that, the coast continues west toward the quieter beaches of **Coconut Hill** and the fishing harbour.

Rent a scooter for the day (around $10–15 CAD) and ride west along the coastal road to **Weligama** — a slightly larger town with a good surf break if you want another session.

### Accommodation

Mirissa has excellent budget options. Dorm beds from $10–18 CAD, beach bungalows from $25–50 CAD. The beach road has the highest concentration of places to stay.

### Transport to Next Stop

Mirissa to Galle is barely 45 minutes by bus (50 rupees, ~$0.25 CAD) or train from nearby **Weligama or Matara station**. One of the cheapest legs of the trip.

---

# Galle (2 Nights)

Galle Fort is one of the most atmospheric places in Sri Lanka — a 17th-century Dutch colonial fortification enclosing a living neighbourhood of restaurants, boutiques, galleries, and hotels within unchanged colonial-era streets. It's also small enough to walk end to end in 20 minutes, which means it rewards slow exploration rather than a checklist approach.

## Day 15: The Fort

Walk the **fort walls** — the full circuit takes about an hour, and sunset from the lighthouse at the southern tip is exceptional. Inside the walls, the streets are lined with restored Dutch colonial buildings that now house everything from tailors and jewellers to outstanding cafes and restaurants.

The **Galle National Museum** inside the fort covers the Dutch colonial history and is worth an hour. Entrance is modest.

Head to **Unawatuna Beach** (3km east by tuk-tuk, 500 rupees) in the afternoon — a sheltered bay with good swimming and a backpacker scene at the beach bars.

## Day 16: Day Trip or Linger

If you're beach-saturation resistant, **Hikkaduwa** is 20km north of Galle by bus (about 40 minutes, very cheap) — a longer stretch of beach with a more party-oriented vibe, snorkelling on the coral reef offshore, and a strong backpacker infrastructure. Worth a day trip if you're in the mood.

Otherwise, Galle Fort rewards a second day of slow wandering. The morning fish market at the harbour outside the fort walls is worth seeing early.

From Galle, you're 2.5 hours from Colombo by train or bus for your return flight.

### Accommodation

There are budget guesthouses in the streets just outside the fort walls from $20–40 CAD for a private room. Staying inside the fort itself is more expensive. Hostels with dorm beds can be found in the town outside the fort from $12–20 CAD.

## General Tips

### Buses
Sri Lanka's intercity bus network is extensive and cheap. For most routes, just show up at the relevant bus station or major junction, ask which bus goes where, and pay the conductor on board. Fares are rarely more than $2–5 CAD even for several hours of travel. Air-conditioned "luxury" coaches cost a little more but are well worth it for journeys over 3 hours.

### Trains
Book ahead for the Kandy–Ella journey — reserved 2nd class seats can be booked online through Sri Lanka Railways and sell out. For other rail legs (Mirissa → Galle, Galle → Colombo), you can usually just turn up and get on.

### Tuk-Tuks
Non-negotiable in Sri Lanka. Agree on a price before getting in, or use **PickMe** (the local ride-hailing app) for metered fares in larger towns. Short journeys within town should cost 200–500 rupees ($1–2.50 CAD); longer trips proportionally more.

### Accommodation Booking
**Arugam Bay in surf season (July–September)** books out weeks in advance. Everything else on this itinerary can generally be booked a day or two ahead, though the Ella guesthouses also fill up quickly in peak season (Dec–March).

### Street Food & Eating Cheap
Rice and curry is the staple and it's excellent — a full plate with several curries costs $2–4 CAD at local spots. **Kottu roti** (chopped flatbread stir-fried with vegetables and egg) is the backpacker staple and available everywhere for similar prices. Avoid restaurants on the main tourist drags near beaches and you'll eat better and cheaper.

### Cash
Sri Lanka is still largely cash-dependent outside major hotels and tourist sites. Bring more rupees than you think you need before heading to Arugam Bay — ATMs there are limited. **Colombo, Kandy, Galle, and Mirissa** all have reliable ATMs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Sri Lanka safe for backpackers?

Yes. Sri Lanka is one of the safer destinations in South and Southeast Asia for solo and budget travellers. The local population is famously welcoming and petty crime targeting tourists is relatively low. Standard precautions apply: keep an eye on your belongings in crowded areas and use PickMe rather than flagging random tuk-tuks.

### How do I get around Sri Lanka on a budget?

Intercity buses are the cheapest option and cover most of the island. For longer distances, the train is more comfortable, often faster, and not much more expensive. Within towns, tuk-tuks are the default — agree on a fare before you get in, or use the PickMe app for metered pricing in major towns.

### Is Arugam Bay worth visiting outside surf season?

Arugam Bay's surf season runs from June through September. Outside those months the bay is quieter but still beautiful — the beach remains excellent for swimming and the nearby Kumana safari is less crowded. Guesthouses and restaurants are open year-round, though some reduce their hours during the shoulder months.

### What is the best time for this backpacking route?

October to November or March to April are the sweet spots. These shoulder periods offer dry or drying conditions across most of the island. Avoid the southwest monsoon (May through September) if you're planning time in Mirissa or Galle, and avoid January through March if Arugam Bay surf is a priority.

### How much does two weeks in Sri Lanka cost?

On a tight backpacker budget, expect $650 to $950 CAD per person for 15 days excluding flights. A comfortable budget — private rooms, occasional restaurant meals, all the activities — runs $1,000 to $1,500 CAD. The Sigiriya entrance fee ($40 CAD) and any safari costs are the biggest single-day spikes to plan for.

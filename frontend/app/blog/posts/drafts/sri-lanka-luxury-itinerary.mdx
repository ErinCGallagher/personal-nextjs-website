Sri Lanka punches far above its size. In two weeks you can move from ancient Buddhist temples to highland tea estates, dense jungle safari parks, Portuguese-Dutch colonial forts, and some of the finest beaches in Asia — all without ever needing a flight. The country is compact enough to cover by road and rail, and the quality of its luxury hotels is genuinely world-class.

This itinerary is built around the classics done properly: the cultural triangle anchored by Kandy, the unmissable train journey to Ella, a Yala leopard safari, the historic charm of Galle Fort, and a long, slow close on the southwest coast. Fourteen days, very little rushing.

# Kandy (2 Nights)

## Day 1: Arrival & the Sacred City

Kandy sits at around 500m elevation in Sri Lanka's central highlands, which makes it noticeably cooler and greener than the coast. The city wraps around an artificial lake, and its pace is unhurried compared to the sprawl of Colombo.

**Myst by Cinnamon** sits above the city on a hillside, with sweeping views over the lake and the surrounding hills. Check in, get your bearings, and head down into the city in the late afternoon when the heat has eased.

The **Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic** (Sri Dalada Maligawa) is the most important Buddhist site on the island and should be your first stop. The temple holds what is believed to be the left canine tooth of the Buddha, and the daily rituals of veneration are deeply atmospheric. There are three puja ceremonies per day (dawn, midday, and evening) — the evening puja around 6:30pm is the most dramatic, with drumming, chanting, and large crowds of pilgrims. Dress conservatively and remove shoes before entering. Entrance fee is approximately 1,500 rupees ($7 CAD) for foreigners.

A walk around Kandy Lake in the early evening is a pleasant way to end the day. On weekends there is a cultural performance — traditional Kandyan dance and drumming — at several venues near the temple. Worth attending if the timing works.

### Hotels

- **Myst by Cinnamon** — Hillside luxury hotel with panoramic views; one of the finest properties in Kandy

### Things to Do

- **Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic** — Non-negotiable; go at puja time
- **Kandy Lake walk** — Pleasant evening stroll around the waterfront
- **Kandyan Cultural Show** — Traditional dance, fire-walking, and drumming; touristy but well-done
- **Kandy Market** — The Kandy Municipal Market is worth a wander for local colour

## Day 2: Day Trips from Kandy

Kandy is a strong base for day trips into the hill country.

**Royal Botanical Gardens, Peradeniya** — 6km outside Kandy and one of the finest botanical gardens in Asia. 60 hectares of manicured lawns, orchid collections, a bamboo grove, a giant java fig tree that covers nearly half an acre, and a canopy walk. Allow 2–3 hours.

**Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage** — About an hour from Kandy on the road from Colombo. A sanctuary for orphaned and injured elephants; you can watch the herd bathe in the river twice daily. It is one of the most popular tourist attractions in the country, which means it is busy — go early. Mixed views on the ethics of elephant tourism are worth considering; Pinnawala is generally regarded as one of the more responsible operations in the country.

**Tea Country & Factory Tour** — The road up from Kandy into the highlands passes through some of the most beautiful tea estate country in the world. Several factories welcome visitors and offer guided tours showing the full process from leaf to cup. Worth combining with the drive to Ella the next day.

**⭐ Day Trip Option — Sigiriya:** If you have a car and driver (which we'd strongly recommend for Sri Lanka), Sigiriya Rock Fortress is about 2.5 hours from Kandy. The ancient rock citadel rises 200m straight out of the jungle and is one of the most dramatic archaeological sites in Asia. A UNESCO World Heritage Site. It makes for a long day trip but is absolutely worth it if this is your only chance.

**Combine with Pidurangala Rock:** Directly adjacent to Sigiriya, Pidurangala is a 45-minute hike whose summit looks back at Sigiriya rising from the jungle — the view many people consider the better photograph of the two. It pairs naturally with a Sigiriya visit on the same day (entry around 500 rupees, ~$2.50 CAD).

# The Train to Ella

## Day 3: Kandy → Ella by Rail

The train from Kandy to Ella is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful railway journeys in the world. For six hours you roll through lush forests, past waterfalls, through tea estate after tea estate, and across the dramatic central highlands landscape. The carriages fill with travellers hanging out of the open doors for photographs, and the atmosphere is unlike any other train journey.

Book seats in advance through the Sri Lanka Railways website or through your hotel — 1st and 2nd class observation cars have reserved seats. The 3rd class carriages are unreserved and can be extremely crowded.

**⭐ Tip:** Sit on the right side of the train for the best views heading from Kandy to Ella.

The train passes through **Nanu Oya** (nearest station to Nuwara Eliya, worth a stop if you have an extra day), **Haputale**, and arrives at **Ella** in the late afternoon. The journey from Kandy departs in the morning and gives you an afternoon to settle into Ella.

# Ella (2 Nights)

## Days 3–4: Ella's Three Iconic Hikes

Ella is a small hill town with big scenery. Three hikes cover the must-sees: **Ella Rock** (the most demanding, with the best views), the **Nine Arches Bridge** (colonial-era stone railway viaduct in the jungle), and **Little Adam's Peak** (easy walk with panoramic views, usually combined with the bridge).

[We have a full guide to all three hikes here.](ella-sri-lanka)

With two nights in Ella, you have time to do Ella Rock on one morning (start early — the heat by 10am is significant), and the Nine Arches/Little Adam's Peak combination as an afternoon outing the following day.

The town itself is small and pleasant. There are good cafes along the main street, and the evenings are cool enough to sit outside.

### Hotels

- **98 Acres Resort & Spa** — Hilltop resort with sweeping valley views; the infinity pool is one of the best in Sri Lanka
- **Ella Flower Garden Resort** — Mid-range, well located, friendly staff

### Things to Do

- **Ella Rock** (7.1km, 313m elevation — see [full guide](ella-sri-lanka))
- **Nine Arches Bridge + Little Adam's Peak** (4.2km, 208m elevation)
- **Rawana Falls** — A short tuk-tuk ride from town; one of the widest waterfalls in Sri Lanka

# Yala (2 Nights)

## Days 5–6: Safari in Yala National Park

Yala National Park in Sri Lanka's deep south has the highest density of leopards of any national park in the world. Leopard sightings are not guaranteed, but the chances here are better than almost anywhere else on earth. The park also holds elephants, sloth bears, crocodiles, water buffalo, and a remarkable diversity of bird life.

From Ella, the drive to the Yala area takes about 2–3 hours through increasingly flat and arid landscape — a dramatic contrast to the green highlands you've just left.

**Safari structure:** Safaris operate as morning game drives (departing before dawn) and afternoon/evening game drives. The morning drive is typically more productive for wildlife as animals are active before the midday heat. With two nights, you'll have the opportunity for one full safari day with morning and afternoon drives, plus your travel day.

Yala is best experienced with an experienced local guide and a reliable jeep. Your lodge can arrange this, or organise through a reputable operator in advance.

**⭐ Tip:** The park has multiple blocks — Block 1 is the most accessible and most heavily visited. Blocks 2–5 are less crowded and sometimes more productive for wildlife, particularly for leopards. Ask your guide for their recommendation based on recent sightings.

# Galle (2 Nights)

## Days 7–8: Galle Fort & the South Coast

Galle Fort is one of the best-preserved examples of Dutch colonial fortification in Asia, and it is an extraordinary place to spend two days. The fort walls enclose a living neighbourhood of restaurants, boutique hotels, art galleries, and independent shops — all within a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The street layout is unchanged from the 17th century; what's changed is the quality of the cafes.

Walking the walls at sunset is one of the better free activities in Sri Lanka. The lighthouse at the southern tip of the fort is a particular spot.

Beyond the fort, the beaches at **Unawatuna** (3km east) are among the most popular on the south coast — sheltered bay, good swimming, plenty of beach restaurants. **Jungle Beach** nearby is quieter and less developed.

**Whale watching:** Between November and April, blue whale and sperm whale sightings are frequent off the Mirissa coast, about 30km from Galle. Half-day tours depart from Mirissa harbour early in the morning. The blue whale sightings here are among the most reliable in the world.

### Hotels

- **Amangalla** — The finest hotel in Galle Fort; occupies a beautifully restored Dutch colonial building in the centre of the fort
- **Fort Bazaar** — Boutique hotel within the fort walls; excellent restaurant on site
- **The Fort Printers** — Characterful small hotel in a converted printing house

### Things to Do

- **Walk the fort walls** — Allow 1 hour; don't miss sunset from the lighthouse end
- **Fort Street exploring** — The streets inside are an afternoon in themselves
- **Galle National Museum** — Dutch colonial history of the fort
- **Unawatuna Beach** — 3km from the fort; good for swimming
- **Whale watching from Mirissa** — Book in advance; blue whales and sperm whales (season dependent)
- **Turtle watching** — Sea turtles nest on the beaches near Galle; several conservation projects offer night tours

# Kosgoda (5 Nights)

## Days 9–13: Slow Days on the Southwest Coast

The **Sheraton Kosgoda Beach Resort** sits on a long stretch of palm-lined beach on the southwest coast, about 90km north of Galle. This final stretch of the itinerary is the decompression: five nights with no agenda beyond the beach, the pool, and whatever day trips appeal.

Kosgoda is best known for the **Sea Turtle Conservation Project** — one of the oldest turtle hatcheries in Sri Lanka. The project collects eggs from the beach at night, incubates them, and releases hatchlings directly into the sea. It's a remarkable thing to witness and a straightforward excursion from the hotel.

The **Bentota River** runs into the sea just north of Kosgoda, and river tours by boat are a pleasant way to spend a morning — weaving through mangroves, spotting water monitors, kingfishers, and occasional crocodiles.

**Colombo** is approximately 90 minutes north and worth a day trip if you want a sense of the capital. The Pettah Market area, the historic Fort neighbourhood, and the waterfront promenade are the main draws.

## General Tips

### Getting Around

Sri Lanka's roads are narrow and the driving style takes getting used to. We strongly recommend arranging a **private driver for the full trip**, including the airport transfers. A reliable local driver who knows the country makes an enormous difference — they'll handle logistics, suggest stops you wouldn't have found, and make the long road days between destinations genuinely enjoyable. Your hotel can recommend drivers, or arrange through a reputable local agency.

The Kandy–Ella **train** is the exception — take it by rail. It is one of the most scenic railway journeys in the world and should not be substituted with a car.

### Currency

Sri Lanka uses the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). USD and EUR are widely accepted at major hotels and tourist sites, but rupees are needed for local restaurants, tuk-tuks, and smaller shops. As of 2026, $1 CAD is approximately 215 LKR.

### Health & Vaccinations

Check with a travel health clinic before visiting. Standard recommendations typically include Hepatitis A and B, Typhoid, and ensuring routine vaccinations are up to date. Malaria prophylaxis is sometimes recommended depending on itinerary and season — the Yala area has some risk. Get professional advice specific to your dates.

### Best Time to Visit

Sri Lanka's two coasts have opposite dry seasons:
- **Southwest coast** (Galle, Kosgoda): December to April
- **East coast**: May to September

This itinerary is on the southwest coast for its beach section, making it best suited for December through April. Kandy and the hill country are pleasant year-round but can be very wet during the inter-monsoon periods (October–November and May).

### Tuk-Tuks

For short journeys within towns — Kandy, Ella, Galle — tuk-tuks are the right tool. Agree on a fare before you set off, or use **PickMe**, Sri Lanka's equivalent of Grab, which has metered fares in major towns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 14 nights enough for a luxury Sri Lanka circuit?

Yes — 14 nights is a comfortable pace for this itinerary. The route moves deliberately without rushing any destination. If you want more time in the hill country or on the coast, an extra night can be added at Kandy, Ella, or Galle without disrupting the overall flow.

### What is the best time for this Sri Lanka itinerary?

December through March is ideal. The southwest coast (Galle, Kosgoda) is in its dry season, Yala safaris are excellent as animals congregate around remaining water sources, and the hill country is pleasant year-round. Avoid May through September for the beach section — the southwest coast is affected by the southwest monsoon during these months.

### Do you need a private driver for this itinerary?

Strongly recommended. Sri Lanka's roads are narrow, signage is inconsistent, and the driving style takes adjustment. A good private driver makes the long transfers between Kandy, Ella, Yala, and Galle genuinely enjoyable rather than stressful. Your hotel can arrange one, or book through a reputable local agency before arrival.

### Can the Kandy–Ella train be booked in advance?

Yes, and it should be. First-class observation car seats and 2nd-class reserved seats can be booked online through the Sri Lanka Railways website or through a local agent. The 2nd-class reserved carriage is very comfortable and a fraction of the cost of the observation car. Book at least 2 to 3 weeks ahead during the December to March peak season.

### How much does a luxury Sri Lanka trip cost?

Expect $5,500 to $8,000 CAD per person for a genuine luxury experience covering accommodation, transfers, safaris, and activities. The Yala tented camp accommodation is the single biggest variable — all-inclusive camps can push daily costs significantly higher than the rest of the trip.

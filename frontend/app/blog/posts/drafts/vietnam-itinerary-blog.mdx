Vietnam is a country of stunning contrasts, from the limestone karsts of Ha Long Bay to the mountain towns of Da Lat, the ancient charm of Hoi An, and the bustling modern city of Ho Chi Minh. This 2-week itinerary takes you through the best of what Vietnam has to offer, combining cultural experiences, natural beauty, culinary adventures, and plenty of time to unwind. 

This route flows naturally from north to south, with a mix of guided tours, independent exploration, and opportunities to relax. Whether you're a first-time visitor or returning explorer, this itinerary can be adjusted to match your interests and pace.

## Day 1: Arrival & Acclimation in Hanoi

Vietnam's capital is a city of contrasts: ancient temples, colonial architecture, and chaotic streets filled with scooters, food carts, and energy. We spent 3 days here to start off the trip.

After traveling, this is a perfect day to settle in, combat jet lag, and begin exploring at a relaxed pace. Travelling from North America meant our body clocks had us awake before dawn, so we walked down to Hoan Kiem lake and watched the locals come out and perform their morning routines. The Vietnamese are early risers and like to spend time outdoors before the sun becomes too much to bear. You’ll find them hanging out, exercising, and even dancing together down by the lake. Most breakfast spots don't open until 7 or 8am, so we had to make do with coffee alone until then. 

We got breakfast at [Bancông Cafe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/yodb4LvQxkJsHxin9) right as it opened. It serves local dishes and western brunch, and has a nice balcony facing the morning sun, looking over the bustling streets below.

After breakfast we headed over to [Train Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KyTycYYPqcZnwyP87) and didn't have to wait long before everyone was ushered off the tracks and a train came rolling right through. If you've never seen it before, it is quite exhilarating, it seems so at odds with the western sense of Health and Safety, every year there are fresh rumors that it will be shut down, and yet it endures.

Afterwards we treated ourselves to a massage at one of the countless parlours in the city. They are all stellar value, expect to pay $25-40 CAD for an hour's treatment.

The old town has several streets dedicated to garments and luggage, all famous brands like The North Face or Patagonia, all at unbelievably low prices 😉, it's hard not to take a look at what's on sale.

We got dinner at [CO](https://maps.app.goo.gl/YUMsZiLuhUiizg5G6), a restaurant serving local fare, with a lot of vegetarian options.

### Hotels

- **Carillon Boutique Hotel** — Mid-range, excellent location in the Old Quarter
- **Hanoi Paon Hotel and Spa** — Mid to Upscale, spa services available for jet lag recovery, located in French Quarter

## Day 2: Exploring the City's Rich History

We started the day with a coffee and pastries from a local bakery, [Movitas](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dCj44LwZjWaCmVKbA), and then went to check out the Ho Chi Minh Musuem. We had wanted to see the mausoleum, where you can see the nation's former leader lying in state, but it keeps peculiar opening hours, so we missed it. The museum follows his life, and how he came to be leader of the country. Yes, there is a lot of nation-building propaganda, but it is still worth visiting. Also worth noting is the dress code. No exposed shoulders or knees for both the museum and the mausoleum!

Later in the day we took a free (tip supported) walking tour which started at the temple on Hoan Kiem lake, took us into a temple, through the food markets, up some very narrow alleyways, into a large textile market, and finally up to the "Eiffel tower of Hanoi". This is in fact a very long railway bridge, designed by Gustave Eiffel, famous for his eponymous Parisian tower. The last stop, right next to the bridge, was a bar selling the local draft beer, served ice cold for $0.50 a pint.

## Day 3: Ha Long Bay Cruise


If you're reading this, you are likely planning a trip to Vietnam, and I'm sure Ha Long Bay is at the top of your list. It is easily one of the most photographed and heavily hyped destinations in the country. But with huge crowds and endless identical tour options clogging up the internet, it is easy to wonder:

Is it actually worth it? In short: **yes, 100%.**

While the initial departure process can feel chaotic, the moment your boat leaves the port and glides past the first massive limestone karsts, you realize exactly why this place is so popular. It is a bucket-list experience for a reason.

We were picked up from out front of the Opera House at 8am, ferried around for 30 minutes in a cramped taxi (nothing like what we'd booked), before swapping to a larger minibus (which exactly matched what we'd booked). We weren't explained this up front, so it was equally confusing and concerning, but eventually it all worked out. This is a common theme in Vietnam; you are right to question with the tour operator, but also expect some chaos like this throughout your stay.

We arrived at the ferry terminal by 11am, in good time to check in and board our little tender boat which took us out to the ferry.

# Da Nang (4 Days)

## Day 4: Ha Long Bay Cruise & Travel day to Da Nang 

Our second day on the boat started early with a sunrise Tai Chi class on the roof. This was very enjoyable, especially as the boat sailed back through the limestone karsts as the sun peeked between them.

After breakfast it was back onto the tender boat and off to the shore. We boarded another bus and we were on our way back to Hanoi, ready to catch an afternoon flight to Da Nang.

There are many, many flights between Hanoi and Da Nang, so just pick a time that suits you. Given the travel time to get to the airport, we'd recommend no earlier than 5pm.

Da Nang airport is very close to the city, so it should only take 20 minutes in a taxi to get to your hotel.

Finding dinner will not be a problem. We have spent a month in Da Nang, so have a rich, vetted list of restaurants to pick from, see below!

## Day 5: Da Nang 
If you're comfortable riding a scooter, it is a great way to get around in Da Nang. If not, then Grab is a cheap alternative. We rented a scooter from Henry Motorbike Rental (Whatsapp +84 37 755 5365) on both occasions we were in Da Nang. He will bring the scooter to you at no extra charge and charges around $5 a day - you can even pay with credit card!

Da Nang is most famous for its huge, golden beach. You can get a lounger with a sunshade from $5 for the whole day, we picked one that came with a free fresh coconut drink and a 30 minute foot massage included in the price! There are also boat rides, jet ski rentals, and parasailing (check if your travel insurance covers it, not all do) at reasonable prices.

If you're looking for something more calm or spiritual, pay a visit to the Lady Buddha, a 67-meter tall white status looking out over the beach in the north of the city. It's a 20 minute ride from downtown and there's a small entry fee, but it was nice to walk through the bonsai tree gardens and look out over the bay at the city.

For thrillseekers there is Ba Na Hills, a huge theme park about an hour's drive from the city. We didn't visit, but many people have good things to say about it.

## Day 6: Da Nang 

It's worth mentioning that massages in Da Nang are equally good value to Hanoi, so we enjoyed a few while we were in the city. 

We wanted to get some custom clothing made for us, and just an hour to the south of the city is Hoi An, the tailoring hub of the country. We took a grab down to [Tuong Tailor](https://maps.app.goo.gl/szo6CS1CDDJb9nxd6) which was highly recommended online and didn't disappoint. We were guided through the styles and fabrics available and they took some measurements for us and said to come back in a couple of days. You can get a rush service for an additional fee if you don't have the time.

## Day 7: Da Nang 

While the sun is strong in Vietnam, and it does get hot, Da Nang is blessed with a strong sea breeze that keeps things at a pleasant temperature. The giant streets are seldom filled with traffic, and the cars and bikes don't honk quite as much as in Hanoi. We found the city to be a very relaxing place to be, and just enjoyed being there, strolling along the beach, spoiled for choice with cheap and delicious coffee, reading in cute cafes. (We enjoyed it so much we returned the following year and spent a month there!)

We also took a [cooking class](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HyFSuwZApY7TF1e58) in which we visited a local food market to shop for ingredients, learned how to make pho, bun cha, spring rolls and fresh rolls. 

On Friday and Saturdays the dragon bridge puts on a show at 9pm. The eastern side of the bridge has a dragon head that shoots fire and water high into the air and is quite a spectacle. It's quite surreal and there is a night market nearby to grab some snacks or do some late night shopping.

# Hoi An (2 Days)

A charming UNESCO World Heritage town frozen in time, Hoi An offers ancient architecture, excellent food, and the unique experience of having custom clothing made by skilled tailors.

## Day 7: Hoi An

We handed our scooter back in and took a grab to our hotel in Hoi An. The city is very hot in the day with little shade, so the streets are very quiet. We went to visit our tailor for a test fitting and then spent the rest of the day relaxing at the hotel pool.

In the evening the ancient city comes alive and it is a joy to walk along the maze of old streets filled with restaurants, bars, and boutiques selling beautiful artwork, trinkets, and other gifts. The city has a lot of riverbanks as it was historically a trading port for centuries, and after dark the water is filled with a sea of lantern-bearing boats which you can charter for a relaxing trip up and down the river.

We took in a show at the [Lune Center for Performing Arts](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Lq3fDfqDCvfuDX2YA) which has a couple of very impressive nightly acrobatic shows. Book in advance though, we could only snag seats in the last row and due to the domed shape of the building, we were not the most comfortable for the hour long performance.

## Day 8: Hoi An

Time to collect our suit and dress! It was an impressive turnaround and very good tailoring. We paid $300 CAD for the custom suit and a shirt, and $100 CAD for a custom dress.

Another day of relaxing by the pool followed by an evening in the night markets and sunset cocktails on the roof at [92 Station](https://maps.app.goo.gl/mWPFhD9MrQitPfz29). 

After that we caught a light and dance show at [Hoi An Memories Land](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KyF5HBUjh6NdcQMK7) which had a theatrical performance of an old folk tale. If you can only pick one, go for the Lune acrobatics show, but this was still worth seeing.

### Additional Activities
- **Lantern Making Class** — Make your own traditional silk lantern
- **Hoi An Beach (An Bang Beach)** — Peaceful, less crowded than Da Nang's My Khe
- **Assembly Hall of Cantonese Chinese** — Beautiful example of traditional architecture

### Hotels

- **Renaissance Hoi An** — Upscale beachfront resort hotel with excellent amenities, but it is 15 minutes away from downtown
- **Rokas Hoi An** — Mid-range boutique hotel right in the heart of downtown, comfy beds. Friendly, family-run hotel built in 2025


### Restaurant Recommendations

- [**Mot**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/tv3W7fyvT2tsZ67u9) - You HAVE to try the iced tea, but there is usually a line. If you go at lunch time it will be quieter. Their local dishes are also very good.
- [**The Son Bistro & Craft Beer**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8NWQsZrjS9oxAHgY9) - good local fare and a fine selection of local craft beers.

# Da Lat (2 Days)

Nestled in the Central Highlands, Da Lat is a mountain town with French colonial architecture, waterfalls, adventure activities, and some fun quirks. In a bygone era the wealthy French colonissts would come here to cool down as the altitude offers some respite to the often opressive heat of the coastal lowlands.

## Day 9: Travel to Da Lat

From Hoi An to Da Lat is approximately 10-12 hours by bus. We recommend catching a flight from Da Nang (return to Da Nang, ~1 hour drive, then 1-hour flight).

Depending on your travel choice, this could be a long day, so just plan to arrive, enjoy some food at [Quan Kim](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bCfQ7H4kmtDg6H6N8) which is just a few minutes walk from Mooka's Home.

If you have the energy to explore more, definitely check out [Maze Bar](https://maps.app.goo.gl/F15H96DfPQRdZCWZA) around sunset. It is a surreal, multi-story labyrinth packed with winding tunnels, hidden rooms, whimsical sculptures, and a rooftop garden. Throughout there are many pockets to hang out in private, with the occasional other patron wandering through, looking lost. The rooftop garden has a great view over the city which is perfect to enjoy at sunset. It took us about 20 minutes to work through the maze and find the rooftop, don't worry, there are toilets up there! There is no entry fee; you simply buy a drink at the ground-floor bar to explore.

**Warning:** Da Lat police are not as lenient as in Da Nang, and are very strict on stopping motorists without licenses. Since it is not possible for foreigners to obtain a Vietnamese license, you will be pulled over, you will be fined, and your vehicle will likely be impounded for a week.

## Day 10: Da Lat
After breakfast, head to Hang Nga Guest House (Crazy House) — one of the most unique and bizarre accommodation/attractions in Vietnam. Even if you're not staying there, it's worth a tour. Surrealist architecture, dark corridors, and strange rooms.

Entrance costs 80,000 VND per person.

Afterwards, we took a Grab to Datanla to enjoy a mix of outdoor adventure and scenic views. We spent a few hours on the high rope course, testing our bravery on the tarzan swing and our grip strength on the more challenging obstacles. Next, we took the alpine coaster down the mountain-side, and wrapped things up by checking out the waterfall at the bottom. 

It cost us 650,000 VND per person to visit all three.

After all that excitement, relax with a massage at [An Lac Massage Spa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/M8oZ3qq7BFBYqeQU7), a one hour massage costs 250,000 VND.

### Additional Activities

- **Flower Gardens** — Da Lat is known for flowers; visit gardens (especially beautiful November-February)
- **Trai Mat Village** — Old train and traditional village experience
- **Dalat Palace** — Historic hotel with heritage architecture; visit for a coffee or meal
- **Thien Vien Truc Lam Pagoda** — Peaceful Buddhist temple complex
- **Market exploration** (Dalat Market)
- **Da Lat Train Station** — Historic French colonial station (no longer operational for passengers, but beautifully preserved)

### Hotels

- **Mooka's Home** - modest but comfortable hostel with twice-weekly rooftop barbequeues, a great way to socialise with fellow travellers and enjoy some good food.
- **Crazy House** - the most surreal accommodation you'll ever stay at! Nestled inside the Gaudi inspired maze of a property, you'll enjoy whimsical surroundings in a truly unique space. It's also very reasonably priced. Don't worry, the staff will block off the areas around your room so you aren't intruded upon by visitors.

### Restaurant Recommendations

- [**Quan Kim**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bCfQ7H4kmtDg6H6N8) - a delicious mix of local and international fare, small menu but very well cooked. Check opening hours on google as they are a little unusual.



## Mui Ne (2 Days)

A small coastal beach town famous for its red and yellow sand dunes, seafood, and the stunning Fairy Stream. Mui Ne is more laid-back than Da Nang and offers adventure and relaxation in equal measure.

## Day 11: Travel to Mui Ne
Mui Ne has no airport, so the only way there from Da Lat is by road. You have three options: private taxi, luxury sleeper bus, or regular sleeper bus. We took a regular sleeper bus, even though it was in the middle of the day. This was arranged through our hostel, so we weren't aware of the luxury sleeper bus at the time. A minibus collected us and took us to the bus station where we boarded the sleeper. The regular sleeper is a cramped, 3-row affair, with 2 aisles separating the "beds". If you're over 6ft like me, you won't be comfortable. Compounding the discomfort is the first hour of the 5 hour journey is along some bumpy, winding mountain roads, so make sure you prepare accordingly (I took some dimenhydrinate which helped). The bus dropped us off directly outside our hostel.

If you think Mui Ne is a beach town, you may be sorely disappointed. While it is a coastal town, almost every meter of the beach is privately owned and inaccessible from the road without paying for access via a property. If your hotel has beach access, you're fine, but otherwise, you will have few choices to get to the sea.

After the ordeal was over, we treated ourselves to a delicious hot-stone steak at [Modjo](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pRhKtrkLP1AAHgBP8) which was delicious and raised our spirits again, ready for the next day!

## Day 12: Mui Ne

One of the top things to do in Mui Ne is to visit the sand dunes in Phan Thiet, which we booked through [Johnny Tours](https://johnnytours.com/). We were picked up at our accommodation in our own sixties-era Jeep and the tour guide for the whole group rode with us. First, we stopped at the Fairy Stream, which was a shoes off affair so you get to feel the super-soft sand under foot and you walk up stream to the first set of dunes. There are restaurants and gift shops along the way in case you get hungry.

Next, we hopped back in the jeep to Phan Thiet harbour, where we looked out at thousands of little fishing boats resting in the bay. It was a sight to behold, and the Mixue ice cream shop across the street offered a sweet treat to cool down with while we looked out at the view.

Mui Ne is **VERY** hot, even in April the temperature was hovering around 40C, so be prepared - bring lots of water, and take any opportunity to cool down that you get.

The third stop was the yellow dunes: a desert area with some impressive bright yellow dunes. We paid a little extra and got to ride an ATV around them for 20 minutes. You can either drive yourself, or ride as a passenger. If you ride yourself, you will have a safety driver with you who clings precariously to the side but guides the way through the maze of dunes and steers you clear of danger (like tumbling down a dune!).

Included in this stop is the hair-raising ride over the side of the tallest dune. You board an ATV with a professional driver and he takes you down what feels like a vertical drop. A few exhilarating moments later and you're dropped off at the next viewpoint where the tour guide met us.

We then got back in the jeep for one last stop at the red dunes, where we all walked up to the final viewpoint. 

If you like seafood there are a plethora of fresh seafood restaurants along the water.

### Hotels

Unfortunately we cannot recommend the hostel we stayed in (Miami hostel). While it was cheap, it was not comfortable. The air conditioning in the rooms is switched off at 6am and is not switched back on until after sunset, so by 7am the rooms are unbearably hot.

### Additional Activities
- **Phan Thiet City** — Explore the larger city nearby; fishing harbor and fish sauce production
- **Ke Ga Lighthouse** — Historic lighthouse with panoramic views
- **Po Shanu Cham Tower** — Partially ruined Champa tower with valley views

### Restaurant Recommendations

- [**El Latino**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/4WYwVcQRx4JhGdky8) - pretty good tacos, cheap beer, often have live music
- [**Dunes**](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Qgh3sdb5dFw3Wc2A6) - great brunch spot


## Ho Chi Minh City (3 Days)

Vietnam's largest city, formerly known as Saigon, is a whirlwind of energy, culture, history, and incredible food. It's the perfect finale to your southern Vietnam journey.

## Day 13: Travel to Ho Chi Minh City
Since we hated our hostel so much, we left a day early and took a four hour luxury sleeper bus to Ho Chi Minh city. It was not much more expensive than the regular sleeper, and you get a much roomier, 2 x 1 x 1m padded private cabin with a window. The roads are also flat and smooth all the way so it was a much more pleasant experience. 

We checked into our hotel in the afternoon, enjoyed some hotpot for dinner at [Happy Lamb Hotpot](https://maps.app.goo.gl/4a3VcmmCc72X5DdD6) and relaxed

In the evening we ventured out to [Bui Vien Walking Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/wb8xma6p8HhavB2h9), a crazy street, crammed with bars and clubs blasting music at volumes I promise you have never experienced before. It is a sensory overload, and I strongly recommend bringing ear plugs, even if you don't go inside any of the bars. There are DJs playing electronic music, and many live bands playing covers of western favourites. We found a spot with an acceptable volume and a very good cover band playing Fleetwood Mac and enjoyed the night.

## Day 14: Ho Chi Minh City - Day Trip to Cu Chi Tunnels

We decided to take a day trip to visit the historic Cu Chi Tunnels from the Vietnam War. We booked through [Get Your Guide](https://gyg.me/Pvr6bDhW), although these tours seem to be commodified across platforms. The drive took about two hours, picking us up from our hotel in a comfortable minibus. Once we arrived, our guide walked us through the first exhibit, captured American military vehicles including a huge C130 transport aircraft, tanks, helicopters and more! 

We entered the forest while he explained how this massive underground network was constructed and used. We got a firsthand look at the clever camouflage techniques, hidden trapdoors, and various booby traps used throughout the area. The most memorable part of the day was crouching down to crawl through a section of the actual tunnels, which gave us a real appreciation for the cramped conditions. 

In the evening we took a Grab to [Clay](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pj472VJbvyF2vNM69) to get some pizza and cocktails.

## Day 15: Ho Chi Minh City

We got brunch at [The Hungry Pig](https://maps.app.goo.gl/9uG1EfZxdTXMXtWa6) before heading to [Ben Thanh Market](https://maps.app.goo.gl/1h9vUEooxSixRrqg6) to do some clothes and souvenir shopping. Ho Chi Minh is full of lots of crafty stores and we found more a short walk away at [The New Underground](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ssAA5VcAWAnaWtyh7). Since the city is very hot, year round, we'd recommend stopping for an iced coffee at [Cong Ca Phe](https://maps.app.goo.gl/UPMwv2NMUTwhMVm47) to enjoy a coconut iced coffee in the air conditioned cafe. This particular spot is uniquely themed and embodies the Vietnamese take on the coffee culture they inherited and adapted from the French.

From there, walk northwest towards [Notre Dame Cathedral of Saigon](https://maps.app.goo.gl/ySUcaYFNLyNATvDTA), another French colonial hangover means roughly 10% of the Vietnamese population are Christians. Opposite the cathedral is the [Central Post Office of Saigon](https://maps.app.goo.gl/EoiZZdoC67B9wYAv6) which is worth a visit for its grand scale and ornate finishings. There are many vendors selling creative postcards and you can write and mail them directly. Finally, take a stroll down [Book Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/c5nPwzBXWYqwVQoY6) and you'll see just how much the Vietnamese love to read. Don't worry, there are books in English too!

Your last night deserves some local fare, so grab some Pho from [Pho Viet Nam](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VDkf6SXyiUxynAAQA). When you arrive you will notice the many Michelin stars this little place has earned over the years, and once you taste it you'll understand why!

## General Travel Tips

### Cash vs Card
During our time in Vietnam roughly 80% of our spend was on card. You should definitely carry cash as it does come in handy, especially when shopping in the markets or buying a coffee from a street vendor.

The best **Free to use ATM in Vietnam** is VP Bank. You will find there's often a queue of foreigners waiting to use them because other ATMs charge egregious fees. They are easy enough to find on Google Maps.

**Our recommendation** is to use your mobile wallet (E.G. Apple Pay) when paying by card as your card cannot be cloned. While this didn't happen to us in Vietnam, it did in Bali and ever since then we've used Apple Pay as much as possible and have not suffered another card cloning incident.

### Street Food
Both times I ate meat from a street food I got food poisoning (chicken Banh Mi, pork bao bun). If you stick uncut/unpeeled fruits, coffee, coconuts, you'll probably be fine. But otherwise, exercise caution when chosing what to eat!

### Taxis / Ride Hailing Apps
Grab is your best friend for much of Southeast Asia, and Vietnam is no exception. Rides across the country are very cheap, from just a few dollars for a 15 minute scooter ride, or $30 for an hour long airport transfer!

### Long Distance Trains in Vietnam
We opted not to take any trains in Vietnam as I've done so before and it doesn't make financial or logistical sense for most people. The train is more expensive than flying between Hanoi and Da Nang. 

A flight will cost $35-100 one way and takes an hour (plus 2-3 hours for airport time either end).
A train, by contract, will cost $65 (or more depending on if you book "luxury class") and takes 18 hours.

If you've never done a sleeper train and want to try it, there are worse places than Vietnam, but just know that you'll have a bed to sit and sleep in, and nowhere else to sit. The rails are almost a century old and the journey is not smooth.

### Renting Scooters in Vietnam
Since foreigners cannot obtain a Vietnamese driving license (unless you can read, write, and speak Vietnamese), there is no legal way to ride a scooter or motorcycle in the country. With that said, some parts of the country are more lenient than others. This is by no means legal advice, purely anecdotal experience, but here is a list of places you can/cannot rent a scooter in the country:

**Places it is Possible to Rent a Scooter**
- Da Nang - No police, no worries. I rented twice from Henry Motorbike Rental (Whatsapp +84 37 755 5365) and scooted around the city for a month without any issues.
- Ninh Binh -  No police, no worries. Not mentioned in this itinerary, but I have rented and rode scooters here without issue. I picked up a scooter from a vendor who approached me at the train station, although some hostels also rent them out to guests too.
- Hoi An - No police, no worries. Worth noting that much of the downtown area is pedestrians only, you'll need to park up and walk the rest of the way.

**Advisory Against Renting Scooters**
- Da Lat - The police are hot on the case of foreigners renting scooters here, fines and impoundings are common. I narrowly avoided police interactions 3 times in an hour so handed the scooter back. I would not recommend renting a scooter in Dalat.
- Hanoi - The capital has the highest police presence and coupled with the chaotic traffic situation, I would not recommend renting a scooter in Hanoi.
- Ho Chi Minh City - Again, significant police presence and coupled with the chaotic traffic situation, I would not recommend renting a scooter in Saigon.
